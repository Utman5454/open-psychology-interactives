# Teaching notes - Dichotic Listening and Selection Theories

`modules/cognitive/tools/07-dichotic-listening-selection/`

**What it teaches.** In this visual two-stream analogue, a physical
change to the ignored stream, bold or underlined text plus a marked
tone, gets noticed far more often than the ignored word's meaning gets
recognised. That split between noticing a change and recognising what it
was is exactly what separates early-selection, attenuation and
late-selection accounts.

**Before students start.** This is a visual analogue of dichotic
listening, not two spoken messages at two ears, and only a learner's
very first trial is genuinely naive.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what will be reportable about the ignored stream: nothing, a
   physical change, its meaning, or both.
2. Set the attended side, the attended-task rule and whether tones are
   on, then run a practice trial.
3. Run a block of four trials, pressing for every target on the
   attended side.
4. After each trial, say whether anything on the ignored side looked
   different, then, if so, pick which word it was.
5. Run a second set at the harder rule, or load the worked example,
   then sort five findings against the theories they trouble.

**What to look for.** In the worked example, a marked change on the
ignored side is noticed 88% of the time under the easy rule and 72%
under the harder rule, while a plain, unmarked change is noticed only
17% and 10% of the time. Recognising what the word actually was happens
less often still than merely noticing that something changed.

**Debrief.** On a trial where you noticed a change but could not name
the word, what had been processed and what had not? Why does making the
attended task harder reduce how often the ignored change is noticed at
all?

**Common misconception / caution.** "This is dichotic listening" is
worth correcting straight away: it is a visual analogue with an optional
tone layer, and reading, unlike listening, is voluntary and allows free
eye movement. Only a student's very first trial is naive; after that
they know a word is planted, so later trials measure divided attention
rather than a genuine failure of selection.

**Use in class / timing.** Eight minutes covers a demonstration. Practice
plus one set takes 12. With the harder-rule set, the challenge and the
debrief, about 30 minutes total.
