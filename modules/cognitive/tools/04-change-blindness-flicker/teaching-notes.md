# Teaching notes - Change Blindness

`modules/cognitive/tools/04-change-blindness-flicker/`

**What it teaches.** A blank screen inserted between two otherwise
identical scenes removes the local flash or motion that a change would
normally produce at its own location, turning instant pop-out into slow,
effortful search. That does not mean people store nothing about a scene
between glances.

**Before students start.** The four scenes each vary a different kind of
change, so no scene-to-scene comparison is licensed. Only the
blank-versus-no-blank comparison within one scene is.

**What students do.**
1. Predict how long it will take to find a change that large.
2. Pick a scene and search for the difference with the blank in place.
3. Say what changed and check it against the reveal.
4. Run the same or a new scene with the blank switched off.
5. Answer the closing challenge on what the blank actually removes.

**What to look for.** With the blank in place, a change that would pop
out instantly can take dozens of seconds to find. Switching the blank off
in the same scene collapses the search time back toward immediate
detection. How wide that gap is for any one student says nothing about
that person.

**Debrief.** What exactly does the blank remove? It is not the change
itself. While searching, was the person "blind" to the rest of the
scene? What were they doing the whole time?

**Common misconception / caution.** Someone will say this shows people
store nothing between glances. They do not have to be storing nothing:
the paradigm compares across views rather than testing storage capacity,
and nothing here measures memory. The four scenes vary change type,
position and size one at a time, so a scene-to-scene comparison of which
property is easier to spot is not something this design can support.

**Use in class / timing.** Five minutes covers a front-of-room
demonstration. Running two or three scenes yourself, with the challenge
and debrief, takes about 25.
