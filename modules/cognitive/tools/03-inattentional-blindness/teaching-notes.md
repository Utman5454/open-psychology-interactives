# Teaching notes - Inattentional Blindness

`modules/cognitive/tools/03-inattentional-blindness/`

**What it teaches.** A fully visible, unmoving object can go completely
unreported while attention is absorbed by a demanding counting task. That
shows attention and awareness are not the same thing, and that failing to
report something is not the same as failing to see it.

**Before students start.** Say nothing about anything beyond the counting
task. Mentioning that anything else is on screen empties out the whole
demonstration.

**What students do.**
1. Give a mild opening judgement about how accurate their count will be.
2. Choose a variant and count the target shape in a static, self-paced
   display.
3. Enter their count, then answer what else, if anything, was on screen.
4. Compare against the reveal, which rings the object and lists what each
   variant changes.
5. Sort four design changes in the closing challenge.

**What to look for.** Watch the gap between getting the count right and
reporting the unexpected object. The tool never states a noticing
percentage anywhere on the page; an invented one would be worse than
none, since people remember numbers long after they forget the caveat
attached to them.

**Debrief.** If you missed it, do you think you actually saw it? What
would settle that question? One of the six recognition options is "a
star." How does offering that option differ from an open question, and
which way might it bias someone toward saying yes?

**Common misconception / caution.** "I'm bad at attention" is the
misconception to catch immediately: one trial and one answer give no
reliability at all, and nothing here measures anyone. Do not let anyone
leave with an invented noticing percentage in their head; the tool
withholds one for exactly that reason.

**Use in class / timing.** The demonstration itself takes about 5
minutes. With the reveal and the variant table, 12. With the challenge
and full debrief, 20.
