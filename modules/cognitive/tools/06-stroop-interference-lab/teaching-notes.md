# Teaching notes - Stroop Interference Laboratory

`modules/cognitive/tools/06-stroop-interference-lab/`

**What it teaches.** Interference and facilitation are two separate effects measured against a neutral baseline. Naming an ink colour is slowed by a conflicting word and helped by a matching one, and subtracting congruent from incongruent collapses both into one number that hides which of them moved.

**Before students start.** Say that the browser timings are illustrative rather than a measurement of anyone, and that the neutral condition is what makes the rest of the activity readable.

**What students do.**
1. Predict where a neutral block will sit relative to congruent and incongruent.
2. Run six practice trials, then a scored block, or load the worked example instead.
3. Read interference and facilitation off the results panel, both measured against neutral.
4. Work through the transfer challenge on four changes to the task.

**What to look for.** In the worked example, incongruent trials run 78ms slower than neutral and congruent trials run 24ms faster, so neutral sits between the two rather than beside either one. Watch for students reporting only the incongruent-minus-congruent gap, which hides that it is two effects added together.

**Debrief.** What can you say with the neutral condition on the chart that you could not say without it? Interference is usually larger than facilitation; what does that asymmetry rule out for a simple "the word gets read automatically" account?

**Common misconception / caution.** Watch for "the Stroop effect is incongruent minus congruent." It throws away the comparison neutral makes possible. Worth stating alongside it: twelve trials per condition is nowhere near enough for a stable difference score, and no reaction time on this page is a Stroop score for the person who produced it.

**Use in class / timing.** The worked example alone runs the whole idea in 5 minutes. A full session with a live block, the transfer challenge and debrief takes 25 to 30.
