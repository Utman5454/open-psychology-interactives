Cognitive Psychology

Stroop Interference Laboratory

A word appears in coloured ink. Name the ink, not the word. Almost everybody can do it, almost nobody can do it as fast when the word disagrees — and the interesting question is what the neutral condition adds to that sentence.

Illustrative browser timing — a demonstration, not a measurement of anybody

Learning objective

By the end you should be able to split a Stroop effect into interference and facilitation against a neutral baseline, and say what moves each of them.

About 25 minutes. Nothing you do here is saved or sent anywhere.

Before you start — what this task needs

The task requires telling four ink colours apart. That is the task, not a design accident, so it cannot be removed. If colour discrimination is difficult for you, a two-colour version (blue and yellow, which differ in brightness as well as hue) is available in the settings, and the worked example reaches every conclusion on this page without responding to a single colour.

Nothing flashes. One word is on screen at a time and it stays until you answer — no trial is timed out. Nothing animates, so there is nothing for a reduced-motion setting to switch off.

  1. Answer the prediction below — it unlocks the experiment.
  2. Run the practice trials. They are untimed and tell you what happened.
  3. Run a block. On each trial, name the ink colour as fast as you comfortably can.
  4. Read the two effects: interference above neutral, facilitation below it.
  5. Then the transfer challenge — four changes to the task, and what each should do to the effect.
  6. Prefer not to perform it? Load the worked example: two documented simulated blocks with fixed seeds.

First, a prediction

Three kinds of trial. On a congruent trial the word names the ink it is printed in. On an incongruent trial it names a different colour. On a neutral trial the word is not a colour word at all — an ordinary word such as chair — printed in one of the same inks. Your job is always the same: name the ink.

Where do you expect the neutral condition to sit?

The experiment

Press the button for the ink colour, or use the number keys shown on the buttons. The word stays on screen until you answer, so nothing is lost by thinking — but the measure is how long you took, so answer as soon as you are sure.

Key terms
Congruent and incongruent
Whether the word names the ink colour it is printed in.
Neutral trial
A trial whose word does not name a colour. It is the baseline both effects here are measured from.
Interference
Naming time on incongruent trials, measured against neutral.
Facilitation
Naming time on congruent trials, measured against neutral.
Trial mix
The proportion of congruent, incongruent and neutral trials in a block. It is one of the things you can change here.

Name the ink colour

Six untimed practice trials with feedback, then a scored block with no feedback until it ends. Results are withheld during the block on purpose: a running mean would change how you answer the next trial.

What this demonstrates

The neutral condition is what makes it two effects

Compare incongruent with congruent and you get one number, and that number hides a question: did the mismatching word slow you down, or did the matching word speed you up, or both? Only a baseline can tell you. Interference is incongruent minus neutral; facilitation is neutral minus congruent. In most published work interference is much the larger of the two, and that asymmetry is itself something an account has to explain.

Automaticity is a description, not yet an explanation

"Reading is automatic" is the usual one-line summary, and it is doing less work than it looks. It has to mean something like: a skilled reader's response to the word is produced quickly and without being asked for, and it competes with the response the task actually wants. Notice that the explanation is about competition between responses, not about the word being impossible to ignore — the ink is named correctly on almost every trial. What is costly is resolving the conflict, not preventing the reading.

The effect moves when the context moves

If most trials are incongruent, the effect shrinks: the system adjusts how much weight it gives to the word. If the instruction is reversed — read the word, ignore the ink — the interference from the ink is small or absent, which is the asymmetry the automaticity account was invented to handle. An effect that moves this much with context is not a property of a person.

Conflict, control and what a difference score is

The modern reading is in terms of conflict monitoring and control: a conflict is detected, control is adjusted, and the adjustment shows up on the next trial and across the block. That account is productive and it is not the only one — accounts based on the relative speed of the two processes, on translating a colour into a word, and on learning the statistics of the stimulus set all survive the basic finding. The demonstration on this page cannot choose between them.

What this does not demonstrate

  • It does not measure your executive control. A difference between two noisy means, each from a dozen trials, is mostly noise. Difference scores are also famously unreliable even when each condition is measured well, because the reliable variance the two conditions share is subtracted away.
  • It does not show that reading is unstoppable. The ink is named correctly on nearly every trial. What the data show is a cost in time, not a failure of control.
  • It does not choose a mechanism. Speed-of- processing, response-competition, translation and statistical-learning accounts all predict a cost on incongruent trials.
  • It is not a clinical or occupational measure. Nothing here supports a judgement about anybody's attention, impulsivity, fitness for a task, or anything of the kind.
  • Browser timing is imprecise. The page paints on the display's refresh schedule, keyboard and pointer events are queued, and background load adds more. Laboratory work uses hardware that removes exactly this uncertainty.
  • The worked example is simulated. Its interference and facilitation come from documented generators with fixed seeds, chosen to make both effects legible. They illustrate the pattern; they are not evidence for it, not norms, and not anybody's data.
