# Teaching notes - Decision Framing Laboratory

`modules/cognitive/tools/11-decision-framing-laboratory/`

**What it teaches.** Identical-value options described as something
"saved" versus something "lost" pull people toward different choices, a
shift in the proportion of a group choosing the safe option rather than
a property of any one person. The certainty of the safe option, not just
its safety, does separate work: removing the guarantee costs it far more
support in the gain frame than in the loss frame.

**Before students start.** A single person's four decisions cannot show
a framing effect on their own, since the comparison needs two people
looking at the same scenario under different frames.

**What students do.**
1. Predict which wording pushes people toward the risky option.
2. Set whether the safe option is completely certain or only very
   likely, and how the framing describes what is at stake.
3. Make four decisions, each shown in one frame only.
4. Compare against the reveal, which shows the same arithmetic under
   both frames and how often groups chose safe.
5. Rewrite one scenario in the closing challenge to make the framing
   matter less.

**What to look for.** In the worked example, removing the guarantee
costs the safe option 21 percentage points of support in the gain frame,
74% down to 53%, but only 4 points in the loss frame, 33% down to 29%.
Point at that asymmetry, not just the frame effect on its own.

**Debrief.** Why can a single person's four decisions not show a framing
effect on their own? Removing the guarantee costs the safe option far
more support in one frame than the other. What does that suggest about
what certainty is actually doing?

**Common misconception / caution.** "This proves people are irrational"
claims too much: it shows a group-level shift in proportions, and
preferring a certain gain to a risky one of equal value is a normal,
well-documented pattern, not a mistake. Four decisions from one person
show nothing, since each scenario here is seen in only one frame.

**Use in class / timing.** The worked example demo takes about 6
minutes. Four decisions plus the reveal take 10. A full session with the
rewriting challenge and debrief runs about 25 minutes.
