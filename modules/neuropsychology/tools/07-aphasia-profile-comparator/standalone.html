Aphasia Profile Comparator

Six fictional language profiles. Put any two side by side and work out which single measure separates them - and then work out what a separation is worth.

Simulated — invented profiles, original task descriptions, no clinical materials

Learning objective

By the end you should be able to say which measure carries most information about a particular contrast, and what a separation is worth.

About 30 minutes. Nothing you do here is saved or sent anywhere.

No categories, no clinical materials

The tool never names a syndrome, no standardised battery, subtest or item is reproduced, and every task description is original. The six profiles are invented and nothing here assesses anybody.

  1. Choose two profiles.
  2. Before looking at the separation column, decide which single measure you think does the most to tell them apart.
  3. Check, read why, and choose another pair.

What this demonstrates

A measure is informative about a contrast, not in general

Repetition separates profiles A and D almost perfectly and tells you nothing at all about A against B. Comprehension does the reverse. There is no such thing as the most informative language measure. There is only the most informative measure for the question you are asking, which is why hypothesis-driven assessment beats running a long battery and looking at what comes out.

Repetition is informative because it can be short-circuited

Repeating a spoken word can be done by understanding it and saying it, or by a shorter route from the sounds coming in to the sounds going out, without meaning being involved. A person whose repetition is disproportionately poor while comprehension and speech are relatively spared is showing that the short route is unavailable - and that is a claim about a connection rather than about a region. A person whose repetition is disproportionately good is showing the opposite: the short route is intact and doing the work while the rest is not.

The categories are prototypes, not kinds

The classical categories were defined by which symptoms tended to occur together in the cases available a century ago. They are useful shorthand with three well-known problems. A substantial minority of people fit none of them, the same label covers very different profiles, and the anatomical claims attached to them are much looser than the diagrams suggest. Profile F is not an anomaly; it is what a continuous space looks like when you try to sort it into boxes.

Fluency is a summary of several things at once

"Fluent" bundles rate of speech, phrase length, effort, articulation, grammatical structure and how much content the words carry. Two people rated equally fluent can differ on every one of those, and a single fluency score hides it. The same is true of every measure here - each one is a compound, and a difference on it is a difference in the compound.

None of this locates anything

The tool mentions no anatomy anywhere. Language depends on a distributed left-hemisphere network and on the white matter that joins it. The same behavioural profile follows damage in several places, and the classical region-to-syndrome mapping has not survived the imaging evidence in the form it was taught. A profile constrains what has gone wrong with the processing. It does not constrain the anatomy nearly as tightly as the textbook diagrams imply.

What this does not demonstrate

  • The 0-to-10 scale is a teaching device. It is not a real scoring system, the scores are invented, and no real person or published case appears anywhere.
  • Six measures is a caricature. A real language assessment separates single-word from sentence comprehension, word from non-word repetition, regular from irregular reading, spontaneous from picture-described speech, and much more. Each of those distinctions carries information this tool merges away.
  • Profiles are not stable. They change with time since onset, with fatigue, with the material used and with the day. A single profile is a snapshot.
  • No syndrome is named. If you map these profiles onto textbook labels, notice how much of the individual pattern the label discards.
  • Separation is not causation. That one measure separates two profiles says the two people differ in what that measure needs. It does not say what that is, or where.
