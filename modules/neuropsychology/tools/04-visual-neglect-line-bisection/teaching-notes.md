# Teaching notes - Visual Neglect Laboratory

`modules/neuropsychology/tools/04-visual-neglect-line-bisection/`

**What it teaches.** Neglect and visual field loss are not the same
failure, and no single task separates them cleanly. Bisection errors
run in opposite directions, neglect biting to the right of centre,
field loss towards the blind side. Cancellation and copying add what
bisection cannot: a prompt to search the whole page roughly halves a
neglect profile's error but does nothing for field loss, and copying
alone reveals whether neglect is organised around the viewer or around
each object.

**Before students start.** Read "neglect is not blindness on one side"
aloud. Field loss removes the input; the person searches for what is
missing and reports the gap. Neglect leaves the input present; the
person does not attend to it and does not report anything missing.

**What students do.**
1. Predict which single task separates field loss from neglect most
   cleanly.
2. Choose a task, bisection, cancellation, or copying, and a fictional
   profile, with or without an examiner's prompt to search the whole
   page, and read the outcome.
3. In the closing challenge, use three blinded evidence lines from one
   hidden profile to identify which of the five profiles produced
   them.

**What to look for.** Bisection alone can mislead: it moves opposite
ways for neglect and field loss but by itself cannot say which
profile is which without a reference point. The prompt cuts the
neglect profiles' errors by roughly half and leaves field loss
unchanged, since field loss has nothing to do with attention.
Copying is the task that tells viewer-centred and object-centred
neglect apart: object-centred neglect drops the left side of each
object wherever that object sits on the page, and the page-search
prompt does nothing for it.

**Debrief.** Ask why bisection, cancellation and copying are all
needed, given that bisection alone cannot decide between the two
conditions.

**Common misconception / caution.** "Neglect is blindness on one
side" collapses two different failures. No localisation is offered
here: neglect follows damage in several regions and in the connections
between them, and while it is more common and more persistent after
right-hemisphere damage, the reverse occurs too.

**Use in class / timing.** Ten minutes is enough to compare one
profile across all three tasks. Cover every profile and the closing
challenge and the session runs closer to 40.
