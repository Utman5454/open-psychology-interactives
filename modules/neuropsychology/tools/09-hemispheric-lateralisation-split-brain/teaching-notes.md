# Teaching notes - Split-Brain Laboratory

`modules/neuropsychology/tools/09-hemispheric-lateralisation-split-brain/`

**What it teaches.** After a complete callosal section, whether a
response succeeds depends on routing: which hemisphere received the
item against which hemisphere controls the requested response, not on
which hemisphere is cleverer or "dominant." Only three of the six
field-and-response combinations succeed once the callosum is cut.
Popular left-brained and right-brained talk extends a narrow finding,
about which hemisphere can act on an item at a given moment in a
handful of surgically disconnected people, into a claim about
connected brains, where both hemispheres receive almost everything at
once.

**Before students start.** The callosum control starts on "intact," so
a first trial shows the unaffected baseline before anyone cuts
anything. Read the caution that nothing here is flashed to one visual
field; a browser cannot control fixation, and the tool reports what a
routing model predicts.

**What students do.**
1. Build a trial: pick a visual field and a response channel, predict
   whether it will succeed, then run it and read the routing diagram.
2. Cut the corpus callosum and run the same trial again.
3. In the second experiment, hold or drop each of five conditions the
   classic result depends on, and read the verdict.

**What to look for.** Only three of the six sectioned combinations
succeed, a word in the left field picked out by the left hand, a word
in the right field spoken, a word in the right field picked out by the
right hand, and each of the other three fails in its own way, staying
silent, groping and guessing, or naming the wrong object. Dropping
fixation control alone makes the trial uninterpretable whatever else
holds. Dropping cross-cueing control lets a correct answer through by
a route the design was meant to exclude.

**Debrief.** Why can the same word, seen by the same hemisphere, be
picked out by one hand and not spoken?

**Common misconception / caution.** "The left hemisphere is dominant,
so it overrides the right" misreads silence as suppression: the left
hemisphere says nothing because it never received the information, not
because it beat the right hemisphere to an answer. Left-brained and
right-brained personality talk makes the same error at a larger scale.

**Use in class / timing.** Eight minutes is enough for the classic
trial and a single section change to land. Both experiments in full,
with the challenge, take about 30.
