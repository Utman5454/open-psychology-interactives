# Teaching notes - Recovery and Plasticity Simulator

`modules/neuropsychology/tools/12-recovery-plasticity-simulator/`

**What it teaches.** Restitution, relearning, compensation and
practice all look like "getting better" on a trained task, but only
restitution and relearning show up on a task nobody practised. The gap
between the trained and untrained curves is exactly what compensation
and practice contributed, computed from the model rather than
asserted. Recovery is not unlimited: the ceiling on how far each
process can go shrinks as injury severity rises, so even every setting
maximised cannot return a severe injury to its starting level.

**Before students start.** Predict what accounts for the early rise in
scores after a simulated injury, before opening the simulator.

**What students do.**
1. Answer the opening prediction.
2. Set injury severity, rehabilitation intensity and the month you
   measure, then compare five simulated people's trajectories, each
   with its own variation and measurement noise, on the trained task.
3. Load two different routes to the same trained score, and compare
   the trained and untrained task across all twenty-four months.

**What to look for.** Setting rehabilitation intensity to zero still
leaves the early curves climbing, since most of the early rise happens
whatever anybody does. Two routes can reach the identical trained
score at the same month with very different gaps to the untrained
task, because only compensation and practice inflate the trained score
without ever touching the untrained one.

**Debrief.** Ask what evidence, beyond the rise itself, would be
needed to attribute an observed improvement to one particular
rehabilitation programme rather than to time passing.

**Common misconception / caution.** "Neuroplasticity means the brain
can rewire around anything" does not survive the severe-injury preset
at every setting maximised: the ceiling itself shrinks with severity,
so recovery stays bounded even under the most favourable settings
available. A rising curve on a trained task is not evidence that a
programme worked without a comparison group who did not receive it.

**Use in class / timing.** A single route through experiment 2 lands
the point in about 10 minutes. Both experiments together, with the
closing challenge, run to about 40.
