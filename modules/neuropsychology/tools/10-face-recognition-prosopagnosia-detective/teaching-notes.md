# Teaching notes - Face Recognition Detective

`modules/neuropsychology/tools/10-face-recognition-prosopagnosia-detective/`

**What it teaches.** Recognising someone breaks into several
separable steps, general visual processing, face perception,
familiarity, identity knowledge and name retrieval, alongside a
parallel route from voice to the same identity and name stages.
Testing which step a fictional profile's task pattern is compatible
with is a live search across all six locations: some profiles fit one
step uniquely, one fits two equally well, and one fits none at all.

**Before students start.** Read the "no anatomy" panel. No brain
region is named, because these steps do not map onto single areas and
the same profile can follow damage in several places, or a
developmental difference with no lesion at all.

**What students do.**
1. Predict where a difficulty most likely sits for someone who cannot
   recognise colleagues by sight but knows exactly who is speaking on
   the phone.
2. Damage one step and read the resulting profile across seven tasks,
   including a non-face control task and the separate voice route.
3. Choose among six fictional profiles and test which step, if any,
   uniquely accounts for its pattern.

**What to look for.** Damaging general visual processing and damaging
face perception give identical results on every face task; only a
non-face control task, as hard as the face task, tells them apart. One
profile fits no step at all. Another fits two steps equally, because
it was never given the control task that would have separated them.

**Debrief.** Ask what the two-step-fit profile shows about why a
control task has to be matched for difficulty, not merely present.

**Common misconception / caution.** "Confirms a face-specific
impairment," from a below-range face-matching score alongside intact
object matching, holds only if the two tasks were matched for
difficulty. Faces are far more similar to one another than everyday
objects are, so an easier control task can look intact for reasons
that have nothing to do with faces.

**Use in class / timing.** One damaged step and one profile make the
point in about 10 minutes. Both experiments with the challenge run to
about 45 minutes.
