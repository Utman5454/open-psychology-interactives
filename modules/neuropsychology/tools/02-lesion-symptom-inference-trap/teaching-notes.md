# Teaching notes - Lesion-Symptom Inference Trap

`modules/neuropsychology/tools/02-lesion-symptom-inference-trap/`

**What it teaches.** A single lesion case can establish that some
damaged tissue was necessary for a task, in this person, at this time.
It cannot establish what that tissue does in general. Students rate
their confidence in the general claim "fluent speech output is
localised to the left inferior frontal region," then watch the
evidence ceiling for that same claim fall from 50 to 12 across six
independent complications, while the specific necessity claim about
Person M is untouched throughout.

**Before students start.** Read the "localisation is not the villain"
panel aloud. Lesion evidence is one of the few sources of information
about necessity; the argument is about what one case can carry, not
against lesion studies as a method.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what the referral supports, choosing among four readings of
   "damage here, difficulty there."
2. Rate confidence in the general localisation claim, then reveal six
   complications one at a time, locking a rating before each new one
   appears.
3. Sort four closing claims into well supported, partly supported, or
   not supported by this case.

**What to look for.** The ceiling drops on each reveal, an oversized
lesion, a shared vascular territory, oedema and diaschisis, unknown
premorbid language organisation, a second unrelated lesion, and finally
a confound in how comprehension was tested, while the four numbers
describing Person M never change. Most students do not lower their
rating enough between complications, and the gauge names that gap
directly. The premorbid-level complication is the one to watch for
misreading: it raises uncertainty about how atypical her language
organisation might already have been, not evidence that left-handers
have reversed lateralisation.

**Debrief.** Ask what exactly stays true throughout: the claim about
this person's tissue on these tasks, or the claim about what the region
does in general. Then ask what would have to change about the case for
the general claim to survive complication six.

**Common misconception / caution.** "The lesion is in the region, so
the region does that job" collapses necessity into function. What
damage shows is that a task could not be done without that tissue in
this person; what the tissue contributes to the task in general is a
separate question that one case, however clean, cannot answer.

**Use in class / timing.** The prediction and the first three
complications run to about 12 minutes from the front. The full six
complications with the closing challenge run to about 40 minutes.
