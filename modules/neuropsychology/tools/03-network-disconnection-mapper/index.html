Neuropsychology

Network Disconnection Mapper

Five regions, six pathways, four tasks. Break any one thing and see which tasks notice - including the ones that fail while every region is intact.

Simulated — a conceptual diagram, not an anatomical atlas

Learning objective

By the end you should be able to explain how damage to a connection produces a deficit far from the lesion.

About 25 minutes. Nothing you do here is saved or sent anywhere.

This diagram is a toy

Five boxes and six lines. No real brain has five regions, no real pathway carries one thing, and nothing here is positioned anatomically. The diagram exists to make one logical point visible: a network can fail at a node, at an edge, or at several points that look identical from the outside. Treat it as a cartoon of an argument, not as a map.

  1. Answer the opening question.
  2. Damage a region or cut a pathway - one at a time to begin with - and watch the four task outcomes above the controls.
  3. Find two different single failures that produce exactly the same profile. There are several.

First, a judgement

A fictional person understands everything said to them and names pictures without difficulty. Asked to repeat a spoken word back, they cannot - and the difficulty is worst for unfamiliar words.

    What this demonstrates

    A deficit does not need a damaged region

    The clearest state in the tool is the one where every box is intact and a task still fails. Two regions that each work perfectly cannot do anything together if what joins them is cut. Historically this was Wernicke's and Lichtheim's argument, it was largely abandoned for most of the twentieth century, and modern tractography brought it back: a substantial part of what lesion studies attribute to cortex is attributable to the white matter underneath it.

    The same profile, several causes

    Damage the word-sound store, damage the speech-output region, or cut the pathway between them, and the four tasks respond identically. Behaviour alone cannot distinguish them. This is the general form of the problem: a symptom constrains where the failure is, and rarely to one place. Any inference that runs from a symptom to a single location has quietly assumed that the other candidates were ruled out.

    Networks degrade before they stop

    Two of the four tasks have a second, weaker route. Cut the main one and the task becomes possible but wrong in a characteristic way - pictures named with a related word, familiar words repeated but unfamiliar ones not. The pattern of errors is often more informative than the score, because it says which route is being used. A tool that only reported pass or fail would throw that information away.

    Localisation and networks are not rivals

    Nothing here says regions do not matter; damage a node and tasks fail. The claim is narrower: which tasks fail depends on the connectivity as much as on the tissue, so a map of regions is an incomplete description of a system whose behaviour depends on how the regions are joined. The useful question stopped being "where is function X" some time ago; it is "what has to be intact and connected for task X to be possible".

    What this does not demonstrate

    • The diagram is not anatomy. Five boxes, six lines, positioned for legibility. No real region, tract, connectome or published model is reproduced, and the layout carries no anatomical meaning.
    • Real networks are not binary. Nothing in a brain is intact or cut. Connections are partially damaged, signal is degraded rather than absent, and regions participate in many networks at once. Three outcome states is already a large simplification of a continuum.
    • Boxes are not modules. Calling something "the word-sound store" is a description of a job the network has to do somewhere, not a claim that a piece of tissue is dedicated to it.
    • No syndrome is being defined. Some of the states resemble textbook aphasia categories. Those categories are contested, they fit individual people poorly, and the tool deliberately never names one.
    • No one is being assessed. The four tasks are descriptions, not tests. Nothing here diagnoses anybody or offers a prognosis, and the person in the opening scenario is fictional.
    • Second routes are not recovery. A task surviving on its weaker route is not the network repairing itself; it is a different route being used, with different and usually worse results. Restitution, compensation and strategy substitution are different things.
