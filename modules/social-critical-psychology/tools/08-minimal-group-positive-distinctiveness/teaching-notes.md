# Teaching notes - Minimal Group Allocation Laboratory

`modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/08-minimal-group-positive-distinctiveness/`

**What it teaches.** The behaviour worth pointing at is the option that gives your own category fewer points than the own-group-most choice does. Choosing it means pursuing being further ahead of the other group, even though nobody can allocate to themselves and the points buy nothing real.

**Before students start.** Say clearly that nothing is stored, nothing is scored and no profile of anybody is produced. Some students expect a "how prejudiced are you" result.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what people most often do.
2. Answer the sorting question and get assigned to a category on a basis the page admits is trivial.
3. Make six allocations in the equal-starting-position condition, reading the arithmetic printed under each option.
4. Change the starting position to your category already ahead, and repeat the six choices.
5. Judge the five claims in the closing challenge.

**What to look for.** Run the worked example and compare the two rows of the comparison table: the strategy counts are identical in both conditions, three fair, one own-group-most, two largest-lead, yet the gap it produces runs from +0 to +25 in the equal condition and from +300 to +325 when your category starts ahead, exactly the pair of numbers worth putting on screen for the room.

**Debrief.** Fairness is probably most students' most-used strategy; does that weaken the finding? Should reasoning about the same six choices have changed between the two starting positions?

**Common misconception / caution.** The three starting positions are this tool's own addition, not part of the classic paradigm, and are stipulated rather than earned, worth saying before anyone treats the already-ahead condition as evidence about real privilege. Expect at least one student to say this explains racism: prejudice involves beliefs, feelings and a history that the paradigm removes by design, so what it actually rules out is any account that needs prior hostility to produce differential treatment.

**Use in class / timing.** Six minutes from the front with the worked example. Both starting positions with the challenge and debrief take 35 to 40.
