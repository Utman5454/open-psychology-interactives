# Teaching notes - Minimal Group Allocation Laboratory

`modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/08-minimal-group-positive-distinctiveness/`

**What it teaches.** One allocation option gives your own category fewer
points than the own-group-most choice does, in exchange for a bigger
lead over the other side. Choosing it buys nothing real: allocators
cannot allocate to themselves and the points have no value outside the
exercise. What is being chosen is being further ahead of the other
group, not simply having more.

**Before students start.** Say clearly that nothing is stored, nothing
is scored and no profile of anybody is produced. Some students expect a
"how prejudiced are you" result.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what people most often do.
2. Answer the sorting question and get assigned to a category on a
   basis the page admits is trivial.
3. Make six allocations in the equal-starting-position condition,
   reading the arithmetic printed under each option.
4. Change the starting position to your category already ahead, and
   repeat the six choices.
5. Judge the five claims in the closing challenge.

**What to look for.** Run the worked example and compare the two rows
of the comparison table. The strategy counts are identical in both
conditions, three fair, one own-group-most, two largest-lead choices,
yet the gap produced runs from +0 to +25 in the equal condition and
from +300 to +325 when your category starts ahead. The same strategy
profile does two very different things depending on where it starts.

**Debrief.** Fairness is probably most students' most-used strategy.
Does that weaken the finding? Should reasoning about the same six
choices have changed between the two starting positions?

**Common misconception / caution.** The three starting positions are
this tool's own addition, not part of the classic paradigm, and
stipulated rather than earned. Expect at least one student to say this
explains racism. It does not: prejudice involves beliefs, feelings and
a history that this stripped-down paradigm removes by design. What the
result does show is narrower and still useful. Differential allocation
appeared here with no prior hostility, no competition and no group
history at all, so none of those three is necessary to produce the
effect in this setting. That is not the same as ruling out every
account of real prejudice.

**Use in class / timing.** Six minutes from the front with the worked
example. Both starting positions with the challenge and debrief take
35 to 40 minutes.
