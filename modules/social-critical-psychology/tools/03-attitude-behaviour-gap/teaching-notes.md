# Teaching notes - Attitude-Behaviour Gap Laboratory

`modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/03-attitude-behaviour-gap/`

**What it teaches.** Four people can score identically on a
well-built attitude questionnaire and still differ hugely in how often
they act on it, because norm, control, habit, constraint and
especially opportunity each shift the model independently. The same
300 simulated colleagues' attitude-behaviour correlation swings from
about 0.20 to about 0.87 depending only on how uniform their
conditions are and how closely the measure matches the behaviour.

**Before students start.** All four colleagues in the first experiment
score identically, 6.2 of 7, on the same well-constructed scale.
Predict each one's frequency band before revealing any of them.

**What students do.**
1. Predict whether one person's 6.2 score means she speaks up nearly
   always, often, sometimes, or that the score licenses none of these.
2. Predict which frequency band each of four colleagues, all scoring
   6.2, falls into, then reveal the model's answer for each.
3. Move one slider, opportunity, on a fifth colleague, then explore
   the other sliders and the three levels of measurement match, and go
   deeper to see how the correlation across 300 colleagues depends on
   uniform versus varying conditions.
4. Judge two claims against what the evidence actually supports.

**What to look for.** Opportunity multiplies the whole estimate rather
than adding to it: if the occasion never arises, nothing else in the
model matters. The identical group's attitude-behaviour correlation
ranges from about 0.20 to about 0.87 depending only on whether
conditions are held uniform and how closely the measure matches the
specific behaviour.

**Debrief.** What would "attitudes predict behaviour" need to specify
before it became a claim you could actually check?

**Common misconception / caution.** This does not show attitudes are
unimportant. Attitude carries the largest single coefficient in the
model; it is necessary without being sufficient. A modest correlation
across a sample also does not license a frequency claim about any one
person in it.

**Use in class / timing.** The first experiment alone makes the point
in about 5 minutes. The full sequence with the deeper level and the
closing challenge runs to about 35.
