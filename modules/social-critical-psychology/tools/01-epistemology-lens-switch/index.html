Social and Critical Psychology

Epistemology Lens Switch

One fictional school, one reported rise in classroom disruption, and three starting points about what the social world is. Build a study from each and watch the object, the question, the evidence, the method and the defensible claim move together.

Fictional case - Meadowbank Academy, its figures and its documents do not exist

Learning objective

By the end you should be able to say what each of three epistemologies takes its research object to be, and why the method follows from that rather than the reverse.

About 30 minutes. Nothing you do here is saved or sent anywhere.

None of these is the sophisticated one

Each position buys something and pays for it. Measuring well can mistake a change in recording for a change in the world. Reasoning to mechanisms can drift into stories nothing could disconfirm. Studying how a category is assembled makes it hard to answer "how much, and is it getting worse" - which is the question the governors will actually ask. The tool marks combinations as coherent or in tension; it does not mark positions as right or wrong, and it has no favourite.

How to use this

  1. Read the case and the evidence the school already holds.
  2. Commit to an answer to the question below.
  3. Declare an epistemological starting point, then make the five decisions that build a study from it.
  4. Run the same case again from a different starting point, and compare what each study can say.
  5. Judge the five claims at the bottom against your studies.

The case: Meadowbank Academy

A fictional 11-16 school of about 900 pupils. Over two terms, senior staff have become convinced that "low-level disruption" in lessons is rising, and have asked for research. Everything below is the evidence the school already holds. It is the same for every lens.

What the school holds

  • Recorded incidents. 260 logged in the autumn term, 412 in the spring term. A logged incident is a referral typed into the behaviour system by a member of staff.
  • A staff survey. 68% of the 74 respondents agree that "behaviour in lessons has got worse this year". 12% disagree.
  • A policy change. In January the behaviour policy was revised. Referral, previously discretionary, became the expected response to a second warning.
  • Staffing. Eleven of the 62 teaching staff are in their first year at the school, against three the year before.
  • Rooms and timetable. Two departments moved into temporary accommodation in January; both are among the four highest-referring departments.
  • Pupil focus groups. Notes from two sessions. Recurring phrases include "they only write you up if they don't like you" and "it depends whose lesson it is".

What nobody yet knows

Whether pupils are behaving differently. The incident log records staff referrals, not pupil behaviour, and the thing that most obviously changed in January is the rule about when to refer.

That ambiguity is not a flaw in the case. It is the ordinary condition of an institutional record, and it is why the first decision a researcher makes - what the object of study is - settles far more than it looks as though it does.

All figures are invented for teaching. They are not estimates of anything, and no real school, policy or dataset is described.

First, a judgement

Three research teams are given exactly the material above. They differ only in what they assume the social world is like and how it can be known.

What will differ most between the three studies?

The lens switch

Declare a starting point, then make five decisions. Each decision offers the same four options whichever lens you declared - the tool never labels an option with the position it belongs to, because in a real research meeting nobody does either.

Need a hand?

Nothing here changes the controls for you, and none of it says what you are going to find.

Key terms
Ontology
What a position takes its research object to be — an underlying mechanism, a regularity among observables, or something produced in talk.
Epistemology
What that position treats as knowledge of the object, and therefore what could count as evidence about it.
Positivism
Looking for regularities among observable quantities, and treating careful measurement of them as the route to knowledge.
Critical realism
Holding that there are real structures beyond what is observed, while our access to them stays fallible and theory-laden.
Social constructionism
Treating the object of study as produced in interaction and language. It is a claim about how the object comes to be what it is, not a claim that nothing is real.

    What this demonstrates

    The method is downstream of the ontology

    Students are usually taught methods as a toolbox: pick the technique that suits the question. That is true as far as it goes, but it hides the step before it. A structured observation schedule is only a sensible instrument if disruption is the kind of thing that can be counted under a definition settled in advance. A discourse analysis of referral forms is only a sensible instrument if the definition is itself part of what is happening. Neither choice is a preference about data; both are commitments about what the object is.

    The same record answers different questions

    All three studies can use the incident log. The realist/positivist study treats it as a - noisy - measure of pupil behaviour. The critical realist study treats it as an outcome whose generating conditions include the policy change, and looks for the mechanism that would produce exactly this pattern. The constructionist study treats it as a document that does something: it converts an interaction into an attributable act by a named pupil. The log has not changed. What it is a record of has.

    Constructed is not the same as unreal

    Saying that "disruptive pupil" is assembled through referral practice does not say that nothing happened in the lesson, that teachers are inventing things, or that the exclusion which follows is imaginary. Categories with institutional force have entirely real consequences - that is precisely why how they are assembled is worth studying. Critical realism makes the same point from the other side: our concepts are fallible and socially produced, and there is still something there that our concepts are concepts of.

    Every lens costs something

    The realist/positivist study can say how much and how confidently, which is what a funding decision needs, and it can be badly wrong about what it counted. The critical realist study can name a mechanism and say what would disconfirm it, and it is always at risk of a plausible story that nothing could disturb. The constructionist study can show how the problem was made into this problem, and cannot easily tell the governors whether things are getting worse. Choosing a lens is choosing which of these costs to bear, not escaping them.

    Where the problem is located is a decision

    Option D throughout - screen the pupils, identify the responsible minority - is a coherent research programme with a long history. What the exercise makes visible is that it is a choice to locate the problem inside individual pupils rather than in the lesson, the policy, the building or the record, and that the choice is made before any data are collected. Asking who benefits from a given location is not a rhetorical flourish; in this case it is the difference between a screening budget and a review of the referral rule.

    What this does not demonstrate

    • Three positions is a simplification. Realism, positivism, empiricism, critical realism, relativism, social constructionism and constructivism are not four boxes, and serious writers disagree about where the lines fall. The tool uses recognisable family resemblances so that the downstream consequences are visible; it is not a taxonomy.
    • Nobody actually declares a lens. Real research teams inherit assumptions from their training, their funder and their journal. The declaration here is a teaching device to make visible a step that is normally invisible.
    • "In tension" is not "forbidden". Good mixed designs deliberately combine positions and say how they are holding them together. The tool flags a tension so you can describe it, not so you can avoid it.
    • No real school is described. Meadowbank Academy, its figures, its policy and its focus-group quotations are invented for teaching. No number here estimates anything.
    • Nobody is assessed. The tool scores the coherence of a study specification you build about a fictional case. It asks nothing about you and produces no score for anyone.
    • Coherence is not truth. A perfectly coherent study can be badly designed, unethical, or answer a question nobody needed answering.
