# Teaching notes - Epistemology Lens Switch

`modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/01-epistemology-lens-switch/`

**What it teaches.** Realist/positivist, critical realist and social
constructionist starting points commit a researcher to different
research objects, methods and defensible claims from the identical
case file. A coherence score only counts how many decisions matched
the declared position; it is the minimum a design has to clear, not a
mark of quality, and the tool says so directly.

**Before students start.** Read the Meadowbank case file aloud. All
three lenses work from the same evidence; nothing about the file
itself changes between builds.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what will differ most between three studies built from the
   same case.
2. Declare a lens, then work through its decisions, reading what each
   option commits you to and any tension with the declared lens.
3. Build the case again from a second lens, compare what each design
   can say, cannot say and hides, then judge five governors'-report
   sentences against the fixed answer key.

**What to look for.** A fully coherent design is not automatically a
good one. Watch what recedes from view under each lens rather than
only what it permits, and notice that a fourth, person-centred
position sits inside every decision without ever being one of the
three declared starting points.

**Debrief.** What would a person-centred account say about the same
file, and why does it follow from none of the three declared lenses?

**Common misconception / caution.** "Socially constructed" does not
mean invented or unreal. The constructionist lens's own caution states
that analysing how a category is assembled is not a claim that the
interactions it names are fictional. The realist/positivist lens gets
the matching caution the other way: a measurement schedule still has
to encode somebody's definition before the first observation, so
treating that lens as neutrally reading off the facts is its own
mistake.

**Use in class / timing.** The prediction question and one lens make
the point in about 15 minutes. Building a second lens with the
closing challenge runs to about 40.
