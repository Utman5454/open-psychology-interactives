# Teaching notes — Person or Setting? Workplace Explanation Laboratory

`modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/12-person-or-setting-workplace-lab/`

The correlation of 0.61 is real, would replicate, and survives every
intervention in the tool. It is also entirely compatible with the whole team
sitting at 74 because of a rota.

---

## Running it from the front

Do not tell the room that more evidence is coming. Let them commit to round 1 believing that is the file. The moment worth having is the one where somebody who confidently chose the individual explanation at round 1 reads item 11 and has to say out loud what changed.

And then push the other way. The correlation of 0.61 does not go away when the rota changes. Those individual differences are real, replicable and stable, and a student who concludes at round 2 that they were an illusion has learned the wrong lesson as thoroughly as the one who stopped at round 1.

Run the two ten-unit extremes back to back: everything individual and relational, against the two structural changes alone. The second gets a lower exhaustion score. Then read what each one leaves untouched, and notice that the structural combination leaves two people with a documented training gap exactly where they were.

The line that does the most work is the last one in the result, and it is the same after every combination: the rank order of the fourteen staff barely moves. The same people are still the most exhausted. A real, stable difference between people is not evidence about where its cause lives.

## Intended level

Second-year undergraduate upwards, including applied and occupational strands.
No statistics beyond understanding what a correlation is. It closes this module
by putting tool 03's attitude–behaviour problem, tool 10's levels of explanation
and tool 11's measurement questions into a single decision somebody has to fund.

## Learning objectives

After the activity a student should be able to:

1. construct individual, relational and organisational explanations of the same
   problem;
2. explain why a real difference between people does not locate its own cause;
3. say what each intervention targets and what it leaves in place;
4. state the limits of a matched-team comparison;
5. resist both easy conclusions — that psychological intervention is a
   distraction, and that structural explanation wins by default.

## Estimated duration

- **Demonstration from the front:** 8 minutes — round 1, then round 2, then the
  worked example in experiment 2.
- **Students working through both experiments:** 20 minutes.
- **With the challenge and the debrief:** 40 minutes.

The evidence now arrives in two rounds rather than three: round 1 is the
individual-level file, round 2 is everything else at once. Nothing was cut.

## Preparation

Read the eleven items and decide where you would have stopped. Then decide how
you will handle the two failure modes, because both will appear in the room:

- the student who commits to the individual explanation at round 1 and feels
  caught out — they were not caught out; round 1 supports that reading, and the
  tool says so;
- the student who reaches round 2 and concludes that the individual differences
  were an illusion — they were not; the correlation is still there after every
  intervention, and two staff still have a training gap that no rota change
  closes.

## The demonstration worth doing from the front

1. **Round 1, and do not tell them more is coming.** Read the four items, take a
   vote, and record it. The individual explanation will win, and it should.
2. **Round 2.** Read item 11 aloud: two teams matched at recruitment, on the same
   floor, thirty-six points apart. Take the vote again. Then point at the trail
   in the pinned panel and say: *nothing about Team B changed between those two
   votes.*
3. **Experiment 2, "Worked example".** It models both ten-unit extremes.
   Everything individual and relational reaches **53**; the two structural
   changes alone reach **40**, which is close to Team A's 38 without quite
   reaching it — worth saying aloud, because a structural remedy is not a
   guarantee of parity either.
4. **Then read what the structural row leaves running.** Two staff still have a
   documented training gap. That is the sentence that stops the session becoming
   a lecture on how structure beats psychology.
5. **Finish on the last line of the result**, which is the same after every
   combination: the rank order of the fourteen barely moves.

## Prediction question

*The review finds that exhaustion correlates 0.61 with performance across the
fourteen, and the three who left were the three most exhausted. What does that
establish?*

"An orderly difference between people, and nothing yet about why" is the intended
answer. "The problem lies in the individuals" is the productive error the whole
laboratory is built around. "Nothing — it's noise" is the overcorrection, and the
feedback pushes back on it too.

## Activity sequence

1. **Commit to the prediction.** It opens both experiments.
2. **Round 1**, four items, commit. **Round 2**, all eleven items, commit.
3. **Read the trail**, and the closing note about item 1.
4. **Experiment 2.** Model at least three combinations, including one that is
   entirely individual and one that is entirely structural.
5. **Read what each leaves running.**
6. **The challenge**, then the debrief.

## Debrief questions

1. At round 1 you had four items and an explanation. What was wrong with it, and
   was it your reasoning or the file?
2. Who decided what round 1 contained? What would it have cost to include item
   11 from the start?
3. Item 3 is two leavers describing themselves as "not cut out for it". Read it
   again after item 7. What kind of evidence is it now?
4. The correlation survives every intervention. What does that tell you about
   what a between-person correlation can and cannot detect?
5. Your combination left something running. Name it, and say who will be blamed
   when it produces the next resignation.
6. Item 11 is the strongest evidence in the file. Give two reasons it is not
   proof.
7. Resilience training is cheap, fast, needs nobody's permission and can start on
   Monday. The rota change needs a scheduling system rewritten. Which will be
   chosen, and what does that have to do with which is better?

## Likely misconceptions

| What students say | What to do with it |
| --- | --- |
| "The correlation shows it's the individuals." | A correlation between people measures variation within one setting. Anything constant for all fourteen cannot appear in it, so it cannot be ruled out by it. |
| "So individual differences don't matter." | Claim 2. Two staff have a real training gap; the three most exhausted are still the three most exhausted after every combination. |
| "Resilience training is a con." | Claim 3. It is insufficient, not wrong — and the sharper objection is about what offering it *instead of* a rota change does to who owns the failure. |
| "The rota change fixes everything." | Claim 4, and the leaves-running list. No scheduling change closes a documented skill gap. |
| "Team A proves it." | Claim 5. Matched at recruitment is not matched now; three people left Team B, and they were the three most exhausted. |
| "I was tricked at round 1." | No. Round 1 supports the individual reading and the feedback says so. The question is who scoped the file. |
| "The model shows structural change works." | It shows what the model was built to show. That is stated on the page, in the metadata and here. |

## Limitations and cautions

- **Kelbridge is an invention.** Teams, review, scale, scores and projections
  were written for this page. No figure is a norm, a prevalence or a published
  effect.
- **The projections are model output, not forecasts.** That the rota change
  outperforms the training is true by construction.
- **It does not show individual intervention is wrong**, and it does not show
  structural explanation always wins.
- **It does not establish causation.** Nobody randomised anyone to a rota.
- **The budget is a teaching device.** Real organisational change is not a menu
  with prices, and who gets to write the menu is left outside the laboratory.
- **Burnout is a contested construct**, and a number out of 100 conceals the
  argument about whether it is one thing and whether a scale score is the right
  home for it.

## Accessibility considerations

- Nothing is timed and nothing is scored against the person.
- All controls are native radios, checkboxes and buttons in labelled fieldsets.
  Committing with nothing selected produces an explanatory message, and "several
  at once" is treated as a real answer with its own feedback in every round.
- Evidence released in later rounds carries a heavier left rule, but each item
  states its own number and the round heading names what has just arrived, so
  nothing rests on the rule.
- Interventions the budget cannot cover are disabled, dimmed *and* say so in
  their own cost line.
- The outcome chart is hidden from assistive technology and paired with a
  sentence giving the before and after scores, the comparison team and the
  cost. Every value is printed beside its own bar.
- Both experiments announce every change through the shell's polite live region.
- Either reset button restores the entire page, including the prediction gate and
  the other experiment.
- Headings run h1 to h5 without skipping. Focus moves to the first experiment's
  heading when the gate opens. The stage track carries `aria-current` with a done
  state in text. Targets are at least 2.75rem. Forced-colours rules provided. No
  horizontal page scroll at 320px, 375px, 1366px, 1440px or 1920px.

## Optional extension tasks

1. **Write round 4.** What four items would you add that would make the
   individual explanation the best-supported reading again? Are any of them
   plausible?
2. **Cost the missing option.** The budget has no line for "hire two more staff".
   Price it, and say why it was not on the menu.
3. **Take it to a real review.** Find a published organisational review and mark
   which level each of its findings sits at. Then ask what the scope of the
   review left out.
4. **The other case.** Write a workplace case with no Team A, where the same
   evidence is available except the comparison. What can be concluded, and what
   would you have to do to get further?
5. **Follow the incentive.** Write 400 words on why an organisation might prefer
   the individual-level explanation, without assuming that anybody in it is
   acting in bad faith.

## The model

Documented in full at the top of `tool.js`. Nothing is random.

Team mean exhaustion starts at **74** on a 0–100 scale; Team A sits at **38**.

| Intervention | Level | Cost | Points off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A Resilience and stress-management workshops | Individual | 2 | 6, **halved to 3** if nothing structural is chosen |
| B Coaching for the two with training gaps | Individual | 2 | 1 on the team mean |
| C Weekly protected debrief | Relational | 3 | 9 |
| D Supervisor feedback practice | Relational | 3 | 8 |
| E Rota and recovery redesign | Structural | 5 | 18 |
| F Target and discretion change | Structural | 5 | 16 |

Budget **10**; all six would cost **20**. There are no combination bonuses: a
package is worth the sum of its parts, because the panel is not a puzzle with a
best answer. The one interaction that remains is substantive — resilience
training is halved when nothing structural changes, because the conditions
reload what the training discharged.

**Reference values a lecturer can check:**

| Combination | Cost | Exhaustion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nothing (the baseline shown before you model anything) | 0 | 74 |
| A alone | 2 | 71 |
| C + D | 6 | 57 |
| A + E | 7 | 50 |
| A + B + C + D | 10 | 53 |
| B + C + E | 10 | 46 |
| E + F | 10 | 40 |

The pair to put on screen is `A + B + C + D` against `E + F`: same cost of ten
units, thirteen points apart, and the second leaves two people with a documented
training gap exactly where they were. `B + C + E`, also ten units, is the row
that reaches all three levels at once and is worth setting beside both.

## Citation and evidence notes

- **Maslach and Leiter (2016)** for the argument that burnout is a property of
  the fit between person and job rather than of the person, which is the case
  this tool dramatises.
- **Karasek (1979)** and **Bakker and Demerouti (2017)** for demand-control and
  job demands-resources, which is where the rota, the queue and the discretion
  controls come from.
- **Panagioti et al. (2017)** and **Awa, Plaumann and Walter (2010)** for reviews
  finding organisation-directed interventions outperforming person-directed ones
  while both do something - the empirical shape the model imitates.
- **Ross and Nisbett (1991)** for the situationist argument, and **Fleeson (2004)**
  for the density-distribution account that keeps real individual differences
  inside it. Together they are the debrief's first section.
- **Robinson (1950)** on the ecological fallacy, and its individualistic mirror,
  which is the pair of errors in claims 1 and 4.
- **Ryan (2018)** on "resilience" as an organisational discourse, for the point
  that offering training *instead of* a change relocates the problem.
- **Rose (1990)** for the longer history of psychological expertise being invited
  into workplaces to explain outcomes at the level of the worker.

Full references are deliberately not embedded in the page, so the invented
figures are not mistaken for anything derived from them.
