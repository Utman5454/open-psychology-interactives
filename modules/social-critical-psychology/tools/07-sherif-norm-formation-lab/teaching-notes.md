# Teaching notes - Norm-Formation Laboratory

`modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/07-sherif-norm-formation-lab/`

**What it teaches.** On a display that carries no scale and genuinely
has no correct answer, a later, private judgement can land nearer a
group's earlier norm than a person's own first guesses did, with
nobody watching and nobody asking for agreement. That is convergence
under ambiguity, not a decision to go along with anyone. Four
different groups facing the identical display can settle on norms
roughly thirty centimetres apart, purely from who happened to answer
first.

**Before students start.** Read the caption aloud: there is no ruler,
no reference object and no scale, and any number is as defensible as
any other.

**What students do.**
1. Predict whether estimates converge and stay converged, converge
   only while the group is present, do nothing, or converge on a true
   answer.
2. Give fifteen individual judgements: five alone, five with three
   others visible, five alone again.
3. Reveal three other simulated groups' norms on the identical display
   and compare the spread between them.

**What to look for.** A later, private judgement can land nearer the
group's earlier norm than a person's own first guesses did, which is a
change in what a plausible answer looks like, not a decision to
conform. The four groups' norms on the same display sit roughly thirty
centimetres apart, from who spoke first alone.

**Debrief.** What would have to be true of the task for this
convergence to count as caving in rather than a reasonable use of
evidence?

**Common misconception / caution.** Converging is not being wrong.
With no information beyond three other estimates, using them is the
rational move, and there is no true answer here for anyone to have
abandoned.

**Use in class / timing.** A worked demonstration makes the point in
about 10 minutes. The full fifteen rounds with the debrief run to
about 35.
