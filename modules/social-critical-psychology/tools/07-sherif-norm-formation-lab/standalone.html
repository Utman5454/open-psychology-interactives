Social and Critical Psychology

Norm-Formation Laboratory

Fifteen rounds of a judgement with no right answer: five alone, five with three other people, and five alone again. Then a look at what three other groups arrived at.

Simulated - the other participants are generated by a model, and nothing you type is stored or sent

Learning objective

By the end you should be able to say why a frame formed with others persists once they are gone, and how that differs from agreeing with a demand.

About 25 minutes. Self-paced: nothing is timed. Nothing you type is saved or sent anywhere.

This is a demonstration, not a study

The other three participants are generated by a model whose rules are printed in the teaching notes. Your estimates are used only to draw the chart in front of you and are discarded when you leave the page. Nothing here measures anything about you: a person who converges and a person who does not are both showing something the activity is designed to make visible. The historical experiment this borrows its logic from is described separately, below the laboratory.

How to use this

  1. Commit to an answer to the question below.
  2. Make five estimates alone. There is no right answer and no scale; that is the condition, not a fault in the display.
  3. Make five more with three other people's estimates visible.
  4. Make five more alone again.
  5. Compare your group with three others.

First, a judgement

You are about to judge how far a faint marker moves in a dark field. There is no ruler, no reference object and no correct answer. After five rounds alone you will see three other people's estimates before each of the next five, and then you will be alone again.

    The historical experiment, described separately

    In the 1930s Muzafer Sherif used the autokinetic effect - a stationary point of light in a completely dark room appears to move, because the eye has no reference against which to stabilise it - to create judgements that could not be checked. Participants gave estimates of how far the light had travelled, first alone, then in small groups where estimates were spoken aloud, and in some conditions alone again afterwards.

    What he reported was not that people copied one another. Alone, individuals settled into their own characteristic range. In groups, those ranges converged towards a common one, and different groups converged on different values. When participants returned alone, they carried the group's range with them. Sherif read this as the formation of a frame of reference rather than as compliance: nobody had been asked to agree, nobody was watching, and the frame outlived the situation that produced it.

    This activity borrows that logic and none of its materials. There is no dark room, no point of light and no autokinetic effect here; the display is a drawing and the other participants are generated by a documented model. Nothing on this page is a replication, and no number produced here corresponds to anything Sherif reported.

    Criticisms worth knowing

    • Demand characteristics. Participants who have just heard three numbers may reasonably infer that agreement is what is wanted, and the design cannot separate that from a genuinely changed perception.
    • Ambiguity is doing a great deal of work. The effect is largest exactly where the judgement is least answerable, which limits how far it generalises to judgements that can be checked.
    • Small samples, and a particular population. The original studies used small numbers of young men in one country in one decade.
    • The persistence result has been contested. How long a frame survives, and whether it survives a change of setting, depends on things the original design did not vary.
    • A norm here is not a social norm. Converging on a number in a laboratory is not obviously the same process as the norms that govern queueing, dress or dissent, and treating them as one is an inference the study does not license.

    What this demonstrates

    Convergence under ambiguity is not caving in

    Nobody in this laboratory is asked to agree, nobody sees your answer, and there is no cost to differing. What the three other estimates provide is not pressure but information - the only information available about a judgement that has no answer. Taking it into account is reasonable. That is why this phenomenon is usually treated as the clearest case of informational influence, and why it sits so awkwardly beside the classic conformity studies, where the judgement was easy and the pressure was the point.

    A frame outlives the situation that produced it

    The interesting round is round 11. The others are gone, nobody is watching, and most people's estimates stay much closer to the group's range than to their own opening one. Whatever happened in rounds six to ten was not a decision to go along with anybody: it changed what a plausible answer looks like. That is the difference between complying with a demand and acquiring a definition of the situation.

    Different groups, different worlds

    The three other groups saw exactly the same display and arrived somewhere else. Nothing distinguishes their norms except who happened to be in the room and what they happened to say first. Once a norm exists it feels like a property of the display, which is the whole trouble: shared frames present themselves as facts about the world rather than as the residue of a particular set of conversations.

    Where this goes next

    The mechanism scales badly and interestingly. Later work has traced such frames across generations of participants, with each new arrival inheriting a norm from people who inherited it from people who invented it - and the norm surviving long after everyone who was present at the start has left. That is a laboratory model of tradition, and it is worth asking what it does and does not capture about the real thing.

    What this does not demonstrate

    • The other participants are not people. They are generated by a documented model that is built to converge. That the demonstration works is therefore not evidence that anything converges in the world; the evidence for that is in the literature, not on this page.
    • No autokinetic effect is involved. The display is a drawing with no scale. The ambiguity is stipulated, not perceptual, which is a real difference from the original work.
    • It does not show that all norms form this way. Most social norms are not arrived at by four strangers estimating an unanswerable quantity. Norms backed by sanctions, by institutions or by material interest are different objects.
    • Ambiguity is a boundary condition, not a detail. Where an answer can be checked, this process is much weaker, and the interesting cases in social life are usually a mixture.
    • Converging is not being wrong. With no information other than three other estimates, using them is the rational move. The demonstration is about how a shared reality gets built, not about a failure of judgement.
