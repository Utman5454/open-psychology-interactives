Social and Critical Psychology

Power Lens Laboratory

Nine things are known about a fictional further-education college and the air in its workshop block. Nothing is hidden and nothing is added. You will work through the same nine items three times, using three different analyses of power, and end up with three different lists of things to go and change.

Fictional case - Brackwell College, its committee papers and its staff exist only here

Learning objective

By the end you should be able to apply three analyses of power to one case, and say what each explains well, leaves in shadow, and implies as a remedy.

About 40 minutes. Nothing is timed, and nothing you select is saved or sent anywhere.

This is not a matching exercise

You will not be asked which theorist said what. Each lens is introduced by its central question rather than by a name, and the task is always to say what happens to this evidence when you look at it that way. The three lenses genuinely overlap on several items, and the feedback says so item by item rather than pretending each piece of evidence belongs to one of them. None of the three is the correct answer.

How to use this

  1. Read the case file and commit to an answer to the question below.
  2. For each lens in turn: select the evidence it brings into focus, and say what it leaves less visible.
  3. Read what the lens actually makes visible, what it costs, and the remedy that follows from it.
  4. Compare all three side by side at the synthesis stage.
  5. Then apply what you have to a different case.

The case file

Brackwell College, a fictional further-education college. For three years there have been reports about fumes, headaches and a persistent smell in Workshop 3. Nothing has been done. These nine items are everything a review found, and every lens below gets all nine.

    The laboratory

    Key terms
    Decisions, agendas, preferences
    Lukes's three dimensions: who prevails in an open contest, what reaches the agenda at all, and what people come to want.
    Normalisation
    A standard becoming the unremarkable measure cases are assessed against, without anybody having decided that it should be.
    Testimonial injustice
    A speaker being given less credibility than their account merits, because of who they are.
    Interpretive resources
    The shared concepts available for making sense of an experience. Where they are missing, something can happen that nobody can name.

    What each lens commits you to

    A framework's blind spot is not a fault that could be patched. It is the direct consequence of the thing that makes it useful, and each of these three buys its clarity with a specific commitment.

    Decisions, agendas and shaped preferences

    Commits you to the idea that there are real interests being thwarted - that the technicians would want something different if the situation were different. That is a strong claim about people's real interests, made by an analyst who is not them, and it has been the standing objection to this account since it was first put.

    Strongest where somebody could have acted and did not. Weakest where nobody is in charge of the thing doing the damage.

    Knowledge, classification and normalisation

    Commits you to treating power as productive and dispersed rather than held. That is what lets it see a definition or a sampling protocol as an exercise of power. It is also why it struggles to say who should be held responsible, or whose interests were served, without importing a vocabulary it has set aside.

    Strongest on the machinery that keeps running by itself. Weakest where somebody needs to be answerable.

    Credibility and interpretive resources

    Commits you to locating the wrong in an epistemic relation: who is believed, and what vocabulary is available to say things with. That makes a specific and often invisible harm nameable. It can also make an institutional problem look like a problem of individual virtue, and a budget line or a performance measure will keep working whatever anybody believes.

    Strongest on what happens to a speaker. Weakest on machinery that does not need a speaker at all.

    Challenge - a different case

    Ferrand Borough Council, also fictional. A three-year backlog of repairs on one estate has been reported to councillors as a resourcing problem. A review establishes four things. The repairs contractor is paid against a single measure, jobs closed per week. A job that is reopened within twenty-eight days is counted as a new job. Residents' calls are logged by the first keyword an operator selects from a fixed list of twelve. The tenants' association has asked in each of the last four years for "damp" to be added to that list, and no decision on the request has ever been recorded.

    What this demonstrates

    Nobody decided, and something still happened

    The single most useful discovery in the Brackwell file is that there is no decision in it. No meeting resolved that the workshop was safe. No manager weighed the technicians' reports and rejected them. An item was deferred, deferred again, moved to any other business and not minuted; a register ranked by insurance exposure never listed it; and people stopped reporting because they had learned what counted as a real problem. An account of power that can only look at decisions finds nothing here, which is precisely the argument for having accounts that look at agendas and at what people come to want.

    A definition can do what a decision would have had to do

    "A reportable environmental concern is one supported by an instrument reading", written into a manual by nobody in particular, settles the question of whose experience can enter the record - and it settles it in advance, permanently, without anybody having to take a view on Workshop 3. Add a survey that measured one point in a week the room was shut, and a summary method that ranks themes by keyword frequency, and the institution has produced a body of knowledge in which there is no problem. This is what it means to say that power works through what counts as knowing.

    Two different things go wrong with a speaker

    Eleven of fourteen reports came from technicians and evening students, and two of those became "odour complaint - no action". That is one harm: a speaker downgraded because of who they are. But there is a second, and it is not about belief at all - the system has no category for an intermittent exposure whose symptoms arrive hours later. Even a completely credible speaker has nothing to put in the box. Keeping those two apart is what stops "they weren't believed" from swallowing the whole case.

    Three remedies, and what each one leaves running

    Publish every deferral and let staff put items on the agenda, and the survey protocol still says there is no exceedance. Rewrite the definition and hand the technicians the monitoring equipment, and the committee can still defer it. Weight reports by exposure rather than by grade and add a category for delayed intermittent exposure, and the register still ranks by insurance value. Each remedy is real. None of them is sufficient, and the reason is that they are aimed at three different mechanisms that were all working at once.

    Where the problem is located, and who benefits

    Notice what each lens implies about who has to change. The first points at the people who run meetings. The second points at documents, protocols and categories, and at nobody in particular. The third points at how reports are received, which can slide into the individual virtue of whoever receives them. A college that wanted to look responsive while changing nothing would find the third framing the most comfortable, and a college that wanted nobody named would prefer the second. That is not an argument against either lens. It is a reason to notice which one an institution reaches for.

    What this does not demonstrate

    • Brackwell and Ferrand are inventions. The case, its papers, its survey and its staff were written for this page. No real institution, incident, inquiry or person is described.
    • The "correct" selections are a teaching key, not a fact. They are one defensible reading of an invented case, written to make the contrasts sharp. A well-argued selection the key marks as belonging to another lens may be better than the key.
    • These are not the only three lenses. Analyses built on class and material interest, on race, on gender, on disability, on organisational routine or on regulation would each bring different features of the same file into focus.
    • Applying a lens is not evidence. Deciding that the definition of a reportable concern is doing the work is an interpretation of the file, and a different analyst with the same nine items could reasonably reach somewhere else.
    • Nothing here diagnoses a real organisation. The activity teaches how to read a case; it is not an audit method and produces no finding about anywhere.
    • "All three at once" is not a conclusion. It is the beginning of the harder question, which is what to do first with a finite budget - and this tool does not answer it.
    • Each framework is contested in its own literature. The commitments panel above is a summary of live arguments, not a settled account of any of them.
