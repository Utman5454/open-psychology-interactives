# Teaching notes - Power Lens Laboratory

`modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/10-power-lens-lukes-foucault-fricker/`

**What it teaches.** Three lenses, named only by their analytic
question until debrief, bring different items in one case file into
focus and leave different things less visible. Each lens's remedy
fixes something specific and leaves something else running: rewriting
a reporting definition still leaves a committee's power to defer an
agenda item; universal belief still leaves no category for an
intermittent, delayed-onset exposure to be logged under. The three do
not reduce to interchangeable ways of saying power is everywhere.

**Before students start.** Read each lens's analytic question aloud
rather than announcing whose framework it is. The names, Lukes,
Foucault, Fricker, are deliberately withheld until debrief.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what best describes what happened at Brackwell.
2. Apply each of three lenses in turn: pick which case items it brings
   into focus, then read what it leaves less visible and the remedy
   it implies.
3. Judge whether one lens has it or all three mechanisms operate at
   once, then transfer the same three questions to a second case.

**What to look for.** The "what counts as knowing" lens needs no
agent at all: the reporting definition just runs, and would keep
running if every person in the file were replaced tomorrow. Each
lens's remedy leaves something specific standing: fixing the
definition still leaves the committee's power to defer an agenda
item; a fully credible workforce still leaves no category to log an
intermittent, delayed-onset exposure under.

**Debrief.** What would a critic holding all three lenses see that a
critic holding only one would miss?

**Common misconception / caution.** Do not flatten the three into
different ways of saying power is everywhere. One of them needs no
agent and does not require anyone to intend anything; treating every
interaction as evidence of domination erases the distinctions the
case is built to show. The credibility lens carries its own warning
too: applied carelessly, it can make an institutional problem look
like a problem of one person's character.

**Use in class / timing.** The worked example alone takes about 6
minutes. Add the transfer case, and the full sequence runs to about
30.
