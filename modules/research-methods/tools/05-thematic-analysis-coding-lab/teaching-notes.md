# Teaching notes - Thematic Analysis Coding Laboratory

`modules/research-methods/tools/05-thematic-analysis-coding-lab/`

**What it teaches.** A code in reflexive thematic analysis is an
analytic decision made from a stated position, not an extraction from
the transcript. Two competent analysts producing different codes for
the same extract is expected, not a reliability failure.

**Before students start.** Inter-rater agreement is not the standard
this method is judged against. Mention this before anyone starts
comparing codes for a "correct" match.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what determines whether a code is good.
2. For each extract, write your own code, then say whether it reads
   as semantic or latent.
3. Compare your reading against three prepared passes: semantic,
   latent and situated.
4. Answer a short reflexive prompt about your own position.
5. Move through as many of the six extracts as time allows.
6. Sort the closing challenge on which of five labels are usable
   codes.

**What to look for.** The three passes over the same extract produce
three different, defensible sets of codes. Watch for students
expecting one of the three to be the right answer rather than three
legitimate readings from different analytic positions.

**Debrief.** Three passes over the same extract produce three
different sets of codes. On what grounds could a reader reject one of
them? What is the difference between a code like "help-seeking as an
admission of deficit" and a paraphrase like "participant has low
academic self-efficacy"? Both sound analytic.

**Common misconception / caution.** "We should calculate inter-rater
reliability" reaches for the wrong standard: that fits
coding-reliability or content-analysis designs, not reflexive thematic
analysis, where the analyst's engagement is the instrument. Six short
extracts are not a dataset, and the tool never claims to demonstrate a
full transcript analysis.

**Use in class / timing.** Two or three extracts, shown from the
front, take about 12 minutes. All six with the challenge and debrief
take about 45.
