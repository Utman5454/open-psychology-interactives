# Teaching notes - Operationalisation Laboratory

`modules/research-methods/tools/02-operationalisation-laboratory/`

**What it teaches.** An operational definition is a claim, not a
translation. Every measure trades construct under-representation, the
facets it misses, against construct-irrelevant variance, the things it
also picks up, and combining measures raises coverage but cannot buy
the construct itself.

**Before students start.** The coverage percentage is arithmetic on a
facet list somebody wrote down, not a validity coefficient. Make that
clear before anyone reads too much into the number.

**What students do.**
1. Predict which single measure comes closest to academic engagement
   on its own.
2. Choose a construct and tick any combination of six measures.
3. Read the live coverage map and what else each chosen measure also
   records.
4. Tick every measure, then compare against a smaller, defensible
   two-measure plan.
5. Sort the closing challenge on a VLE-hours-only intervention design.

**What to look for.** Ticking all six measures still caps coverage at
nine of ten facet points, since one facet never reaches full coverage
no matter which measures are combined. Feasibility falls with each
measure added beyond two, so the fullest plan is also the least
practical one.

**Debrief.** Why does the tool take the highest facet level across
measures rather than adding them up? Two measures from the same family
agree closely; why is that weaker evidence than two measures from
different families agreeing?

**Common misconception / caution.** "Use more measures and you will
get there" is the misconception the all-six option is built to
puncture: coverage still stops short of ten, and feasibility collapses
as measures pile up. The coverage percentage itself is arithmetic on a
facet list somebody wrote down, not a validity coefficient.

**Use in class / timing.** A single-construct demonstration runs about
8 minutes. Comparing two constructs with the challenge and debrief
takes about 30 minutes.
