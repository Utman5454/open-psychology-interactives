# Teaching notes - Theme or Topic?

`modules/research-methods/tools/06-theme-or-topic-challenge/`

**What it teaches.** A developed theme is an analytic claim organised
around one idea that the extracts illustrate, not a summary of what is
most common or most often said. The three fabricated "overreach"
statements in the activity each fail for a different, specific reason.

**Before students start.** A topic summary, a staging sentence and a
theme are easy to blur. Telling them apart is the point of the
activity, not a warm-up before an obvious answer.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what actually distinguishes a theme from a topic.
2. For each of three clusters, classify four candidate statements as
   topic, staging, theme or overreach.
3. Check your classifications against the tool's notes.
4. Sort the closing challenge on which rewrites give a section a
   central organising concept.

**What to look for.** The reasonable-sounding, true, descriptive
sentence in each cluster, the staging candidate, is the one most often
mislabelled as the theme itself. Its position among the four options
changes from cluster to cluster, so do not expect it in the same slot
each time.

**Debrief.** The three overreaching candidates fail for three
different reasons; name them. Take the cluster 3 theme and try to move
one extract out of it. Does the theme change, and what does your
answer tell you?

**Common misconception / caution.** "A theme is what most people
said" treats prevalence as the criterion; a theme can rest on two
extracts showing one concept from different angles. The developed
themes given here are one defensible reading among several, not the
only correct one, and the activity itself accepts two different
rewrites as equally valid.

**Use in class / timing.** One cluster on its own runs about 10
minutes. All three with the rewrite challenge and debrief take about
35 minutes.
