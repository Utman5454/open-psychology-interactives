# Teaching notes - Confound Detective

`modules/research-methods/tools/03-confound-detective/`

**What it teaches.** A variable biases a comparison only when it both
differs systematically between conditions and has a route to the
outcome. A bigger sample narrows the interval around an estimate; it
cannot move the estimate itself when bias is present, and adjustment
only removes bias from confounders that were measured, measured well,
and modelled correctly.

**Before students start.** The bias figures throughout are invented
for teaching, not estimates of any real confounding. Mention this
before anyone treats a number here as a finding.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what must be true of a variable for it to count as a
   confound.
2. Classify five variables in the revision-app study as confound,
   nuisance, treatment or constant.
3. Apply repairs on the repair bench and watch the estimate move
   toward the true effect as bias is blocked.
4. Try recruiting a much larger sample instead, and compare what
   changes.
5. Repeat the classify-and-repair cycle for the quiet-ward and
   typeface studies.
6. Sort the closing challenge on a 200-volunteer exercise trial.

**What to look for.** Recruiting 400 students instead of 60 narrows the
interval but leaves the estimate at 17.0. Blocking tutor and time
brings it to 8.5, and only randomising away the selection bias brings
it further toward the true effect of 3.0. In the quiet-ward study the
unrepaired estimate is 2.5 days against a true effect of 0.3.

**Debrief.** Randomising students to app or no app leaves two large
biases untouched; why? In the ward study, adjusting for severity
removes about three-quarters of that bias. What would have to be true
for it to remove all of it, and why does randomisation not need that
condition?

**Common misconception / caution.** "A bigger sample will fix it" is
the one to correct directly: the sample-size repair narrows the
interval and leaves the estimate exactly where it was. The bias
figures throughout are invented for teaching, not estimates of any
real confounding.

**Use in class / timing.** Ten minutes covers one study as a
demonstration. All three studies with the challenge and debrief take
about 45 minutes.
