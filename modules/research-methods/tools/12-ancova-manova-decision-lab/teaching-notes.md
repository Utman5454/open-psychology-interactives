# Teaching notes - ANCOVA / MANOVA Decision Laboratory

`modules/research-methods/tools/12-ancova-manova-decision-lab/`

**What it teaches.** Under random allocation, a covariate mainly buys
precision, since adjustment barely moves the estimate. Under
non-equivalent, intact groups, the identical calculation is not a
causal estimate, only how the design menu labels what the number is
allowed to mean. Separately, a joint separation between groups on two
outcomes at once is not several ANOVAs for free: whether it adds
anything over looking at outcomes one at a time depends entirely on
how correlated those outcomes are within each group, and a significant
joint result does not exempt follow-up tests from correction for
multiple comparisons.

**Before students start.** Changing "how groups were formed" between
random allocation and intact classes changes no number on screen, only
what the number is allowed to mean.

**What students do.**
1. Assign roles, outcome, factor, covariate or unusable, to three
   variables in a fictional programme evaluation, then choose ANOVA,
   ANCOVA, MANOVA or a change-score t-test.
2. In Experiment 1, set the covariate-outcome correlation, the group
   gap on the covariate, and how the groups were formed, then draw a
   new sample.
3. Compare the unadjusted and adjusted group difference under random
   allocation, then under intact groups with the same numbers.
4. In Experiment 2, set two outcome differences and a within-group
   correlation, or load a preset, and compare per-outcome separation
   with the joint distance.
5. Sort the closing challenge on which of four fictional designs calls
   for ANCOVA, MANOVA, ANOVA or neither.

**What to look for.** Under random allocation, adjusting for the
covariate barely moves the group difference, since the groups already
start equal on average. Under intact groups with a real gap on the
covariate, the same adjustment moves the estimate substantially, and
the tool insists that estimate still be reported as an adjusted
association, not a causal effect.

**Debrief.** Under random allocation, why does adjustment barely move
the estimate; what is the covariate doing instead? A colleague says
MANOVA means they do not have to correct for multiple comparisons
afterwards; what has gone wrong?

**Common misconception / caution.** "ANCOVA controls for pre-existing
differences" treats adjustment as equivalent to randomisation.
Switching the design menu here changes zero numbers, only the warrant
for what the number means. Whether the joint separation in Experiment
2 adds anything over the two outcomes taken one at a time depends
entirely on their within-group correlation, not on using a
fancier-sounding test.

**Use in class / timing.** Fifteen minutes covers Experiment 1 on its
own. Both experiments with the challenge and debrief take about 50
minutes.
