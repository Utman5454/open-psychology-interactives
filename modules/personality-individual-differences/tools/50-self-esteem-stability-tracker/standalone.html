What this tool will not do

It asks you nothing about yourself, offers no questionnaire and produces no score for anybody. The four people are invented and the scale is arbitrary. Low or variable values here are characteristics that differ between people, not symptoms, and nothing on this page is a clinical statement, a screening instrument or a basis for interpreting anyone's wellbeing — your own included.

Key terms
Level
Where somebody's self-esteem typically sits.
Variability
How much it moves from day to day, which is independent of its level.
Contingency
Which domains it moves in response to. Two people can be equally variable and contingent on quite different things.
Recovery
How quickly it returns after a knock — a third property, not implied by either of the other two.

What this demonstrates

Four characteristics, one number

Level, volatility, contingency and recovery are separable, and a single self-esteem score reports only the first — badly, because it is a sample of one occasion from a moving series. Two people with the same score can differ on all three of the others, which is what the four fictional people here are built to show.

Instability is not low level

Ari and Bea share a baseline. One barely moves and the other swings widely, and neither is lower than the other on average. These are independent dimensions, and the research that separated them found they predict different things — which they could not do if one were a proxy for the other.

Contingency is about what, not how much

Cal is steady until something happens at work. The same event elsewhere barely registers. That is not greater sensitivity in general; it is self-evaluation staked on a particular domain. Someone can be highly contingent and, in a period when that domain is going well, look entirely stable.

Recovery is a third thing again

Dee reacts no more strongly than average and takes far longer to return. Measured once, a fortnight after an event, she would look like a person with lower self-esteem rather than a person still coming back from something. One observation cannot distinguish between a level and a position on a trajectory.

What this does not demonstrate

  • Variability is not pathology. Describing someone as unstable on the basis of a graph is the move to avoid, and no pattern shown here is a symptom, a disorder or a risk indicator.
  • These are not data. A generator, four parameter sets and a decay rate. No study, sample or measure is represented.
  • The model is simple. Real self-evaluation is not a single quantity with additive shocks: it is domain specific, socially embedded, partly reconstructed at recall, and shaped by what a person does in response to an event. None of that is modelled.
