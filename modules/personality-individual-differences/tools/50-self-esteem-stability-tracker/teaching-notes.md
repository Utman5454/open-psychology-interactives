# Teaching notes - Self-Esteem Stability Tracker

`modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/50-self-esteem-stability-tracker/`

**What it teaches.** Four fictional people share the same baseline
level but differ in three separable characteristics: how much they
wobble day to day, how strongly an event in a given domain moves them,
and how quickly that movement fades. The same criticism delivered in a
domain that matters to someone barely registers if delivered in a
domain that does not, and how long its effect lasts depends on a
separate recovery parameter that has nothing to do with how large the
initial drop was.

**Before students start.** None needed.

**What students do.**
1. Predict whether two people with the same average level over six
   weeks must be similarly stable.
2. Compare the four people's day-to-day wobble, independent of any
   event.
3. Deliver the same event to a domain that matters a great deal to one
   person and very little to another, and compare the size of the
   drop.
4. Compare how many days it takes each person's level to return near
   baseline after an event.

**What to look for.** The same criticism delivered in a person's
highest-contingency domain produces a far larger drop than the
identical criticism delivered in their lowest-contingency domain,
sometimes by a factor of seven or more. Two people can drop by a
similar amount and then diverge sharply in how long the effect lasts,
because contingency governs the size of a reaction and recovery
governs its duration, and the two are set independently for each
person.

**Debrief.** Two people show the same average level across six weeks.
What three further things would you need to know before saying
anything about how stable each one actually is?

**Common misconception / caution.** High day-to-day variability is
not a symptom or a sign of a problem; it is one of several separable
characteristics a person can be described on, alongside domain
contingency and recovery, and none of these four fictional people is
written as healthier than the others. A single administration, or a
single average, cannot distinguish any of these characteristics from
each other.

**Use in class / timing.** Comparing two people's wobble and one
domain-contingent event takes about 10 minutes. Covering all four
people and recovery, allow 25 minutes.
