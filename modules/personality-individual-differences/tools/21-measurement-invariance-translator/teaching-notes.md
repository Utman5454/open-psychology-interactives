# Teaching notes - Measurement-Invariance Translator

`modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/21-measurement-invariance-translator/`

**What it teaches.** Two fictional groups, A and B, answer a four-item
assertiveness scale. Loading "one item harder to endorse" gives both
groups a latent mean of exactly 0.00, identical on the trait, and yet
their observed means differ, because a single item's intercept
changed: it costs more to endorse "I speak up when I disagree with the
group" in one setting than the other. A study comparing raw means
would report a group difference that is really a property of one
item.

**Before students start.** Groups A and B carry no nationality,
ethnicity or culture; the mechanism is about a setting an item is
answered from, not about who answers it.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what a significant, large-sample group difference on the
   scale establishes.
2. Load "Everything equal," then "A real trait difference," and note
   that the two groups' item response lines still coincide in both.
3. Load "One item harder to endorse" and watch a vertical gap open
   between the lines while the latent means stay at zero.
4. Load "One item measures differently" and watch a slope difference
   appear, then take the challenge scored against whatever is
   currently set.

**What to look for.** Configural invariance holds whenever both groups
are described by the same one-factor shape; metric invariance
additionally requires equal loadings, licensing comparison of
relationships; scalar invariance additionally requires equal
intercepts, licensing comparison of means. The intercept preset holds
configural and metric but not scalar, and its observed means differ
despite identical latent means. The loading preset breaks metric as
well, at which point even a correlation comparison is off the table.

**Debrief.** A study reports metric but not scalar invariance. What
can it still claim about the two groups, and what can it no longer
claim?

**Common misconception / caution.** Losing scalar invariance does not
mean the scale is broken or that a group difference can no longer be
detected honestly; it means mean comparisons specifically are
unsupported until it is re-established or partial invariance is
argued for. Reaching scalar invariance also does not mean the two
groups are equal, only that comparing their means is now licensed: the
"real trait difference" preset holds all three levels and still shows
a genuine difference.

**Use in class / timing.** The intercept preset alone runs about 8
minutes. With the challenge and debrief, allow 30 to 35 minutes.
