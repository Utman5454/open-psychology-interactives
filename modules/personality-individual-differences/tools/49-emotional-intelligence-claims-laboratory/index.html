Personality and Individual Differences

Emotional-Intelligence Claims Laboratory

Two fictional measures sold as emotional intelligence. One overlaps a personality questionnaire; the other overlaps a reasoning test. So the question is what either of them adds.

Simulated — fictional measures, no published test reproduced

Learning objective

By the end you should be able to say why "it correlates with the outcome" is a weaker claim than "it adds something we did not already have".

About 20 minutes. Nothing you do here is saved or sent anywhere.

What these measures are

All six are fictional descriptions of method types — a questionnaire, tasks with scored answers, ratings by other people. No published emotional-intelligence measure is reproduced, quoted, paraphrased or scored here, and no number on this page is an estimate of any real quantity. The tool takes no position on whether emotional intelligence exists. It is about what a correlation matrix does and does not license somebody to advertise.

How to use this

  1. Commit to an answer to the question below.
  2. Read the three bars.
  3. Slide the one control to the right and watch them swap over.
  4. Move to step 2 and ask whether either measure adds anything.
  5. Judge the five marketing claims against what you found.

First, a judgement

A fictional research group administers four measures, all described in the literature as measures of emotional intelligence: a questionnaire about how you usually handle feelings, a task whose answers are scored against a key, an emotion-recognition accuracy test, and ratings of social effectiveness by colleagues.

How will the four measures correlate with each other?

The laboratory

Six fictional measures: three carry the emotional-intelligence label, one is the outcome the label is sold on, and two are the ordinary measures any claim of added value has to beat. The matrix is what the model implies; the scatterplot shows 300 simulated people drawn from it.

Need a hand?

Nothing here changes the controls for you, and none of it says what you are going to find.

Key terms
Convergent validity
Two measures of the same construct correlating, as they should if they are both measuring it.
Method variance
Shared variance arising from how measures were collected rather than from what they measure. Two self-report scales share a method whatever their content.
Incremental validity
What a measure adds to prediction once the established predictors are already in the model.
Construct proliferation
Introducing a new construct that substantially overlaps ones already in use.

Six measures, one matrix

The sliders do not change anyone's scores. They change what the measures are assumed to be made of — and every correlation on screen follows from that.

    Challenge — five things somebody wants to say

    Each of these is the kind of sentence that appears in a brochure, a press release or a training-day slide. Judge each one against the evidence as you currently have the laboratory set. There is no stored answer key: change the settings and some of these verdicts change with them.

    Claim 1. "Our emotional-intelligence questionnaire measures a distinct capability that ordinary personality questionnaires miss."
    Claim 2. "Emotional intelligence predicts effectiveness at work better than IQ does."
    Claim 3. "Because our task has right and wrong answers, it measures an ability rather than a self-image."
    Claim 4. "All of these measures assess the same underlying emotional intelligence."
    Claim 5. "Scoring high on our emotion-recognition test shows you will be socially effective."

    What this demonstrates

    The self-report questionnaire overlaps a personality questionnaire heavily; the emotion task overlaps a reasoning test. Both are sold under one label, and they barely agree with each other. That is why the question worth asking is never whether a measure correlates with the outcome, but whether it adds anything once the ordinary measures are already in.

    What this does not demonstrate

    • It does not show that emotional intelligence is nothing. Ability measures of emotion perception behave like modest, real abilities. The argument is about what a shared label licenses, not about whether anything is there.
    • The figures are generated, not found. The six measures are fictional method types and every correlation comes from the model described in the teaching notes. No published test is reproduced and no number estimates anything.
