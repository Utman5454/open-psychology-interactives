# Teaching notes - Response-Style Simulator

`modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/14-response-style-simulator/`

**What it teaches.** Seven fictional respondents share exactly the same
true standing on a trait, but seven different ways of answering a
twenty-item questionnaire turn that single standing into seven
different totals. A scale's raw total reflects how someone uses the
response scale as well as what they think, and only balanced keying,
alternating positively and reverse-worded items, keeps an
indiscriminate agreer from being mistaken for a high scorer.

**Before students start.** Read the "word about moralising" panel
before starting; these patterns are not misbehaviour to be judged.

**What students do.**
1. Look at the raw answer grids for all seven respondents before any
   statistic is computed, and notice which patterns are obvious.
2. Read the summary table of five detection statistics and see which
   style each one catches and which slips through.
3. Switch balanced keying off and watch the acquiescent and attentive
   respondents' totals, close together with balanced keying, pull far
   apart without it.
4. Take the identification challenge: name a mystery pattern, then
   answer separately whether that pattern establishes a motive.

**What to look for.** With balanced keying, the acquiescent and
attentive respondents' totals sit about six points apart out of a
hundred; switch keying off and the gap widens to about fifteen,
because nothing now cancels an indiscriminate "agree." A small
residual gap remains even with balanced keying, because the
acquiescent shift is truncated once an answer hits the top of the
scale. Split-half correlation catches random responding and the
longest-run statistic catches straight-lining, but neither one flags
acquiescence.

**Debrief.** Seven totals, one true standing. Which of those totals is
the person's actual score? What did switching the keying change about
the seven people, and what did it change about the questionnaire?

**Common misconception / caution.** A response pattern is evidence
about a pattern, not about a motive: the same grid a careless
respondent produces can also come from a genuinely consistent person
answering a short unbalanced scale, or from someone for whom the items
truly do not apply. Reverse-worded items counter acquiescence but are
harder to read and tend to form their own cluster in a factor
analysis, a trade rather than a free fix.

**Use in class / timing.** The keying switch alone runs about 8
minutes. With the identification challenge and debrief, allow 30 to
35 minutes.
