# Teaching notes - Intelligence-Test Battery Builder

`modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/31-intelligence-test-battery-builder/`

**What it teaches.** Building a test battery trades off coverage
across ability domains, combined reliability, dependence on prior
opportunity, and how much time and effort the battery costs, and no
single battery wins on all four at once. The same seven tasks, chosen
for a research purpose, an educational placement, or a screening
purpose, score differently against each scenario's own priorities,
because validity is a property of a use rather than of a test.

**Before students start.** Some sense of what a reliability coefficient
is helps; the seven tasks and three scenarios are fixed and visible
from the start.

**What students do.**
1. Predict whether adding more tasks to a battery always makes it more
   valid.
2. Select tasks freely against one scenario, watching coverage,
   combined reliability, exposure-dependence and burden update live.
3. Switch the scenario without changing the selection and see the same
   battery scored differently.
4. Try to submit a battery that satisfies a scenario's own time and
   burden limits.

**What to look for.** Vocabulary has the single highest reliability of
the seven tasks and also the second-highest dependence on prior
opportunity, so raising reliability by including it is not free. A
battery built to satisfy the research scenario, which weights coverage
most heavily, can fail the screening scenario outright on time or
burden alone, with no change to the tasks themselves. Combined
reliability rises with more tasks by a Spearman-Brown-type formula,
but coverage depends on which domains those tasks actually sample, not
on how many tasks there are.

**Debrief.** A battery's fit score went up when a task was added. Did
the battery get better, or only better at what this particular
weighted sum rewards?

**Common misconception / caution.** More subtests does not
automatically mean a more valid battery; validity is a property of how
scores are used, not a number a test carries around, and a battery
excellent for research can be a poor choice for a twenty-minute
screening. Dependence on prior opportunity is a property of a task,
not of any person or group who takes it.

**Use in class / timing.** Building one battery against one scenario
takes about 10 minutes. Comparing the same battery across all three
scenarios, allow 25 minutes.
