Personality and Individual Differences

Person–Situation Interaction Theatre

The same four people, five situations. Predict how they will rank before you see it — then find the situation that reorders them, and the one that makes them indistinguishable.

Simulated data — fictional characters, illustrative scores

Learning objective

By the end you should be able to say what makes a situation strong, and why neither “personality matters” nor “situations matter” survives as a summary.

About 25 minutes. Nothing you do here is saved or sent anywhere.

How to use this

  1. Read the four characters.
  2. Rank them for a party, and check.
  3. Rank them for an anonymous online discussion. Most people get this one wrong, which is the point.
  4. Then take the controls: change the situation, its strength, and whether anyone has been given a role.
  5. Try to make the four behaviourally indistinguishable.

The cast

Four fictional people. The trait scores are illustrative values on an arbitrary 0–100 scale — nobody was measured, and these are not norms.

    Now take the controls

    Key terms
    Situation strength
    How strongly a situation constrains what people do in it. Here it is a dimension you set, not a category.
    Affordance
    What a situation makes possible, as distinct from what it forbids. Two situations can constrain equally and afford differently.
    Aggregation
    Averaging behaviour across many occasions rather than reading one.

    The theatre

    What this demonstrates

    Behaviour is a function of both, and the question is badly posed

    The person–situation debate was conducted for two decades as though one side had to win. In the model here neither term is privileged: behaviour is built from a person term and a situation term, and which one dominates is set by the strength of the situation. Asking whether personality or situations matter more is like asking whether length or width matters more to area.

    Strong situations hide people; they do not change them

    Push the strength slider up and the four characters converge. The trait scores in the cast list do not move — nothing about the people has changed. What has changed is that the situation has stopped letting differences show. This matters for reading research: a study run entirely in strong situations will find personality predicts very little, and that finding is about the situations sampled rather than about personality.

    Affordances, not just constraint

    The interesting comparison is not weak versus strong but what each situation affords. The party and the anonymous discussion are both weak, and they rank the cast differently, because one rewards sociability and the other removes the cost of bluntness. Consistency is high across situations that afford the same traits and low across situations that do not — which is exactly what the evidence on behavioural consistency shows.

    Aggregation is why traits look real

    A single act in a single situation is weakly predicted by a trait score. Averaged across many situations and occasions, the same trait predicts well. Both facts are true at once, and the apparent contradiction dissolves as soon as you notice they are claims about different things — one behaviour, or a tendency across many.

    What this does not demonstrate

    • These are not data. Four invented people, five invented situations, and behaviour computed from a formula. No one was observed, and no number here is an effect size from any study.
    • Situation strength is set by hand here. In real research it is inferred from observed behaviour — which makes "behaviour varied little because the situation was strong" close to circular unless strength is established independently. Worth raising with a class rather than hiding.
    • No occasion-to-occasion variability. The same person in the same situation always does the same thing here. Real behaviour varies across occasions even with everything else held constant; the State versus Trait Tracker in this module is where that enters.
    • Traits do not combine or interact. The person term is a weighted sum, so no trait moderates another. Real interactions of that kind exist and are hard to detect.
    • The affordance weights are stipulated. Which traits a situation affords is a substantive empirical question, and the values here were chosen to make the teaching point legible, not measured.
    • Nobody is assessed. You rank fictional characters. The tool asks nothing about you and produces no description of you.
