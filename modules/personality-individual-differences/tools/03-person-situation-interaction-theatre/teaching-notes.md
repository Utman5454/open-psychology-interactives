# Teaching notes - Person-Situation Interaction Theatre

`modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/03-person-situation-interaction-theatre/`

**What it teaches.** A trait and a situation both shape behaviour, and a
strong situation narrows the gap between people without changing their
traits at all. Weak situations, like a party or an anonymous online
discussion, let dispositions show through; a maximally strong one
compresses everyone toward the same behaviour while their trait scores
in the cast list never move. Comparing the rankings from several
situations at once shows how much they agree with each other and
which situations reorder the same four people; it does not converge on
one privileged ordering that the individual situations were hiding.

**Before students start.** Students should already have the idea of a personality trait; Mara,
Jonah, Elif and Theo and all five situations are fictional and shown
as such throughout.

**What students do.**
1. Rank four fictional people twice, once for a party and once for an
   anonymous online discussion, before either is revealed.
2. Compare the prediction against the two actual rankings.
3. Open the situation explorer: pick a situation, drag its strength,
   and add a leadership or observer role.
4. Try to drive the spread between the four people below two points in
   the challenge, then answer what that does and does not show.

**What to look for.** At each situation's own default strength, the
five situations order the four people quite differently. Applying one
shared strength across all five at once gives only moderate agreement
between them, around 0.3 on the tool's own rank-correlation readout,
low enough to trigger its "low agreement" verdict. Pushing strength
toward its ceiling collapses the gap between the four people to a
fraction of a point, while their trait scores never change.

**Debrief.** If four people behave almost identically at a strong
party, has anything about their personalities changed? Two situations
give the four people very different rankings. What would you need to
see across many situations before concluding one of the two rankings
is the more trustworthy one?

**Common misconception / caution.** A strong situation compressing
behaviour is not evidence that traits stopped existing, only that the
situation is currently doing most of the explanatory work. The
opposite error is just as common: a single weak situation showing
large individual differences does not mean traits "won" either, since
a different situation could reorder the same four people. Situation
strength here is stipulated by the activity, not measured from any
real setting.

**Use in class / timing.** A single ranking-and-reveal round takes
about 8 minutes. With the explorer and challenge, allow 25 to 30
minutes.
