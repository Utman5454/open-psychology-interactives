# Teaching notes - State versus Trait Tracker

`modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/04-state-versus-trait-tracker/`

**What it teaches.** A trait is a person's typical level across many
occasions, not the value read off any single one. Four fictional
people are sampled four times a day for a fortnight, each with a
stable mean and a characteristic amount of day-to-day wobble; one
reading can land almost anywhere in that spread. A single
administration can never establish how variable a person is, because
variability is a property of a series, not of one point in it.

**Before students start.** Students should already be able to tell a
trait from a single measurement of it; the fortnight of readings
regenerates from the seed shown in the controls.

**What students do.**
1. See one reading for each of four people and rank them, then reveal
   the fortnight each reading came from.
2. Move the variability and measurement-error sliders and watch the
   four running means and spreads respond differently.
3. Use the stability chart to compare how quickly a calm and a
   volatile person's running mean settles near its true value.
4. Try the two challenges: what a single reading can and cannot
   divide, and what raising measurement error does and does not
   change.

**What to look for.** With the default fortnight, two of the four
people share almost the same mean, around 62, while their spreads sit
worlds apart, around 4.5 for one and 18.6 for the other; a single
reading from the more volatile of the two can land twenty points from
his own mean. Both running means converge on their true average, but
at very different rates: the calmer person's settles within the first
couple of readings, the volatile one's keeps drifting for roughly
forty readings, well over half the fortnight. Raising measurement
error adds noise to every reading and never changes anyone's
underlying trait, though it can nudge a person's own displayed average
slightly too, since the same fixed draws are scaled larger rather than
redrawn.

**Debrief.** Two people share a trait score. Does that mean they
behave alike from one day to the next? What would it take to find out
how variable someone actually is, and why can a single reading never
answer that?

**Common misconception / caution.** High day-to-day variability is not
itself a sign of a problem, only a dimension people differ on; the
most volatile person here is written no less favourably than the
calmest. Variability and measurement error are easy to run together,
which is why the tool keeps them on separate sliders: one changes how
much a person truly moves, the other changes what a single reading
might mean.

**Use in class / timing.** A quick pass through the ranking, sliders
and stability chart runs about 10 minutes. With both challenges,
allow 25 minutes.
