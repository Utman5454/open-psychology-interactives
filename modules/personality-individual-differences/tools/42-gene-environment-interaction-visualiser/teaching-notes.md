# Teaching notes - Gene × Environment Interaction Visualiser

`modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/42-gene-environment-interaction-visualiser/`

**What it teaches.** Two fictional sensitivity groups respond to
environmental quality with different slopes, a genuine interaction,
and where the two group lines cross decides whether the same two
slopes look like diathesis-stress, differential susceptibility, or
vantage sensitivity. All three "theories" here are the identical pair
of slopes with only the crossover point moved; which one a study
appears to support can also change just by restricting which part of
the environmental range gets sampled, with neither slope nor crossover
moving at all.

**Before students start.** This works best once the group can already
tell a main effect from an interaction; the two fictional sensitivity
groups are not modelled on any real genotype.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what a study finding "the more sensitive group does worse
   only in poor environments" establishes about where the two groups'
   outcomes would cross if a wider range were sampled.
2. Load each of the three presets and see that the two slopes never
   change, only where they cross.
3. Load differential susceptibility, then narrow the sampled range to
   only the adverse half, and watch the pattern read as
   diathesis-stress instead.
4. Try to recover vantage sensitivity from the same underlying model
   by sampling only the supportive half.

**What to look for.** With the full environmental range sampled,
differential susceptibility is visible as two lines crossing inside
the window. Restricting the sampled range to only the adverse half
hides the crossing and the pattern reads as diathesis-stress, even
though the crossover point itself has not moved. The reverse
restriction, sampling only the supportive half, makes the same
underlying model read as vantage sensitivity.

**Debrief.** A study samples only a narrow slice of environmental
quality and reports diathesis-stress. What would sampling a wider
range potentially reveal that this study cannot rule out?

**Common misconception / caution.** No gene is named or modelled here,
and nothing in the tool licenses a claim about a specific biological
mechanism; the three "theories" are three positions for the same
crossover, not three different underlying processes. A found
interaction in a real study is not evidence of a gene-environment
correlation, which is a separate pattern; sampling here draws
environment independently of group.

**Use in class / timing.** Comparing the three presets takes about 8
minutes. Adding the range-restriction exploration, allow 20 to 25
minutes.
