Learning objective

By the end you should be able to tell diathesis–stress from differential susceptibility by their patterns, and say how the sampled range decides which one you appear to see.

About 25 minutes. Nothing you do here is saved or sent anywhere.

What is absent

No gene is named and no genotype appears — the grouping variable is a fictional sensitivity score. No disorder, diagnosis or clinical risk estimate is used; the outcome axis is an unnamed standardised outcome. A slope here is an average tendency across a group, never a prediction about a person, and no figure corresponds to any published finding.

Key terms
Diathesis–stress
A pattern in which one group fares worse than the other in adverse conditions and no better in favourable ones.
Differential susceptibility
A pattern in which one group fares worse in adverse conditions and better in favourable ones, so that the two lines cross.
Crossover point
Where the two lines meet.
Range restriction
Sampling only part of the environmental range, leaving the rest of the picture unobserved.

What this demonstrates

Diathesis-stress, differential susceptibility and vantage sensitivity are the same two lines meeting at different places. Which one a study reports depends on how much of the environment it sampled: narrow the range and a crossover that is really there disappears, or an ordinary difference in slope starts to look like one.

What this does not demonstrate

  • The lines are drawn, not fitted. No gene, no measure and no clinical outcome is involved. The two groups are labelled by sensitivity because that is the distinction the three theories turn on, and every figure comes from the straight lines you set.
  • A significant interaction is a beginning. It says two slopes differ in this sample, over this range. It does not say which of the three patterns produced them, and it does not identify anybody as a sensitive type.
