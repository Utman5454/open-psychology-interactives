The one sentence to take away

Heritability is the proportion of variance in a population attributable to genetic variance in that population under those environmental conditions. It is not the proportion of any individual's trait caused by their genes, and it says nothing about whether a trait can be changed.

What this demonstrates

Heritability is a ratio, and ratios have denominators

h² is genetic variance divided by total variance in a particular population. Shrink the environmental variance — equalise schooling, nutrition, income — and heritability rises without a single gene changing. It follows that a high heritability may describe a society that has succeeded in making environments more similar, and a low one may describe a society where environments differ enormously. The number is as much about the population as about the trait.

It is not about individuals, at all

There is no coherent way to divide one person's characteristic into a genetic portion and an environmental portion. They had one genome and one life, and both were necessary for all of it. A statement about the sources of variation between people is not a statement about the composition of any one of them.

It says nothing about malleability

Height is highly heritable and rose substantially across the twentieth century through nutrition. Needing spectacles is highly heritable and is corrected with a pair of spectacles. A heritable trait is not a fixed one, and the inference from one to the other has caused real harm.

The assumptions are the argument

Falconer's formulae are three lines of arithmetic resting on substantive claims: that identical and fraternal twins share their environments to the same extent, that parents pair at random with respect to the trait, and that genetic and environmental influences are independent. All three are known to fail to some degree, and they do not all fail in the same direction — which is why the responsible reading of a heritability estimate is as a rough indication with a direction of likely bias attached.

What this does not demonstrate

  • These are not data. Fictional pairs from a seeded generator. No real twin study, family or dataset is represented, and no trait is named — deliberately, because the argument is about the method.
  • Nothing here concerns any group of people. The simulation has one undifferentiated population. It makes no claim, and can make no claim, about differences between populations — a question to which heritability within groups is simply not the relevant evidence.
  • The model is the simplest possible one. Purely additive genetic effects: no dominance, no epistasis, no sex differences, no age moderation, no gene–environment interaction (as distinct from correlation, which is modelled). Real behaviour-genetic models fit many more parameters.
  • Falconer's method is not what modern studies use. It is shown because it makes the logic visible in three lines. Current work uses structural equation models over many family relationships, with confidence intervals and model comparison.
  • No confidence intervals are shown. Press "draw a new sample" repeatedly to see how much the estimates move between samples of this size — that movement is what an interval would summarise.
  • Nobody is assessed. You set parameters and read estimates. The tool asks nothing about you or your family.
