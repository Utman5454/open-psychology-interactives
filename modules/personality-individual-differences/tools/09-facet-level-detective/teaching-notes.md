# Teaching notes - Facet-Level Detective

`modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/09-facet-level-detective/`

**What it teaches.** Two fictional people can share the same broad
trait score while their behaviour over several weeks diverges
completely, because a domain score is a weighted average of two
facets and an average cannot tell two profiles apart when they reach
it from opposite directions. Facet scores predict specific behaviour
better than the domain does; the domain predicts breadth, stability
and aggregate outcomes better than any single facet can.

**Before students start.** Decide whether the opening question is
answered individually or as a room; a skip button covers the second
case.

**What students do.**
1. Predict why two people with near-identical broad scores behave
   differently, before any facet score is shown.
2. Assign four behavioural observations to the two people using
   behaviour alone.
3. Reveal the facet profiles and compare the domain-based prediction
   against the facet-based one for every observation.
4. Build a matched pair in the studio, then drag the questionnaire's
   item mix and watch the two people's scores separate and cross
   over.

**What to look for.** In each case the two people are built so their
broad domain scores land within a point of each other, while their two
facet scores differ by twenty points or more in opposite directions.
The domain-based prediction column is identical for both people on
every observation; only the facet-based column tells them apart.
Dragging the item mix moves the two domain scores in opposite
directions and can reverse which of the two now scores higher,
without changing either person.

**Debrief.** The prediction table shows two identical domain-based
columns beside two facet-based columns that differ sharply. Is that a
flaw in the questionnaire, or a property of what an average
necessarily throws away? If facets predict specific behaviour better,
why does anyone still report domain scores?

**Common misconception / caution.** Neither level is the "real"
trait; they are descriptions at different grains, and the choice
between them depends on what is being predicted. Facet scores are not
simply more informative for free: they rest on fewer items, carry
lower reliability, and facet boundaries themselves are far less
settled in the literature than the five domains are.

**Use in class / timing.** A single case from prediction to reveal
takes about 8 minutes. With the studio and item-mix stage, allow 25
to 30 minutes.
