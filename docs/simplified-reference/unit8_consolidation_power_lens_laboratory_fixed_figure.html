Lens 1

A community service is being closed

Use each lens to decide which part of the same case it would bring most sharply into focus.

A local authority plans to close a neighbourhood support centre.

Residents challenge the decision, but the closure process also involves meeting agendas, official reports, eligibility rules, monitoring data and public consultation.

Compare

One case, three views of power

The lenses overlap, but they do different analytic work. None gives a complete account on its own.

Lukes

Looks at visible decisions, control of the agenda, and how power can shape what people come to accept as realistic or inevitable.

Foucault

Looks at routines, surveillance, normalisation and the ways institutions produce categories and subject positions.

Fricker

Looks at credibility and interpretive resources: who is believed, and whether people can make their experience intelligible to others.

Key idea: A power lens changes what we treat as evidence. Looking across lenses helps us see power in decisions, institutional practices, credibility and the boundaries of what people come to see as possible. Collective organisation can challenge those arrangements rather than treating powerlessness as an individual deficit.
