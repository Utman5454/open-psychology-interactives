Interactive teaching tools for psychology

A growing, free collection of browser-based tools for university teaching, organised into five modules across the psychology curriculum. Everything runs in the browser, works on a phone or a lecture-theatre projector, and can be reused or adapted for your own course.

Five modules

Each module groups tools around the ideas students find hardest to picture. The modules are set up and their scope is described; the tools themselves are being written now.

  • Module 01

    Cognitive Psychology

    Selective attention, working memory, reconstructive memory, perception as inference and judgement under uncertainty — the processes students are usually asked to take on trust.

  • Module 02

    Research Methods

  • Module 03

    Neuropsychology

    Attention, memory and executive function paradigms, plus the logic of dissociations — so students can run the task before they read about the finding.

9 tools available

All of them are in Personality and Individual Differences, which is being built out first. The other four modules are still at the scaffold stage: their structure, shared styling and accessibility groundwork are in place and they are ready to receive their first tools.

  • Person–Situation Interaction Theatre — Four fictional characters are put through five situations.
  • State versus Trait Tracker — Simulated experience-sampling data for four fictional people over a fortnight.
  • Facet-Level Detective — Two fictional people share a broad domain score almost exactly and behave quite differently.
  • The Alpha Trap — Students maximise Cronbach's alpha by choosing items from a simulated bank, discover that the cheapest route is to ask the same question five ways, and then see the narrow scale they have built fail against a simulated outcome.
  • “Explain This Person” Courtroom — One fictional behaviour and eight explanations that all fit it.
  • Intelligence-Test Battery Builder — Seven generic cognitive task families, one testing session, and three different purposes.
  • Culture-Fair Test Challenge — Students design a non-verbal reasoning task through six decisions and watch six construct-irrelevant demands respond.
  • Twin-Study Simulator — Students set the true variance components, generate fictional twin and sibling pairs from them, and recover ACE estimates with Falconer's formulae — then switch on unequal environments, assortative mating and gene-environment correlation and watch the estimates fail in opposite directions while the truth stays put.
  • Gene × Environment Interaction Visualiser — Students name the pattern in a study that sampled only the adverse half of an environmental range, then watch the range widen and the same two groups turn a diathesis-stress finding into differential susceptibility.

If you would like to write the next one, the contributing guide explains how. Read the contributing guide on GitHub

Why this project exists

Several ideas in an undergraduate psychology degree are far easier to see than to be told. What a sampling distribution does when you change n. Why an unreliable measure caps the correlation you can observe. What a Stroop trial actually feels like. Static slides describe these things; an interactive lets a student predict, change one thing at a time, and watch what follows.

Good teaching interactives do exist, but they tend to be locked inside a publisher's platform, tied to one textbook, dependent on a login, or built on software that stops working after a few years. This project takes the opposite position. Every tool here is a plain web page: no accounts, no tracking, no server, no build step, nothing to install. Download the folder and it still works in ten years.

It is aimed at anyone teaching psychology at university level — lecturers and seminar leaders looking for something to demonstrate live, teaching assistants preparing a lab session, and students who want to work through an idea on their own.

Design principles

  • One idea per tool. A tool that tries to teach five things teaches none of them.
  • Predict before reveal. A student who has committed to an answer learns more from being wrong.
  • Accessible by default. Keyboard operable, screen-reader friendly, and never dependent on colour alone.
  • No dependencies. Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, so nothing breaks when a framework moves on.
  • Nothing leaves the browser. No accounts, no analytics, no data sent anywhere.
  • Openly licensed. Reuse, edit and re-host without asking permission.

How these are meant to be used

The tools are built for four settings. None of them requires students to create an account or install anything.

  1. Live demonstration

    Open a tool on the lecture-theatre display and change one parameter while talking. The layout stays readable when projected and when zoomed for a large room.

  2. Guided lab or seminar

    Students work through a tool on their own devices while you pose questions. Each tool carries teaching notes with prompts and the misconceptions it is designed to surface.

  3. Independent revision

    Link a tool from your VLE and students can return to it whenever they like. There is no login, so a link is all they need.

  4. Adapted for your own course

    Copy a tool's folder, change the defaults, the wording or the worked example, and host your version anywhere. The adaptation guide walks through it.

Reuse and licensing

Everything in this repository is released under the MIT licence. In practical teaching terms that means you may use these tools in your own classes, put them on your own website or VLE, change the wording, the defaults or the design, and build something new on top of them — commercially or otherwise — without asking permission and without paying anything.

The one condition is attribution: keep the copyright notice and licence text with any substantial portion you reuse. A credit line such as “Adapted from Open Psychology Interactives” with a link back is welcome but is not legally required beyond that notice.

There are no photographs, stock images, fonts or third-party libraries bundled here, so nothing carries a separate licence you would need to check before reusing it.

Privacy

These pages set no cookies, load no third-party scripts or fonts, and contain no analytics. Nothing a student does in a tool is recorded or transmitted; any responses they give stay in the browser tab and disappear when it is closed.

That also means the tools can be used offline. Download or clone the repository, open the page from your own disk, and it behaves the same way.
