# Teaching notes - ANCOVA / MANOVA Decision Laboratory (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/research-methods/tools/12-ancova-manova-decision-lab/`

**What it teaches.** A covariate mainly buys precision when groups
were formed by random allocation, and the "how were groups formed"
choice changes no number, only what the number is allowed to mean.

**Before students start.** Switching between random allocation and
intact classes changes no number on screen here, only the wording
around it.

**What students do.**
1. Set the covariate's correlation with the outcome and the size of
   the group gap on the covariate, then draw a fresh cohort.
2. Compare the unadjusted and adjusted group difference, and their
   standard errors.
3. Switch between random allocation and intact classes and see that
   nothing on screen changes except the wording.

**What to look for.** With a real gap on the covariate, adjustment
moves the estimate and narrows its standard error. Switching the "how
were groups formed" setting changes neither number, only the warrant
attached to them.

**Debrief.** If adjustment moved the estimate here, does that tell you
the groups were randomly allocated?

**Common misconception / caution.** "ANCOVA controls for pre-existing
differences" is worth catching here too: the arithmetic is identical
whether groups were randomly allocated or not, only the warrant for
reading it causally differs.

**Use in class / timing.** Drawing a cohort and comparing the two
estimates takes about 6 to 7 minutes.
