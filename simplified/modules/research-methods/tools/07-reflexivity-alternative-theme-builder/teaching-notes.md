# Reflexivity and Alternative Themes — Simplified Edition

**Module:** Research Methods · **Duration:** 5–6 minutes · **Level:** Final year or postgraduate
**Edition:** Simplified. The longer version has three questions and three
lenses, nine prepared accounts, and an accountability check in which five
claims are tested against the current reading. It is at
`modules/research-methods/tools/07-reflexivity-alternative-theme-builder/`.

## Learning objectives

Students should be able to explain how question and lens together shape an
account; describe reflexivity as treating position as part of the analysis;
judge whether a question and a lens are asking after the same kind of object;
and explain why several readings being possible is not any reading being
acceptable.

## Preparation

None. Note before you set it that the activity analyses nothing: four accounts
were written in advance and a pairing selects one. Students sometimes assume
otherwise, and the page says so twice.

## Suggested use

**After the coding laboratory**, with the same six extracts, so the shift from
coding to account is visible.

**Projected, walking the 2 by 2 in order.** Coherent, strained, coherent,
strained. The pattern is the argument.

## Prediction question

> If two teams analyse the same six interviews and produce different themes,
> has one of them done it wrong?

## Activity sequence

Four pairings of two questions with two lenses. The coherent pairings sit on
one diagonal, so changing either control alone moves to a strained one. Each
account shows its themes, where each of the six extracts sits, what the
reading illuminates and what it leaves less visible.

## Debrief questions

- In the strained pairings, what exactly is mismatched?
- Lena is central under one reading and at the margins under another. What
  changed about Lena?
- The category-and-critical account admits it rests on one atypical
  participant. Why does saying so matter?
- If any reading is possible, what stops this being arbitrary?

## The moment worth stopping on

The second strained pairing, where the account "quietly changes the question
into how it feels to be in that category". It is competent, readable and
answers something else. That is what makes incoherence hard to spot in real
work and easy to spot here.

## Likely misconceptions

**"Reflexivity means admitting bias."** It means showing what your position
did to the analysis.

**"Any reading is as good as any other."** Two of these four are visibly
strained, and not because the analyst was weak.

**"The software analysed it."** Nothing was analysed. Four accounts were
written in advance.

**"A good analyst has no lens."** Every account is from somewhere. The
alternative to declaring a lens is having an undeclared one.

## Limitations and cautions

Two questions and two lenses is a simplification; there are many more of both,
and a narrative, psychosocial or feminist reading would produce different
accounts again. The coherence ratings are a teaching judgement rather than a
formal criterion. Nothing here tells a student how to choose a lens for their
own work, which follows from their question and their commitments.

## Accessibility

Keyboard operable throughout, both choices as labelled selects. Where each
extract sits is written out in words, so nothing depends on colour or bar
length. Every account states both what it illuminates and what it obscures.
Changes are announced through a polite live region. No timed content and no
motion.

## Extension

Ask what a third lens would have to look like to make the "how is the category
produced" question coherent in a different way, and what it would push to the
margins.

## Evidence and reading

Braun and Clarke on reflexive thematic analysis and on quality criteria for
it, and any treatment of theoretical sensitivity in qualitative work.
