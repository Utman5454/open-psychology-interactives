What is this question asking for?

What the readings did

The wording does not always settle it

Two of these questions pointed clearly at one kind of answer. The third did not, and no amount of reading it more carefully would have helped.

Key idea: A research question is a claim about what would count as an answer. Read it for that first, and the kind of study follows. Where the wording leaves the kind of answer open, as in why do some students disengage from group work, the choice is not a reading problem to be solved but a commitment the researcher has to make and state. Choosing a test before making that commitment is choosing an answer before knowing the question.

The standings here are one experienced reading of wording invented for teaching, not an answer key. Options marked defensible are genuinely defensible and the note says what each would commit you to. A well-argued alternative from a supervisor is worth more than any of them. No test is named anywhere in this activity, deliberately: the reasoning that stops before the test is the part students most often skip.
