# Research Question to Method Mapper — Simplified Edition

**Module:** Research Methods · **Duration:** 5–6 minutes · **Level:** First year upwards
**Edition:** Simplified. The longer version has six questions, a design
decision, a fourth part on what you would still need to know, and a
select-all challenge. It is at
`modules/research-methods/tools/01-research-question-method-mapper/`.

## Learning objectives

Students should be able to read a question for what would count as an answer;
distinguish the six aims; explain why the aim constrains the kind of material;
recognise that several readings can be defensible and say what each commits
you to; and identify a question the wording does not settle.

## Preparation

None. No statistical test is named anywhere in the activity, deliberately.

## Suggested use

**Before any session on choosing a test.** The reasoning that stops short of
the test is the part students skip, and this is the whole activity.

**Projected, taking the room's answers on question 3.** The disagreement is
the point and it should be allowed to stand.

## Prediction question

> "Why do some students disengage from group work?" Is that a qualitative or a
> quantitative question?

Rooms split, and both halves are right, which is question 3.

## Activity sequence

1. **Belonging.** Interpretation, qualitative. Description is offered as
   defensible with a note on what it would commit you to.
2. **Mindfulness.** Difference, quantitative, with mixed offered as defensible
   if a stated qualitative strand answered a stated part of the question.
3. **Group work.** Explanation and interpretation are both strong; all three
   kinds are strong. The activity says the question is unsettled and does not
   choose.

## Debrief questions

- What would an answer to question 1 physically look like? A transcript, a
  table, a number?
- Question 2 offered "mixed" as defensible. What would the second strand have
  to do to earn the name?
- On question 3, what does the researcher have to decide that the wording
  cannot decide for them?
- Which of your answers would you defend to a supervisor, and how?

## The moment worth stopping on

Question 3. Students trained on quizzes will hunt for the intended answer and
there is not one. The commitment being asked for is not a reading skill but a
decision about what an answer would look like, and it has to be made and
stated rather than discovered in the wording.

## Likely misconceptions

**"Qualitative means small samples, quantitative means numbers."** Neither.
The question is what the analysis works on: meaning or a variable.

**"Mixed methods means collecting both."** A design earns the name when each
strand answers a stated part of one question.

**"There is a correct method for each question."** For two of these there is a
clearly better-supported reading. For the third there is not.

**"'Describe' means descriptive statistics."** In question 1 the describing is
being done by the participants.

## Limitations and cautions

The standings are one experienced reading of invented wording, not an answer
key; a supervisor's well-argued alternative is worth more. Real questions
arrive with a literature, a supervisor and a constraint set attached, none of
which is here. No test is named, so nothing here helps choose one.

## Accessibility

Keyboard operable throughout. Answered options stay in the tab order and can
be re-read. Standings are carried by border style and glyph as well as colour,
each with a visually hidden sentence. Every option has a note whether chosen
or not. No timed content and no motion.

## Extension

Ask students to bring their own project question and run the same two readings
on it, then to write the one sentence stating what they have decided an answer
would look like.

## Evidence and reading

Any methods text covers the aim taxonomy. The useful further reading is on
question formulation and on mixed-methods integration, where "each strand
answers a stated part of one question" is argued properly.
