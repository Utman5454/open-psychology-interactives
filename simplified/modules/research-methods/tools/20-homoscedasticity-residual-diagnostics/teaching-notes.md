# Teaching notes - Homoscedasticity and Residual Diagnostics (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/research-methods/tools/20-homoscedasticity-residual-diagnostics/`

**What it teaches.** Unequal spread across the range of predicted
values leaves the slope roughly where it was, but changes how much the
standard error around it can be trusted.

**Before students start.** The three groups are deliberately
unlabelled by shape, so a verdict has to come from looking, not from
reading a name off a control.

**What students do.**
1. Choose Group A, B or C and a severity level, and look at the
   scatterplot with its fitted line.
2. Press "take the line out and look again" to see the same data as
   residuals.
3. Read the comparison of ordinary and robust standard errors.

**What to look for.** One of the three groups keeps an even spread of
residuals across the fitted line; the other two fan out or bow, and it
is far easier to see which is which once the line has been taken out.

**Debrief.** Why is it easier to judge equal spread from the residual
plot than from the original scatterplot with the line still on it?

**Common misconception / caution.** "A fan means the slope is wrong"
is worth catching here too: the line barely moves between the three
groups, even though the spread around it clearly does not behave the
same way in each.

**Use in class / timing.** Comparing the three groups takes about 6 to
8 minutes.
