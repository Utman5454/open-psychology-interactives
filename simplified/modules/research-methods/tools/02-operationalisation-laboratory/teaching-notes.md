# Operationalisation Laboratory — Simplified Edition

**Module:** Research Methods
**Duration:** 5–7 minutes
**Level:** First year upwards; useful at any point before students design a study
**Edition:** Simplified. The longer version — three constructs, six measures
each, reactivity and feasibility ratings, a comparison table and a challenge on
measures that change what they measure — is at
`modules/research-methods/tools/02-operationalisation-laboratory/`.

## Learning objectives

By the end, a student should be able to:

1. explain why an operational definition is a claim about the construct
   rather than a translation of it;
2. distinguish construct under-representation from construct-irrelevant
   variance, and point to each in a specific plan;
3. predict how adding a measure changes both, and say why they move in
   opposite directions;
4. explain why coverage is the best any single measure achieves rather than
   the sum across measures;
5. recognise that combining measures buys coverage without buying the
   construct.

## Preparation

None. It runs in a browser with no account, no network and no setup, and
works from a downloaded folder.

## Suggested use

**As a five-minute opener** to a session on measurement or on construct
validity. Run it cold; students do not need the vocabulary first. The terms
land better after they have watched the trade-off happen.

**Projected, as a demonstration.** Start on the default plan — lecture
attendance records only — and ask the room what it is missing before adding
anything. Then add the self-report scale and ask what has just been bought,
and what has just been taken on.

**Before a study-design task.** Set it as preparation, then ask students to
bring the operational definition they intend to use and name one thing it
under-represents and one thing it picks up that is not the construct.

## Prediction question

Before students open it, ask:

> A department wants to know how engaged its students are, and has lecture
> attendance records. How much of "engagement" is that?

Most rooms say "not all of it" without being able to say which parts. The
coverage map answers exactly that, and the answer — one facet of five — is
starker than most expect.

## Activity sequence

1. The plan opens on **lecture attendance records only**: the commonest
   real-world proxy, and the most lopsided map. One facet well reached, four
   not reached at all, and a list of four things the measure records that are
   not engagement.
2. Students add and remove measures. Both panels update immediately: the
   coverage map on the right of the construct, the irrelevant-variance list
   beside the plan itself.
3. **What this shows** unlocks after two changes and explains the two failure
   directions and why they pull against each other.

## Debrief questions

- Attendance is "objective". Does that make it a good measure of engagement?
- Which single measure buys the most coverage? What does it cost?
- Add the depth-of-processing task to a plan that already has attendance and
  the self-report scale. Coverage does not move. What *does* move?
- No plan here scores more than 9 of 10. Which facet is out of reach, and
  what would a measure of it have to look like?
- If you could only have one measure, which would you take, and what would
  you refuse to conclude?

## The moment worth stopping on

Attendance plus the self-report scale reaches 8 of 10. Adding the
depth-of-processing task takes it to **8 of 10** — no change at all — while
adding four more sources of irrelevant variance. The task reaches the
cognitive facet well, but the scale already did, and coverage is the best any
single measure achieves rather than the sum. That is the cleanest illustration
in the activity of a measure that costs something and buys nothing, and it is
worth asking the room to explain before you do.

## Likely misconceptions

**"More measures is always better."** The case above is the counterexample,
and it is one click away.

**"Two partial measures add up to a good one."** They do not, and the model
says so: a facet is reached as well as the best single measure reaches it.
Two partial views of a facet are two partial views, not one good measure.

**"Objective measures are not contaminated."** Attendance records are as
contaminated as anything else here — by commuting distance, timetable
clashes, caring responsibilities and whether the lecture was recorded. What
they are is *unobtrusive*, which is a different virtue.

**"The coverage score is a validity coefficient."** It is arithmetic over a
facet list somebody wrote down. Say this out loud if you project the activity.

**"Fixing under-representation is just a matter of effort."** The agentic
facet stays out of reach with everything selected. Some constructs have parts
that the available measures simply do not get at.

## Limitations and cautions

- The five facets are a teaching model. Other accounts of engagement use
  three facets, or four, or argue that engagement is a property of a
  situation rather than of a student. Say so, and say it is contested.
- The coverage numbers are arithmetic over that list, out of 10. They are not
  a validity coefficient, an index of measurement quality, or comparable to
  anything published.
- The measures are described generically. No published instrument, item or
  proprietary assessment is reproduced.
- Which facets a measure reaches, and how well, is a judgement written into
  the activity to make the contrasts sharp. A student who argues that the
  self-report scale reaches the agentic facet better than "partly" is doing
  the right kind of thinking and should be credited.
- The activity says nothing about reliability, or about whether the facets
  are separable in data. Both matter and neither is here.

## Accessibility

Keyboard operable throughout. Each measure is a full-width label containing a
native checkbox, so the whole row is the target and the tick — not a colour —
is what marks it as selected; the focus ring is drawn around the row rather
than the small box. Every facet bar is paired with a word, so no level
depends on reading a bar length or distinguishing a hue. Coverage and the
number of unreached facets are announced through a polite live region on
every change. The facet definitions are given as text beneath the map. There
is no timed content and no motion.

## Extension

Ask students to design a fifth measure that would reach the agentic facet
well, then ask what it would drag in with it. Most proposals — asking
students how often they contribute, counting questions asked in seminars —
turn out to be heavily contaminated by confidence, group composition and who
is teaching. That is the point.

## Evidence and reading

Messick's account of construct validity, and the standard pairing of
construct under-representation with construct-irrelevant variance, are the
background here. Direct students to those rather than to this activity: it is
a way of seeing the trade-off, not a summary of the literature on engagement
or on validity theory.
