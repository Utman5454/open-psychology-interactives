# Teaching notes - Cohen's d and Distributional Overlap (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/research-methods/tools/15-cohens-d-overlap-explorer/`

**What it teaches.** d is a ratio of the gap between two groups to
their shared spread, and the same ratio can come from very
different-looking pairs of numbers.

**Before students start.** None needed. Both groups share one spread
control here, so there is no separate-variances case to flag.

**What students do.**
1. Guess how many of 100 people in the lower-scoring group would score
   above the higher group's average.
2. Drag the difference between the means and the shared spread within
   each group.
3. Press "show me the same d twice" to see three different-scale pairs
   that all give the same d.

**What to look for.** A difference of 8 with a spread of 10 gives
exactly the same d, and the same overlap, as a difference of 16 with a
spread of 20.

**Debrief.** What does d actually depend on: the raw difference, the
spread, or both together?

**Common misconception / caution.** "A large effect means the groups
barely overlap" still is not right: even the largest gaps this tool
can show still leave real overlap between the two groups.

**Use in class / timing.** Trying the difference and spread sliders
takes about 5 to 6 minutes.
