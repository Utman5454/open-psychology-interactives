# Teaching notes - Central Limit Theorem Simulator (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/research-methods/tools/14-central-limit-theorem-simulator/`

**What it teaches.** The distribution of sample means gets narrower
and more normal-shaped as n grows, at a rate set by the population's
own shape, and it centres on the true population mean regardless of
how skewed that population is.

**Before students start.** None needed. The tool opens on a skewed
population with nothing drawn yet.

**What students do.**
1. Draw one sample, then draw fifty, from a skewed population.
2. Change the sample size and watch the pile of means narrow and
   straighten.
3. Switch to a bimodal population and repeat.

**What to look for.** The pile of means centres on the same value
regardless of population shape, and gets both narrower and more
symmetric as sample size grows.

**Debrief.** Does the raw population panel ever change shape, no
matter what is done to the sample size?

**Common misconception / caution.** "With a big sample the data become
normal" is worth catching here too: only the pile of means changes
shape as n grows, not the population it was drawn from.

**Use in class / timing.** Drawing samples from both populations takes
about 5 to 7 minutes.
