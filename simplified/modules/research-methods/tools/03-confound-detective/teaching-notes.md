# Confound Detective — Simplified Edition

**Module:** Research Methods · **Duration:** 6–7 minutes · **Level:** First or second year
**Edition:** Simplified. The longer version has three studies, a fuller repair
set and a select-all challenge. It is at
`modules/research-methods/tools/03-confound-detective/`.

## Learning objectives

Students should be able to state the two conditions for bias; distinguish a
confound from a nuisance variable and say what each does; recognise that part
of the manipulation cannot be controlled away; predict what a repair does to
the estimate rather than to the interval; and explain why more participants
does not reduce bias.

## Preparation

None.

## Suggested use

**Projected, taking the classification from the room.** Variable 2, breakfast,
is where the useful disagreement is: most groups call it a confound.

**Before students critique a paper.** The two conditions are a checklist they
can carry into any methods section.

## Prediction question

> The Tuesday group used the app and scored 17 marks higher. What is the
> largest thing wrong with concluding the app works?

Most rooms name the tutor. Fewer name self-selection, and almost nobody says
that the sample size is irrelevant to any of it.

## Activity sequence

1. **Sort four variables.** Tutor (confound), breakfast (nuisance), the app's
   spaced-repetition schedule (part of the treatment), the shared exam paper
   (constant).
2. **Repair the design.** The study as reported claims 17.0 marks against a
   true effect of 3.0. Repairs move it: 17.0 → 8.5 → 4.5 → 3.0. Recruiting 400
   students instead of 60 moves it to 17.0, and narrows the interval from plus
   or minus 5.1 to plus or minus 2.0.

## Debrief questions

- What are the two conditions, in your own words?
- Breakfast plainly affects exam performance. Why is it not a confound here?
- Why can the spaced-repetition schedule not be controlled away?
- Apply only "recruit 400 students". What has improved, and what has not?
- Which single repair buys the most, and why?

## The moment worth stopping on

Applying the big-sample repair first, before any other. The interval halves,
the estimate does not move a thousandth, and the number being reported with
new confidence is still wrong by 14 marks. That is the clearest statement in
the activity that precision and accuracy are different things, and it is one
click.

## Likely misconceptions

**"Anything that affects the outcome is a confound."** It also has to travel
with the conditions. This is the classification students get wrong most.

**"A bigger sample fixes it."** It fixes noise. Bias is untouched.

**"Control for everything."** Controlling for part of the treatment deletes
the effect you are trying to measure.

**"A constant is a confound because it matters."** The exam paper matters for
other reasons and cannot produce a difference between groups.

## Limitations and cautions

The bias figures are invented and chosen for legibility. Real confounds
interact, point in opposite directions and sometimes cancel; additive here is
a deliberate simplification. The true effect is knowable only because the
activity invented it, which is exactly the thing a real study never has. The
activity does not cover statistical adjustment, which is a different and less
reliable route to the same goal than design.

## Accessibility

Keyboard operable throughout; each repair is a full-width label with a native
checkbox, so the whole row is the target and the tick is the non-colour state
signal. The estimate figure prints the reported value and the true effect as
text and carries a description stating both and the gap. No timed content and
no motion.

## Extension

Ask what repair would be needed if the app could only be delivered to a whole
seminar group at a time. The answer involves more seminar groups rather than
more students, which is a different design and a different unit of analysis.

## Evidence and reading

Standard treatments of internal validity and threats to it. For the bias
versus precision distinction, any account of estimation that separates
systematic error from random error will do.
