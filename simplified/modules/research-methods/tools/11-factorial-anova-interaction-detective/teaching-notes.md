# Teaching notes - Factorial ANOVA Interaction Detective (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/research-methods/tools/11-factorial-anova-interaction-detective/`

**What it teaches.** An interaction is a difference of differences
across four cell means, and a small interaction can look invisible or
dramatic depending only on how the vertical axis is scaled.

**Before students start.** Nothing to prepare. The tool opens on an
editable crossover pattern.

**What students do.**
1. Edit the four cell means directly.
2. Toggle the vertical axis between full range and zoomed, and change
   how much uncertainty is shown around each mean.

**What to look for.** An interaction of 2 points on a full 20-to-90
axis looks flat. The same numbers on a zoomed axis, or with the
uncertainty bars removed, can look far more dramatic than the data
support.

**Debrief.** How big does an interaction have to be before you would
trust it over its own uncertainty?

**Common misconception / caution.** A dramatic-looking crossing
pattern and a genuinely flat one can rest on the same small difference
of differences; only the axis changed. Judge the size of an
interaction against its uncertainty, not against how it looks at
whichever scale happens to be showing.

**Use in class / timing.** Editing the cell means and comparing axes
takes about 5 to 6 minutes.
