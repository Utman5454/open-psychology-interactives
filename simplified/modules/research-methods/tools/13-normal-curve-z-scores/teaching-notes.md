# Teaching notes - The Normal Curve and z-Scores (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/research-methods/tools/13-normal-curve-z-scores/`

**What it teaches.** A z-score relabels the same distribution in
standard-deviation units; it does not change its shape, and the same
mark can sit at very different percentiles on two distributions with
different spreads.

**Before students start.** Nothing to prepare. The curve is already on
screen when the page opens.

**What students do.**
1. Drag the mean, the standard deviation, and a mark on the curve.
2. Read the z-score, percentile and area figures, and watch the
   second axis relabel in standard-deviation units.
3. Try the built-in challenge, which cycles through three fixed
   scenarios.

**What to look for.** Narrowing the standard deviation while holding
the mean and mark fixed can move the same mark from an unremarkable
percentile to a very extreme one.

**Debrief.** The mark did not move and neither did the mean; what did,
and why did the percentile change so much?

**Common misconception / caution.** The 68/95/99.7 figures describe
the model, not any dataset in particular; treat them as a property of
the normal curve, not a fact about whatever data you happen to be
looking at.

**Use in class / timing.** Dragging the curve's controls a few times
takes about 4 to 6 minutes.
