# Teaching notes - Three Findings, Two Lines (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/42-gene-environment-interaction-visualiser/`

**What it teaches.** Two fictional groups' slopes and crossover point
never change on this page; only a movable observation window decides
whether the pattern inside it looks like diathesis-stress,
differential susceptibility, or vantage sensitivity. Starting near the
adverse end of the range makes the higher-sensitivity group look worse
off and converging toward the better end, a diathesis-stress reading
of a relationship that, sampled more widely, actually crosses.

**Before students start.** None needed.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what pattern a narrow slice of the adverse end of the range
   will show.
2. Read the default window's pattern and verdict.
3. Move the window toward the supportive end and read the new pattern
   and verdict.
4. Compare the two verdicts against a wider window that spans where
   the two groups' lines actually cross.

**What to look for.** A window placed near the adverse end reads as
diathesis-stress; the same two groups, viewed through a window near
the supportive end, read as vantage sensitivity. Both verdicts come
from the same two fixed slopes; only which part of the range the
window covers has moved.

**Debrief.** Two different-looking findings came from the same
underlying groups. What decided which finding a narrow study would
report?

**Common misconception / caution.** A study's window over the
environmental range is not a neutral choice; two studies that each
sampled a different slice of the same real relationship could report
opposite-looking findings without either one being wrong about what it
measured.

**Use in class / timing.** Comparing the two ends of the window takes
about 5 minutes.
