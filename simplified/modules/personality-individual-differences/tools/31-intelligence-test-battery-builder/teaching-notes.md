# Teaching notes - No Best Battery (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/31-intelligence-test-battery-builder/`

**What it teaches.** Within a shared sixty-minute budget, no selection
of tasks satisfies all three purposes, research, school placement and
brief screening, at once. A battery built for research, reaching all
four ability areas with a composite reliability of 0.96, meets only
one of the three requirements for a brief screen: it takes too long
and costs too much respondent burden, despite its reliability being
excellent by any ordinary standard.

**Before students start.** None needed.

**What students do.**
1. Predict whether a highly reliable battery built for one purpose
   will also work for another.
2. Press "Build the best battery for research" and read its minutes,
   burden, composite reliability and area coverage.
3. Switch the purpose to screening without changing the selection and
   see how many of its three requirements are still met.
4. Try building a battery of your own that satisfies more than one
   purpose within the budget.

**What to look for.** The guided research battery, four tasks in
forty-eight minutes, reaches all four areas and posts a composite
reliability of 0.96, meeting all three of the research purpose's
requirements. Judged against the screening purpose instead, the same
battery meets only one of its three requirements, failing on time and
on respondent burden. No selection that fits the sixty-minute budget
satisfies all three purposes at once.

**Debrief.** The battery's composite reliability was 0.96. Why did
that not make it a good screening battery?

**Common misconception / caution.** A high combined reliability is
not the same as being fit for every purpose a test might be put to; a
battery can be excellent by one purpose's standard and fail another's
outright, without any task in it being a bad measure.

**Use in class / timing.** Building the guided battery and comparing
it against a second purpose takes about 6 minutes.
