# Teaching notes - Five Explanations, All of Them Fit (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/24-explain-this-person-courtroom/`

**What it teaches.** Five explanations all fit a fictional case of
someone volunteering for a difficult task. Requesting the three
available pieces of evidence eliminates two of them outright, but the
three survivors do not survive alike: two staked themselves on every
piece requested and came through, while the broad trait account
survives having committed itself on only one, because a general
disposition is compatible with almost anything a person does on a
single occasion.

**Before students start.** The five explanations and the case are visible from the start;
nothing is asked about the person reading the page.

**What students do.**
1. Rate all five explanations before requesting any evidence.
2. Request the three available pieces of evidence and watch which
   explanations are contradicted, supported, or left untouched by
   each.
3. Try to request the fourth piece, what she does in a different
   setting over the following months, and read why it is marked
   unavailable.
4. Compare how many pieces each surviving explanation actually
   committed itself on.

**What to look for.** Two of the five explanations are contradicted by
the evidence and eliminated. Of the three that survive, two committed
themselves on every piece of evidence requested and were supported
each time; the trait account committed itself on only one piece, which
is why surviving is a much weaker achievement for it than for the
other two. The one piece of evidence that would actually separate the
three survivors, whether the behaviour generalises to a different
setting, does not exist yet.

**Debrief.** Three explanations survived the evidence. Did they
survive for the same reason?

**Common misconception / caution.** Surviving the evidence requested
is not the same as being confirmed by it; an explanation that predicts
nothing about a piece of evidence cannot be contradicted by it, and
that is a weakness of the explanation, not a strength.

**Use in class / timing.** Requesting all three available pieces of
evidence and comparing the survivors takes about 6 to 8 minutes.
