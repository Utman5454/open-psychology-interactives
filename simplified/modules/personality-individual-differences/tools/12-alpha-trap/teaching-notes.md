# Teaching notes - Chasing Alpha (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/12-alpha-trap/`

**What it teaches.** With a fixed ten-item scale, every near-duplicate
added to chase a higher alpha has to displace a question about
something else, because there is no length budget left to add to.
Alpha rises from 0.82 to 0.94 as duplicates replace content, while the
scale's correlation with a tutor's rating of dependability falls from
0.62 to 0.46 over the same run, and by the end well over a third of
the total score is variance about how one question happens to be
worded rather than about the construct.

**Before students start.** The 0.70 convention for Cronbach's alpha
should already be familiar going in.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what happens to the scale's correlation with a tutor's
   rating as alpha is pushed from 0.82 towards 0.94 by swapping in
   near-duplicate items.
2. Move the duplicate count up by hand, watching alpha, validity and
   content coverage after each swap.
3. Press "Chase alpha as far as it goes" to jump straight to the fully
   duplicated scale.
4. Open "What this shows" and read the four descriptions of the same
   questionnaire side by side.

**What to look for.** At four duplicates the scale still covers all
five content areas and alpha has already risen, but validity has
already started falling: the damage begins before any area of content
is lost. At full duplication the scale covers only one of its five
original content areas, alpha reaches 0.94, validity has fallen to
0.46, and more than a third of the total score is wording variance
rather than construct variance.

**Debrief.** A colleague reports an alpha of 0.94 for a ten-item scale
and calls it excellent. What has that number established, and what
would you need to see before agreeing with them?

**Common misconception / caution.** Alpha is not a test of
dimensionality, and this scale becomes both highly repetitive and
narrowly focused on one content area at once, two separate failures
rather than one. Every figure here is a population value implied by a
specified model, not a real estimate, so none of it carries the
sampling error a real alpha or validity coefficient would.

**Use in class / timing.** A single pass from the vote to the fully
duplicated scale takes about 5 to 7 minutes.
