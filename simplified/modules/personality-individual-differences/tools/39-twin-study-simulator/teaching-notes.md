# Teaching notes - The Same Genes, A Different Heritability (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/39-twin-study-simulator/`

**What it teaches.** Genetic variance and shared-environment variance
are held fixed throughout; only how much the environment varies
between people who are not raised together is free to move. Widening
that variation from its narrowest setting to its widest drops the
heritability estimate from 0.67 to 0.19, without a single gene or a
single shared-environment value changing at all.

**Before students start.** None needed.

**What students do.**
1. Predict whether heritability is a fixed property of a trait or
   something that can change without any gene changing.
2. Read the default heritability estimate of 0.43 with genetic
   variance 1.00 and shared variance 0.30.
3. Press the button that narrows non-shared environmental variance to
   its minimum and read the new heritability.
4. Move the slider to its widest setting and read heritability again.

**What to look for.** Narrowing non-shared environmental variance to
its minimum raises heritability to 0.67; widening it to its maximum
lowers heritability to 0.19. Genetic variance and shared-environment
variance never move on this page; heritability is a ratio, and only
its denominator is changing.

**Debrief.** Heritability moved from 0.67 to 0.19 across the two
extremes. Did the genes change?

**Common misconception / caution.** Heritability is not a fixed
amount of a trait that genes contribute; it is a ratio that depends on
how much environments actually vary in the population being studied,
and the same genetic variance can correspond to a high or a low
heritability estimate depending on that population's range of
environments.

**Use in class / timing.** Comparing the narrow and wide settings
takes about 5 minutes.
