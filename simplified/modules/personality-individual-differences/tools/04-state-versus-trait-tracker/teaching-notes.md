# Teaching notes - One Moment, or the Whole Fortnight? (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/04-state-versus-trait-tracker/`

**What it teaches.** Ada and Bo end up with almost exactly the same
average score over a fortnight of readings, 61.9 against 62.0, but
their first readings, 67 and 47, are twenty points apart from each
other. Ada's first reading sits close to her own average; Bo's sits
about fifteen points below his. Averaging more readings brings both
running means toward the same place while their spreads stay far
apart, because spread and average are separate properties of the same
series.

**Before students start.** Ada and Bo's fixed fortnight of readings is generated once,
automatically, when the page loads.

**What students do.**
1. Predict whether one reading is enough to say who has the higher
   average.
2. See the first reading for each: Ada 67, Bo 47, a twenty-point gap.
3. Move the slider from one moment up to the full fortnight and watch
   both running averages and the gap between them change, including a
   point where the gap reverses.
4. Read the two spread figures once the full fortnight is in view.

**What to look for.** By ten moments the gap has fallen to 6.8 points
and has flipped sign: Bo is now ahead. By the full fortnight both
averages sit close to 62, while the spread figures never converge,
Ada's stays near 4 and Bo's near 17, a difference visible well before
all fifty-six moments are averaged in.

**Debrief.** The first reading said Ada was higher. The full fortnight
says they are about equal. Which reading was wrong?

**Common misconception / caution.** Neither reading was wrong; a
single reading simply cannot tell an average apart from a swing away
from it. Bo's larger spread is not a flaw or a sign of a less reliable
person, it is a real difference between him and Ada that the average
alone hides.

**Use in class / timing.** Moving the slider from one moment to the
full fortnight and discussing the reversal takes about 5 minutes.
