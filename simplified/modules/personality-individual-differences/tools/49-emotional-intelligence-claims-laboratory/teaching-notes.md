# Teaching notes - It Correlates. So What? (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/49-emotional-intelligence-claims-laboratory/`

**What it teaches.** A new questionnaire's overlap with an ordinary
personality measure is set directly by a slider, and as that overlap
rises from its lowest to its highest setting, the new questionnaire's
own correlation with the outcome moves only modestly, from about 0.46
up to about 0.48 and back down to about 0.36, while its contribution
to predicting the outcome beyond personality and reasoning collapses
far more sharply, from about ten percentage points to under one. A
similar-looking outcome correlation can mean a genuinely distinct
measure or a largely redundant one, depending on how much it overlaps
with what is already being measured.

**Before students start.** The baseline contribution of personality and reasoning stays fixed
throughout; only the overlap slider changes what the new
questionnaire adds.

**What students do.**
1. Predict whether a questionnaire's correlation with an outcome is
   enough to call it distinct from personality.
2. Move the overlap slider to its lowest setting and read how many
   extra percentage points of the outcome it predicts beyond
   personality and reasoning.
3. Move the slider to its highest setting and read the new figure.
4. Compare the two increments against personality and reasoning's own
   combined contribution.

**What to look for.** Personality and reasoning together already
account for about 23% of the outcome's variance. At the lowest overlap
setting the new questionnaire adds about ten more percentage points;
at the highest overlap it adds under one. The questionnaire's own
correlation with the outcome barely moves across that same range,
staying within roughly 0.36 to 0.48; almost all of the collapse comes
from its rising overlap with personality, not from any large change in
what it correlates with the outcome.

**Debrief.** The new questionnaire's correlation with the outcome
barely changed. Why did its usefulness collapse anyway?

**Common misconception / caution.** A correlation with a real-world
outcome does not establish that a new measure is adding anything
personality does not already predict; incremental validity, what a
measure adds on top of what is already known, and raw predictive
correlation are different questions, and a measure can score well on
one and poorly on the other.

**Use in class / timing.** Comparing the low- and high-overlap
settings takes about 5 minutes.
