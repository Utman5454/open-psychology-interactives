# Teaching notes - How Much Room Does the Situation Leave? (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/03-person-situation-interaction-theatre/`

**What it teaches.** The same four fictional people are shown in two
settings, a party that rewards sociability and a handover that rewards
care, and a single slider turns up how strongly each setting pushes
everyone toward its own demand. As the slider rises, the four people's
behaviour converges toward whatever the setting rewards; nothing about
the people underneath changes.

**Before students start.** Both settings and the strength slider are on screen as soon as the
page loads; nothing needs to be prepared.

**What students do.**
1. Predict whether the most sociable person will also do best at the
   quieter handover.
2. Read the two starting orderings and notice which two people swap
   ends between them.
3. Raise the slider, or press the button that jumps both settings to
   their strongest, and watch the gap between the four people shrink.

**What to look for.** At the default setting the party and the
handover put people in almost opposite orders: the most sociable
person, who leads at the party, finishes last at the handover, and the
reverse for the most caring person. The two people in between keep the
same relative order in both. Turning the slider up to its strongest
setting shrinks the gap between all four to around a point, at which
point comparing their order stops being meaningful.

**Debrief.** Why do the party and the handover put the same four
people in almost reversed orders? What would it take to know how
sociable or caring someone "really" is from a single setting like
either of these?

**Common misconception / caution.** A setting that pushes everyone
toward the same behaviour is not evidence that the underlying
differences between people have disappeared, only that this
particular setting is currently doing most of the work. A different
setting, rewarding something else, could reorder the same four people
again.

**Use in class / timing.** A single pass through both settings and the
slider takes about 5 minutes.
