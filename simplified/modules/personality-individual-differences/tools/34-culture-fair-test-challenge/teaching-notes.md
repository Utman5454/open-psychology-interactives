# Teaching notes - Take the Words Out and See What Is Left (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/34-culture-fair-test-challenge/`

**What it teaches.** Starting from a design with written instructions,
verbal items, strict timing and paper response, all six tracked
demands sit at or above the level the tool treats as substantial.
Stripping the words out, shown instructions and figural items instead
of verbal ones, drops reading demand nearly to zero, but two other
demands, a puzzle-format demand and a test-taking-convention demand,
actually rise as a result.

**Before students start.** None needed.

**What students do.**
1. Predict how many of the six demands a heavily verbal design carries
   at a substantial level.
2. Read the opening design's six demand levels.
3. Switch to shown instructions and figural items and watch which
   demands fall and which rise.
4. Try to find a design that brings every demand below the substantial
   threshold.

**What to look for.** The opening design carries all six demands at a
substantial level, not just most of them. Moving to the wordless
design drops reading demand from 1.00 to about 0.10, but the
puzzle-format and test-taking-convention demands both rise, from 0.40
to 0.85 and from 0.65 to 0.85. No combination of the available design
choices brings every demand below the substantial threshold at once.

**Debrief.** Taking the words out of a test lowered one demand sharply
and raised two others. Does that make the wordless version fairer
overall?

**Common misconception / caution.** A test with no verbal content is
not thereby free of construct-irrelevant demands; removing one demand
can raise another, and sitting a formal test at all carries some
demand, in the model here represented as a floor that no design choice
removes entirely.

**Use in class / timing.** Comparing the opening and wordless designs
takes about 5 to 7 minutes.
