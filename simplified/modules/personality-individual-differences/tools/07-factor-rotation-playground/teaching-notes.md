# Teaching notes - Turn the Axes, Change Every Number (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/07-factor-rotation-playground/`

**What it teaches.** Rotating one pair of axes through ninety degrees
changes every marker's loading on both factors, yet the total variance
the two factors capture between them never moves. The activity uses a
genuine varimax-style simplicity score, and turning the dial to where
that score peaks brings ten of twelve markers cleanly onto one factor
or the other, while two markers refuse to cooperate at any angle.

**Before students start.** The twelve markers' fixed positions are visible as soon as the page
opens; only the rotation angle moves.

**What students do.**
1. Predict whether rotating the axes can raise the total variance
   captured.
2. Turn the dial and watch each marker's two loadings change while the
   total stays fixed.
3. Find, or reveal, the angle near 47 degrees where the simplicity
   score peaks.
4. Identify the one marker that loads on both factors at every angle
   and the one that loads on neither.

**What to look for.** Rotating to 47 degrees brings ten of the twelve
markers to a clear loading on one factor and a small one on the
other. "Enthusiastic" keeps a substantial loading on both factors at
every angle tried; "unusual" never reaches a substantial loading on
either, because its total communality is too low to clear the
threshold on any axis. Factor 1's own variance rises from about 2.62
to about 2.90 across the search, while the two factors' combined total
stays exactly where it started.

**Debrief.** The dial found an angle where most markers look simple.
Did that angle improve how well the two factors describe the twelve
markers, or only how easy the pattern is to read?

**Common misconception / caution.** A marker that never loads clearly
on either factor is not a mistake in the data; it genuinely shares
little in common with either cluster, and no rotation will manufacture
a loading it does not have.

**Use in class / timing.** Finding the peak angle and identifying the
two awkward markers takes about 5 to 7 minutes.
