# Teaching notes - Four Ways to Answer the Same Questionnaire (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/14-response-style-simulator/`

**What it teaches.** Four fictional respondents share the same
reactions to twenty items, expressed four different ways: answering
straightforwardly, agreeing with everything, using only the ends of
the scale, or staying near the middle. Reverse-worded items rescue the
respondent who agrees with everything, bringing them right back in
line with the straightforward respondent; they do nothing at all for
someone who prefers the ends or the middle, because those two styles
multiply the distance from the midpoint rather than adding a constant
to it.

**Before students start.** None needed.

**What students do.**
1. Predict which of the three non-straightforward styles reverse
   wording will fix.
2. Read the four respondents' scores on the balanced questionnaire and
   see how close together three of them sit.
3. Press "Show the unbalanced questionnaire" and watch only the
   agreeable respondent's score move.
4. Read the explanation of why an additive shift cancels under reverse
   wording and a multiplicative one does not.

**What to look for.** On the balanced questionnaire the respondent who
agrees with everything scores indistinguishably from the
straightforward one. Switch to the unbalanced questionnaire and that
respondent's score jumps toward the top of the scale, while the two
respondents whose styles multiply the distance from the midpoint
barely move at all, on either questionnaire.

**Debrief.** Reverse wording fixed one of the three problem styles
completely and did nothing for the other two. Why does the same fix
work for one style and not the others?

**Common misconception / caution.** Reverse-worded items are not a
general defence against inattentive or unusual responding; they
specifically counter a constant pull toward agreement, and a style
that stretches or shrinks answers around the midpoint passes straight
through the same recoding unaffected.

**Use in class / timing.** A single pass through the balanced and
unbalanced views takes about 5 minutes.
