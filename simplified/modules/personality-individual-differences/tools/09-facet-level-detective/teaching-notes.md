# Teaching notes - Same Score, Different People (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/personality-individual-differences/tools/09-facet-level-detective/`

**What it teaches.** Sam and Priya both score 66 on conscientiousness,
an average of 84/48 and 50/82 on its two facets. The domain-based
prediction is 66 for both of them on every one of four behavioural
observations; the facet-based prediction separates them by more than
twenty points each time and gets the pattern right. A domain score is
not wrong here, it simply cannot see what an average discards.

**Before students start.** None needed.

**What students do.**
1. Predict why two people scoring the same on a broad trait behave
   differently over several weeks.
2. Reveal the facet scores and read the prediction table's domain
   column, which reads 66 four times.
3. Read the facet column beside it, which separates Sam and Priya on
   every observation.
4. Drag the item mix, or press the guided button for an all-tidiness
   questionnaire, and watch which of the two now scores higher.

**What to look for.** The domain column cannot change no matter which
behaviour is being predicted, because it never sees the facet scores
that produced it. Moving the item mix toward one facet or the other
moves Sam and Priya's domain scores in opposite directions and swaps
who is ahead, while neither person's underlying facet scores move at
all.

**Debrief.** What does a conscientiousness score of 66 actually tell
you about somebody? If facets predict behaviour better, why does
anyone still report a single domain score?

**Common misconception / caution.** Neither Sam nor Priya is "really"
more conscientious; which one comes out ahead depends on a measurement
choice, not a fact about them. The clean crossover here is a property
of how the two facets were constructed to trade off; a real pair of
facets rarely mirrors each other this neatly.

**Use in class / timing.** A single pass from prediction to the
item-mix stage takes about 5 minutes.
