# Teaching notes - Recovery and Plasticity Simulator (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/neuropsychology/tools/12-recovery-plasticity-simulator/`

**What it teaches.** A large rise on a trained task can be built almost
entirely from components that never reach an untested task. The guided
case climbs from 20 to about 80, yet only around seven per cent of
that climb shows up on the untrained task, because restitution, the
one component both curves share, was kept deliberately small.

**Before students start.** None needed, though the caution revealed at
the end is easy to skip: make a point of reading it aloud rather than
letting the demonstration end without it.

**What students do.**
1. Move four sliders, restitution, relearning, compensation, and
   practice effect, and watch the trained and untrained curves and the
   share that generalises.
2. Press "Show me the case that looks best and means least" to see a
   large rise built mostly from components the untrained task never
   reflects.
3. Once at least two changes have been tried, reveal the caution on
   what the four bands can and cannot tell you.

**What to look for.** The guided case's trained score climbs from 20
to about 80, and only about seven per cent of that climb shows up on
the untrained task, since restitution was kept small on purpose while
the other three components did the work.

**Debrief.** What would the guided case's climb look like, reported as
"improvement," if the untrained task had never been checked at all?

**Common misconception / caution.** The four bands are drawn as if
they simply add up with no interaction between them, and their shapes
are illustrative rather than measured. Nothing here describes a real
patient or predicts a real recovery.

**Use in class / timing.** The sliders and the guided case take about
5 to 7 minutes to try.
