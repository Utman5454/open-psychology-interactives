# Teaching notes - Aphasia Profile Comparator (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/neuropsychology/tools/07-aphasia-profile-comparator/`

**What it teaches.** The measure that separates two profiles most is
not always the one where either profile scores worst. Sometimes both
people are equally poor on a measure, which makes it useless for
telling them apart, and a different measure that neither profile's own
low score pointed to turns out to do the separating.

**Before students start.** No preparation is required. Each pairing
asks for a prediction before the chart appears.

**What students do.**
1. For each of three paired profiles, predict which of four measures,
   fluency, comprehension, repetition or naming, will separate them
   most.
2. Reveal the chart to check, then move to the next pairing.

**What to look for.** The first two pairings are the easy case: the
separating measure is also the profile's own worst score. The third is
not: both profiles are equally poor at repetition, so repetition
settles nothing between them, and naming, a score neither would flag
by eye, is what actually separates them.

**Debrief.** Ask why the third pairing's biggest gap fell on a measure
that neither profile's own low score had pointed to.

**Common misconception / caution.** A person's own worst score is not
automatically the measure that best separates them from someone else;
it only is once the comparison person's score on that same measure is
checked too.

**Use in class / timing.** All three pairings fit into about 5
minutes.
