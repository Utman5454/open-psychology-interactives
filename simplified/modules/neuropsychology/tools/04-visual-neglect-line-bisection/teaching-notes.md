# Teaching notes - Visual Neglect Laboratory (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/neuropsychology/tools/04-visual-neglect-line-bisection/`

**What it teaches.** Bisection alone does not separate neglect from
field loss: both fictional patients here mark to the right of centre.
Cancellation does. The neglect patient leaves most of the left half of
the sheet untouched. The field-loss patient finds nearly all of it,
because he moves his eyes to search for what he knows is missing.

**Before students start.** No preparation is required. Comparison,
Patient A and Patient B are all available from the start.

**What students do.**
1. Choose who is doing the task and which task, bisection or
   cancellation, and read the result.
2. Press "Put the two patients side by side" to compare their
   cancellation sheets directly.
3. Once at least two things have been tried, reveal what the
   comparison shows.

**What to look for.** On bisection, both patients mark to the right,
so the task alone cannot tell the two conditions apart. On
cancellation, the neglect patient misses most of the left half while
the field-loss patient finds nearly all of it.

**Debrief.** Why does bisection fail to separate these two patients
while cancellation succeeds?

**Common misconception / caution.** "Neglect is blindness on one
side" has it backwards: the field-loss patient cannot see the left
without moving his eyes, and moves them, while the neglect patient's
visual input from the left is available but goes unattended and
unacted on. Patient A's right-hemisphere stroke is the typical site
for neglect, not the only one it can follow.

**Use in class / timing.** Five minutes is enough to compare the two
patients on both tasks.
