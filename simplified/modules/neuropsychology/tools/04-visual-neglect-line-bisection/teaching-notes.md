# Neglect or Blindness? — Simplified Edition

**Module:** Neuropsychology
**Duration:** 5–7 minutes
**Level:** Second year and above
**Edition:** Simplified. This keeps the generated performance drawings from
**Visual Neglect Laboratory**. Two further profiles, scene copying and the
viewer-centred against object-centred distinction are in the longer version at
`modules/neuropsychology/tools/04-visual-neglect-line-bisection/`.

## Learning objectives

By the end, a student should be able to:

1. tell left neglect from left hemianopia by the pattern of performance;
2. say why cancellation separates them and bisection does not;
3. say why field loss can be compensated for and neglect cannot;
4. describe neglect as a disorder of attending rather than of seeing;
5. say why no single task diagnoses either.

## Preparation

None. Point out before starting that the first profile is a control and is
there so the two patients have something to be compared with.

## Suggested use

**Projected, in a fixed order.** Bisection first, all three profiles: both
patients deviate right, so the room learns that this task does not separate
them. Then cancellation, and the difference is unmissable.

**In a session on spatial attention**, as the demonstration that attention and
vision come apart.

**Before a clinical placement discussion**, because the practical consequence
matters: a patient who denies any difficulty and misses food on one side of
the plate is not the same as one who turns their head to find it.

## Prediction question

After showing bisection for both patients:

> Both marks are to the right of centre. Which of these two people cannot see
> the left-hand side?

The room cannot tell, and that is the point. Then switch task.

## Activity sequence

1. **Comparison profile, line bisection.** Establish the baseline.
2. **Both patients on bisection.** Both deviate right. No separation.
3. **Switch to cancellation, comparison first.** All 30 found.
4. **Patient A.** Four of fifteen on the left. Head and eyes were free.
5. **Patient B.** Thirteen of fifteen, and it took longer.
6. **Press "Put the two patients side by side"** and read the note.
7. **Read the caution**, which is where the real messiness lives.

## Debrief questions

- Both patients miss things on the left. What is different about why?
- Why does turning your head help one of them and not the other?
- What would you expect each of them to say if you asked whether anything was
  wrong?
- Why is bisection still used, if it does not separate the two?
- A patient has both. What would their sheet look like?

## Likely misconceptions

- **Neglect is blindness on one side.** The whole activity is against this.
- **Neglect is a problem with the eyes.** The visual pathways are intact.
- **A rightward bisection error means neglect.** Both patients here produce
  one.
- **Cancellation diagnoses neglect.** It separates these two clean profiles.
  Real patients often have both conditions, and the caution says so.
- **Neglect means the left half of the world.** It can be the left of an
  object as well, which is where the longer version goes.

## Limitations and cautions

Three fictional profiles drawn to be clean. Real patients are much less tidy:
neglect and field loss frequently follow the same stroke, bisection deviation
varies with line length and page position, and cancellation depends on how
cluttered the array is and how the patient is instructed. Some people with
field loss compensate poorly and look like neglect on one sheet. No single task
diagnoses either condition, and nothing here is an assessment instrument.

## Accessibility considerations

Profile and task are chosen by labelled radio groups, not by clicking the
drawing. Roles are explicit everywhere: the comparison is named as a control
and each patient as a case, in the controls, the figure title, the readout and
the description. A found star is drawn pale and crossed out; one left untouched
is solid and dark, so the distinction is fill plus an added mark rather than
colour. The description gives the deviation in millimetres or the two half
counts and names whose sheet it is. In comparison mode the tiles report both
patients rather than a stale selection.

## Optional extension

Ask what a task would have to look like to detect neglect *within* an object
rather than within the page. Students usually arrive at drawing or reading a
single word, which is exactly where object-centred neglect is demonstrated.

## Evidence and citation notes

For the dissociation see Halligan, Fink, Marshall and Vallar (2003), *Spatial
cognition: Evidence from visual neglect*, **Trends in Cognitive Sciences**,
7(3), 125–133. On why cancellation is more sensitive than bisection see Ferber
and Karnath (2001), *How to assess spatial neglect: Line bisection or
cancellation tasks?*, **Journal of Clinical and Experimental
Neuropsychology**, 23(5), 599–607. On the co-occurrence of neglect and
hemianopia see Karnath, Rennig, Johannsen and Rorden (2011), *The anatomy
underlying acute versus chronic spatial neglect*, **Brain**, 134(3), 903–912.
