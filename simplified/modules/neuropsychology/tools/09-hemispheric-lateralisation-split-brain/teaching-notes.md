# Teaching notes - Split-Brain Laboratory (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/neuropsychology/tools/09-hemispheric-lateralisation-split-brain/`

**What it teaches.** The classic split-brain result depends on brief
exposure as much as on cutting the corpus callosum. Lengthen exposure
alone, leaving the callosum cut, and the same trial that failed now
succeeds, because the eyes have time to move and both hemispheres end
up with the word.

**Before students start.** Nothing to prepare. The diagram updates
live as each control changes, with no run button and no prediction
step.

**What students do.**
1. Set which side the word appears on, how the answer is given, and
   whether the callosum is intact or cut, and read whether the
   response works.
2. Press "Show me the classic trial" for the standard cut, brief,
   spoken-response setup.
3. Press "Now break the condition it depends on" to lengthen exposure
   alone and watch the same trial succeed.

**What to look for.** Lengthening exposure flips a failing trial to a
successful one without touching the callosum at all. Eye movement, not
the section, becomes the deciding factor once exposure stops being
brief.

**Debrief.** Ask what a longer exposure actually restores, given that
the callosum stays cut throughout.

**Common misconception / caution.** Nothing here supports a "two
minds in one head" reading. Brief exposure is a necessary condition for
the classic result, not a lab technicality, and more recent work has
questioned even the sharpness of the perceptual split these patients
show outside a laboratory.

**Use in class / timing.** The controls and both guided buttons take
about 5 minutes to try.
