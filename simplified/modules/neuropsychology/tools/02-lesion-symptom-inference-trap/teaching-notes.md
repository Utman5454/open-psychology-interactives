# How Sure Can One Case Make You? — Simplified Edition

**Module:** Neuropsychology
**Duration:** 5–7 minutes
**Level:** Second year and above, once lesion methods have been introduced
**Edition:** Simplified. This keeps the sequential reveal and the confidence
trace from **Lesion-Symptom Inference Trap**. Three further complications, the
claim-sorting exercise and the full rating trace are in the longer version at
`modules/neuropsychology/tools/02-lesion-symptom-inference-trap/`.

## Learning objectives

By the end, a student should be able to:

1. say that lesion evidence speaks to necessity, not to what tissue does;
2. name three distinct ways a region-to-symptom inference fails;
3. separate a claim about one person from a claim about a region;
4. say why vascular co-occurrence does not average away with sample size;
5. separate damaged tissue from tissue that is swollen or temporarily quiet.

## Preparation

None. Insist that students rate before reading each complication; rating
afterwards destroys the demonstration.

## Suggested use

**Projected, with the room voting on each rating** before it is entered. The
public commitment is what makes the falling ceiling uncomfortable.

**Immediately before a lecture on lesion-symptom mapping**, so that voxel-based
methods arrive as a response to a problem the room has already felt.

## Prediction question

Ask before the first rating:

> A clear lesion, a clear deficit, and a clean scan report. How confident
> would a neuropsychologist be that this region does this job?

Most students say 70 or 80. The most this evidence carries is nearer 25, and
the gap is the lesson.

## Activity sequence

1. **Read the case and the claim.** Note that the claim says *is where it is
   carried out*, which is a claim about function, not about necessity.
2. **Rate, then read complication 1.** Extent: the label names a centre.
3. **Rate again, then complication 2.** Territory: strokes take the shape of
   the vessel.
4. **Rate again, then complication 3.** Swelling and diaschisis: some affected
   tissue is not damaged.
5. **Final rating**, then read the trace and the caution about the ceiling.

## Debrief questions

- What could this case establish, if not what the region does?
- Why does the vascular problem get worse rather than better as you collect
  more cases?
- If some of the deficit is swelling, what would you expect at six months?
- The ceiling is drawn as a number. Should it be?
- What would you need in order to say something about the *region* rather
  than about Mrs D?

## Likely misconceptions

- **A clear lesion gives a clear localisation.** The whole activity is against
  this.
- **More cases fix the problem.** They fix sampling noise; they do not fix
  systematic vascular co-occurrence, which is the point of complication 2.
- **The deficit maps onto the damaged tissue.** Swelling and diaschisis mean
  part of it maps onto tissue that is intact.
- **Necessity and function are the same claim.** They are not, and the
  takeaway separates them explicitly.
- **The ceiling is calculated.** It is a device, and the caution says so
  plainly so that nobody quotes it.

## Limitations and cautions

The ceiling is illustrative. There is no formula that converts complications
into a number, and a different neuropsychologist would draw it elsewhere; what
is defensible is the direction, since every complication here removes support.
The case is fictional and the scan findings invented. Nothing here is a
diagnosis, and no real patient is described.

## Accessibility considerations

The rating is a native range input reporting its value in text. The chart
description gives the ceiling at every stage, every rating made, and what the
shaded band means, so a screen-reader user has the whole argument without the
lines. The learner's line and the ceiling differ by dash pattern and end label
as well as colour. Each complication is announced politely. Nothing is timed.

## Optional extension

Ask students to write the sentence about Mrs D that the evidence *does*
support. It is much duller than the claim on screen, and noticing that is the
point.
