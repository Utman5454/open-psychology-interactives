# Teaching notes - Lesion-Symptom Inference Trap (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/neuropsychology/tools/02-lesion-symptom-inference-trap/`

**What it teaches.** A single case can support a narrow claim, that
some damaged tissue was necessary for this person's speech, on this
occasion. It cannot support the broader claim that a region is where a
function is carried out. Students rate their confidence in that
broader claim about Mrs D, then watch the most the evidence can carry
fall as each complication is revealed, while nothing about her actual
deficit changes.

**Before students start.** None needed. The case and the claim are on
screen from the start.

**What students do.**
1. Rate confidence that the left inferior frontal region is where
   fluent speech production is carried out.
2. Lock in a rating, then read each of three complications in turn, a
   larger lesion than the label suggests, a shared blood supply rather
   than a shared function, and swollen or quietened tissue nearby that
   is not actually damaged, re-rating after each.
3. Read the summary of how often the rating stayed above the falling
   line.

**What to look for.** The line marking what the evidence can carry
falls at every complication, while Mrs D's scan and her speech never
change. Most students stay above that line the whole way through,
because nothing in the three complications ever raises support back
up, only lowers it.

**Debrief.** What does the case still support once all three
complications are in, and what would have to be true of the evidence
for the broader claim to survive them?

**Common misconception / caution.** "Damage here, so the region does
this" turns necessity into function without justification. The falling
line is a teaching device, not a calculation, and a different
neuropsychologist could draw it elsewhere; what is not arbitrary is the
direction, since every complication here removes support and none adds
any.

**Use in class / timing.** Five minutes covers rating confidence and
reading through all three complications.
