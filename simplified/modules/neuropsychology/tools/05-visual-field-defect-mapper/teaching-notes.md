# Teaching notes - Visual Field Defect Mapper (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/neuropsychology/tools/05-visual-field-defect-mapper/`

**What it teaches.** Most sites along the visual pathway remove the
same side of the field from both eyes, or remove one eye entirely. The
optic chiasm is the only site that removes a different side from each
eye, because that is exactly where the fibres carrying each eye's outer
field cross. A cut at the optic tract and a cut at the visual cortex
give the identical pattern, so the shape of the loss narrows the site
without pinning it down.

**Before students start.** Nothing needs setting up. Selecting a site
shows the result immediately.

**What students do.**
1. Choose a damage site along the pathway and read the loss in each
   eye.
2. Press "Show me the one that looks different" to see the chiasm's
   pattern set against every other site.
3. Once at least two sites have been tried, reveal what the shapes
   show.

**What to look for.** The chiasm produces a loss of the outer field in
both eyes, the one pattern here that is not the same side in both. The
optic tract and the visual cortex sites produce an identical pattern
to each other.

**Debrief.** Ask why the chiasm's pattern looks so different from
every other site's, given that the crossing happens only there.

**Common misconception / caution.** A field pattern narrows the
possible site; it does not name a diagnosis. Some people are unaware
of a substantial field loss, so the absence of a complaint is not
evidence that the field is intact.

**Use in class / timing.** The six sites take about 5 minutes to work
through.
