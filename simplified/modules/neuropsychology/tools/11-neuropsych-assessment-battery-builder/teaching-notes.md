# Teaching notes - Assessment Battery Builder (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/neuropsychology/tools/11-neuropsych-assessment-battery-builder/`

**What it teaches.** Leaving out sleep, illness and mood measures does
not just narrow a report, it leaves the whole alternative explanation
for a picture of new forgetfulness untested. An eighty-minute budget
that refuses any tick pushing past it forces that trade-off to be made
on purpose rather than by running out of time.

**Before students start.** Read the referral aloud in full, including
the sentences about poor sleep and a recent chest infection: they
matter to the answer.

**What students do.**
1. Read the referral for a fictional man whose family describes new
   forgetfulness.
2. Tick tasks within the eighty-minute budget, which refuses any tick
   that would push the total over.
3. Write the report and read what the chosen battery can and cannot
   speak to.

**What to look for.** Two batteries covering the same number of
questions can read very differently in the final report, depending on
whether sleep, illness and mood happened to be among the tasks chosen.

**Debrief.** Ask whether any battery buildable here could actually
settle whether this is the beginning of a dementia.

**Common misconception / caution.** No combination of tasks on this
list would be sufficient to diagnose or exclude a dementia. That is a
clinical judgement made over time, with information this exercise
deliberately leaves out.

**Use in class / timing.** One battery, built against the budget,
takes about 5 to 7 minutes.
