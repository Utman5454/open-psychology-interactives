What Each Profile Can Decide

The useful question about a case is not what it shows but which claims it is able to speak to at all.

The evidence

Three profiles

All three are fictional. Each was tested on the same two things: learning new facts, and learning a new manual skill over several days.

Two claims

For each claim, decide what each profile does to it. There are three answers and the third one matters most.

What this shows

Two impairments together decide nothing

Key idea: A profile speaks to a claim when it shows one thing preserved while another is lost, because that is what a claim about separate systems predicts and a claim about one shared system forbids. A profile in which everything is impaired is compatible with both, so it decides nothing, however severe it is and however carefully it was measured. That is why the third answer is the important one: recognising that a case cannot settle a question is a finding about the evidence, not a failure to reach a conclusion. And a single dissociation always leaves the alternative that the lost task was simply harder, which is why two cases running in opposite directions are worth so much more than one case, however dramatic.

Three fictional profiles, described in a sentence each. Real cases are never this clean: impairments are partial, tasks differ in difficulty in ways that are hard to equate, and a person who looks intact on one measure may be impaired on a more sensitive one. Absence of evidence in a single testing session is weak evidence of absence. Nothing here names a syndrome, and no real patient is described.
