# Teaching notes - Attentional Blink (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/cognitive/tools/05-attentional-blink/`

**What it teaches.** Reporting a first target in a fast stream costs
accuracy on a second target that follows soon after, and that cost dips
sharply a couple of items later before recovering, rather than simply
building or staying flat.

**Before students start.** The stream cannot be slowed down without
destroying the effect it demonstrates, which is why a worked example is
offered from the start for anyone who would rather watch it than run it
live.

**What students do.**
1. Run four slowed practice streams with the answers shown, or see a
   worked example instead.
2. Run the scored block: say odd or even for the digit, then say whether
   an X appeared.
3. Read the lag-accuracy chart.

**What to look for.** Accuracy for the second target is high at the
shortest gap, drops heavily two or three items later, and climbs back up
by the longest gap tested. Point at the dip, not just the drop.

**Debrief.** Why would accuracy dip in the middle of the range rather
than fall steadily as the gap shrinks? What would it mean if your
shortest-gap and longest-gap answers came out the same?

**Common misconception / caution.** "It's just hard to do two things at
once" misses the specific timing: a plain dual-task story does not
predict recovery by the longest gap. Five trials per gap is a feel for
the task, not a measurement.

**Use in class / timing.** Six to eight minutes covers the whole thing,
practice included.
