# Teaching notes - Posner Spatial Cueing (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/cognitive/tools/01-posner-spatial-cueing/`

**What it teaches.** A cue students are told to ignore still shifts how
fast they respond: valid cueing speeds them up and invalid cueing slows
them down, both measured against an uninformative neutral baseline.

**Before students start.** This measures reaction time, not attention
as a trait, and the cue is genuinely uninformative.

**What students do.**
1. Run six practice trials with feedback.
2. Run the 72-trial block straight through, with no feedback until it
   ends.
3. Read the benefit and cost the tool draws out from your own neutral
   mean.

**What to look for.** Twelve of the seventy-two trials are invalid, the
fewest of any condition, so that mean is built from the least data in the
block. A cost bigger or smaller than the benefit is normal; a negative
benefit from one person is not evidence against anything.

**Debrief.** Which of your three means came from the fewest trials, and
does that change how much you trust it? If cueing is genuinely
uninformative, why does it change anything at all?

**Common misconception / caution.** Students often take a shift in
reaction time as proof that attention moved without their eyes moving.
There is no eye-tracking here, so an unnoticed glance toward the cue
would produce exactly the same result.

**Use in class / timing.** The whole activity, practice included, runs
in about 5 to 7 minutes.
