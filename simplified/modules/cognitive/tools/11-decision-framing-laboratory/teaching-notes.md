# Teaching notes - Decision Framing Laboratory (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/cognitive/tools/11-decision-framing-laboratory/`

**What it teaches.** Describing identical arithmetic as something
"saved" versus something "lost" changes which option people prefer,
even though the numbers underneath are exactly the same.

**Before students start.** This page has no built-in prediction
question, so put it to the class yourself before the four decisions
begin.

**What students do.**
1. Make four decisions, two problems, each seen once under each frame.
2. Read the "what you did" reveal, which lines the two pairs up side by
   side and says whether you switched.

**What to look for.** Watch for a student who chose the safe option
under "saved" wording but the risky option under "lost" wording, for the
same underlying numbers. That switch is the whole demonstration.

**Debrief.** If you switched between the two framings of the same
problem, what actually changed about the numbers? Nothing. What changed
was the description. Is a preference that depends on wording a mistake?

**Common misconception / caution.** "This proves people are irrational"
claims too much here as well: it is a group-level pattern, not a fault
in any one person. Two decisions per problem, seen by one student, show
at most whether that student switched, not how common switching is.

**Use in class / timing.** Four decisions and the reveal take about 4
to 6 minutes.
