# Teaching notes - Inattentional Blindness (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/cognitive/tools/03-inattentional-blindness/`

**What it teaches.** A fully visible, unmoving object can go completely
unreported when attention is absorbed by a demanding counting task. That
says something about what counts as "seen," not that anybody involved is
inattentive.

**Before students start.** Say nothing about anything beyond counting
triangles. Since the opening prediction is not built into this page, ask
it yourself, out loud, before the display runs.

**What students do.**
1. Count the solid, downward-pointing triangles in a static display.
2. Enter their count.
3. Answer yes or no: was anything else on the display?
4. Compare against the replay, which rings the counted shapes and the
   unexpected object separately.

**What to look for.** The yes-or-no question gives no room for "I think
something was there." Watch for students who answer no with confidence
and then recognise the shape instantly once it is ringed in the replay.

**Debrief.** Do you think you actually saw it, even though you said no?
What would tell the difference between not seeing something and not
reporting it?

**Common misconception / caution.** "I'm bad at attention" is the line to
head off: one trial with one yes-or-no answer proves nothing about
anybody. A third run without changing the target would only teach
students what to look for, not test their attention again.

**Use in class / timing.** This runs quickly: about 4 to 5 minutes from
start to finish.
