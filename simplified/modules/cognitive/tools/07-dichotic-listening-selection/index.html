The task

Watch the left column. Ignore the right one.

Two words will appear at a time, one on each side, about one pair a second. Your job concerns the left column only: press the button, or the space bar, every time the left word is a colour. Ignore the right column entirely. There is nothing you need to do with it.

What this needs. Being able to see two columns at once, and about a minute. Nothing flashes and nothing moves; the words simply change about once a second. If reading two columns at once is not something you can do, use Skip the task: the finding and the explanation are the same, and nothing in them depends on having performed it.

Now, two questions about the column you were ignoring. Answer honestly rather than carefully; a guess you are unsure of is exactly as informative.

Did anything about the right-hand column change part-way through?

Did the right-hand column change?

The right-hand words were all examples of one kind of thing. Which?

What were the right-hand words about?

What got through

Two different questions about the same ignored words

    Key idea: Whether something gets through depends on what you are asking of it. A change in how the words looked is a physical property, available without the words meaning anything, and people usually catch it. What the words were about requires each one to have been recognised as a word and then sorted into a category, and that far more often does not survive. This is the finding the whole argument about selection was built around. An early filter that blocks the ignored channel outright explains why the meaning is lost but not why the physical change is caught. A filter that turns the ignored channel down rather than off, or one that lets everything through and selects at the point of response, handles both. The evidence constrains where selection happens; it does not settle it.

    Three cautions, and the first is the largest. This is a visual analogue of a listening task. The original experiments played different speech into each ear, where the two streams genuinely cannot be sampled at once; two columns of text can be, however hard you try not to, so this understates how completely an ignored channel is lost. Second, one trial from one person proves nothing: failing to report something is weak evidence that it was not processed at all, and the whole difficulty of this literature is that not reporting and not processing look identical from outside. Third, notice that every version of the theory can be made to fit these two results. A theory that absorbs every finding is not thereby supported by them.

    The longer version adds a harder attended task so the pattern can be made to shift, a naive first trial, simulated class data and a comparison of what each theory predicts in each case. It is at Dichotic Listening and Selection Theories in the main collection.
