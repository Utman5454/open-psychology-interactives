# Power Lens Laboratory — Simplified Edition

**Module:** Social and Critical Psychology
**Duration:** 5–7 minutes
**Level:** Second year and above, or first year with a prior session on power
**Edition:** Simplified. The longer version, with evidence sorting against a
three-level key, a full comparison table and a transfer case, is at
`modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/10-power-lens-lukes-foucault-fricker/`.

## Learning objectives

By the end, a student should be able to:

1. apply three accounts of power to one unchanged body of evidence, and say
   what each brings into focus;
2. state what each account leaves in shadow, and why that is a cost of the
   account rather than an oversight by the analyst;
3. recognise that the readings overlap — an item can be core to one lens and
   defensible under another;
4. explain why each lens implies a different intervention, and why no single
   intervention would have stopped the other mechanisms;
5. distinguish applying a lens from establishing a fact about a case.

## Preparation

None. The activity runs in a browser with no account, no network and no
setup, and works from a downloaded folder.

Students do not need to have met Lukes, Foucault or Fricker beforehand. Each
lens leads with its analytic question rather than with its name, so the task
is applying it, not recognising it. If you have already taught the three,
say so and let students check their reading against the feedback.

## Suggested use

**As preparation.** Set it before the seminar on power. Ask students to
arrive able to say which lens they found hardest to apply, and why.

**In a lecture, as a demonstration.** Project it and take the three lens
choices from the room. The interesting moment is any disagreement about item
3 or item 2 — those are the deliberate overlaps, and the activity agrees with
both readings.

**As a five-minute opener.** Run it cold, then move straight to the harder
question the activity deliberately does not answer: a college with one term
and a small budget can do one of the three remedies. Which, and what does it
leave running?

## Prediction question

Before students open it, ask:

> Three years of reports about fumes in a college workshop, and nothing was
> done. Who exercised power?

Most rooms answer "someone decided against the technicians". The file
contains no decision at all, which is the activity's first move.

## Activity sequence

1. The five case items are the options. They appear in the same order in
   every round and nothing about them ever changes — only the question asked
   of them does. Each round asks for the single strongest item, and the
   feedback then names the ones that are genuinely relevant too.
2. **Lens 1 — Lukes.** Which item does an account built on decisions, agendas
   and shaped preferences bring most sharply into focus? Feedback names what
   the lens sees and what it cannot.
3. **Lens 2 — Foucault.** The same five items, asked what is allowed to count
   as knowing.
4. **Lens 3 — Fricker.** The same five items, asked whose word travelled and
   what nobody had the words for. This lens names **two** harms — testimonial
   and hermeneutical — and the feedback says so; item 4 is ranked the stronger
   evidence only because no other lens has any claim on it.
5. **Synthesis.** The three lenses side by side — what each sees, what each
   backgrounds, what each would change — and a diagram showing the three
   remedies running in parallel and never reaching one another.

## Debrief questions

- The file contains no decision. Does that mean nobody exercised power?
- Item 3 — the technicians who stopped reporting — is core to Lukes and
  defensible under both of the others. What is each lens seeing in it?
- Item 5 is not about anybody's credibility. What kind of harm is it?
- Which single remedy would you fund first, and what would you be leaving
  running?
- Who would each remedy make answerable? Notice that one of the three has
  almost nothing to say about accountability.

## Likely misconceptions

**"Power means somebody decided something."** The whole case is built so that
this reading runs out. Nothing was resolved and nobody refused anything.

**"The three lenses are three ways of saying the same thing."** They are not,
and the remedies are how you can tell: each closes a mechanism the other two
leave open.

**"The item I picked was also relevant, so it should have counted."** Each
round asks for the *strongest* evidence, not for everything relevant, and the
feedback names the overlaps rather than dismissing them. Item 3 in particular
is real territory for all three lenses.

**"One of the three lenses must be the right one."** The activity refuses this
explicitly. Watch for students converging on Fricker because the harm is most
legible there; press them on item 2, which does not move however much anybody
is believed.

**"A blind spot is a flaw in the theory."** Each account backgrounds
something *because of* what it foregrounds. Foucault's difficulty with
accountability is the price of treating power as dispersed rather than held.

**Matching names to definitions.** If students are doing this rather than
analysing, the questions above about item 3 will expose it.

## Limitations and cautions

- Brackwell College is invented. No real institution, incident, inquiry or
  person is described.
- The key marking each item as core, defensible or out of scope is one
  defensible reading of an invented case, written to make the contrasts
  sharp. It is a teaching device, not a finding, and a well-argued
  alternative reading should be credited.
- Applying a lens is an interpretation, not evidence.
- These are three accounts among many, and each is contested within its own
  literature. Lukes's third dimension in particular has a long-standing
  objection — that attributing "real interests" to people who do not report
  them is itself a claim someone has to be entitled to make. Raise it if the
  group is ready for it.
- The activity does not adjudicate between the three, and should not be used
  to suggest that critical accounts of power are interchangeable.

## Accessibility

Keyboard operable throughout, with a visible focus indicator at every step.
Answered options are marked `aria-disabled` rather than `disabled`, so
students can tab back through them and re-read the feedback. Verdicts are
carried by border style and a glyph as well as by colour, plus a visually
hidden sentence, so nothing depends on distinguishing green from gold from
blue. The progress strip is an ordered list with `aria-current`. The synthesis
diagram has a description stating the relationship it shows, and the same
content is present as text in the three lens summaries above it. Programmatic
scrolling honours a reduced-motion preference. There is no timed content, so
students may take as long as they like on any lens.

## Extension

Give the group a fourth item and ask which lens claims it, and whether any
lens has to give ground:

> The Estates Committee minutes record that the item was "not progressed
> pending resource clarification". No resource request was ever submitted.

Or run the harder counterfactual: the College adopts all three remedies. What
could still go wrong, and which lens would you need that is not here?

## Evidence and reading

Lukes's three-dimensional view of power, Foucault on discipline, knowledge
and normalisation, and Fricker's distinction between testimonial and
hermeneutical injustice. Direct students to the primary sources rather than
to this activity: it is a way of practising the analysis, not a summary of
any of the three positions.
