# Teaching notes - Power Lens Laboratory (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/10-power-lens-lukes-foucault-fricker/`

**What it teaches.** For each of three named lenses, Lukes, Foucault
and Fricker, one item in the case file is the strongest evidence.
Picking it out from the others shows what that lens is built to
notice, and what it leaves for a different lens to pick up.

**Before students start.** Names are given upfront here, unlike the
Full edition, which withholds Lukes, Foucault and Fricker until
debrief.

**What students do.**
1. For each lens, pick the single case item that is its strongest
   piece of evidence.
2. Read which of the remaining items are also relevant and which
   belong to a different lens entirely.
3. Put all three lenses side by side and read what each could say and
   could not say.

**What to look for.** The same case item can be relevant to more than
one lens, but only one item is its sharpest fit. The credibility
lens's strongest item is a colleague not being believed, not the
missing category for logging a complaint, which counts as relevant
here but is not its best fit.

**Debrief.** Why can the missing-category item be relevant to both
Foucault and Fricker without making the two lenses interchangeable?

**Common misconception / caution.** Do not read the three lenses as
three ways of saying power is everywhere. The point of setting them
side by side is that each notices something different, not that all
three notice the same thing in different words.

**Use in class / timing.** Working through all three lenses takes
about 5 to 6 minutes.
