# Teaching notes - Crowd Behaviour Laboratory: Deindividuation and ESIM (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/09-crowd-deindividuation-vs-esim/`

**What it teaches.** Two accounts of crowd behaviour predict a
different shape of action, not just more or less of it. How
concentrated the action becomes on one target depends on whether a
shared category exists once the crowd turns anonymous, not on
anonymity by itself.

**Before students start.** None needed. The table of what each account
predicts is visible before running anything.

**What students do.**
1. Predict which account better fits a crowd whose action nearly all
   lands on one target, leaving other things nearby untouched.
2. Run each of four scenarios and toggle the crowd anonymous or
   identifiable, reading how concentrated the action becomes and how
   strong the disinhibition index runs.
3. Reveal the comparison of two runs that share an identical
   disinhibition index but very different concentration, and read what
   tells them apart.

**What to look for.** Two runs that score identically on the
deindividuation account's own index can still look completely
different in where the action lands. The index alone cannot separate
those two afternoons, even though the target pattern can.

**Debrief.** What does a shared disinhibition index actually rule in
or out, given that two very different-looking afternoons can produce
the same one?

**Common misconception / caution.** Neither account is refuted here.
Each fits two of the four scenarios better than the other does, and
calling deindividuation discredited ignores that it still predicts the
right direction in half of them.

**Use in class / timing.** Running all four scenarios and the
comparison takes about 6 to 8 minutes.
