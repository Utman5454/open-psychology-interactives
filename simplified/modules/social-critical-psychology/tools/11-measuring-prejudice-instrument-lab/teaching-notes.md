# Teaching notes - Measuring Prejudice: What Does the Instrument Capture? (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/11-measuring-prejudice-instrument-lab/`

**What it teaches.** Four instruments all described as measuring
prejudice differ mainly in what they directly observe, not in
accuracy. Putting them side by side shows a level missing between
them that a fifth instrument would be needed to reach.

**Before students start.** Nothing is withheld here: all four
instruments carry their names from the first screen.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what mainly differs between four instruments all described
   as measuring prejudice.
2. Open each instrument in turn and judge what it directly observes,
   then read what is usually inferred from it and what it cannot
   show.
3. Put all four side by side and read the synthesis on the level none
   of them reaches.

**What to look for.** Each instrument's "cannot show" column rules
out a different claim, and no instrument here is simply a more
accurate version of another.

**Debrief.** What would a fifth instrument, sitting between the
individual and the institutional level, need to measure that these
four do not?

**Common misconception / caution.** One instrument's finding does not
confirm or correct another's; they are not stages toward one hidden
number. Reading any of the four as a diagnosis of one person is a
category error.

**Use in class / timing.** Working through all four instruments takes
about 6 to 7 minutes.
