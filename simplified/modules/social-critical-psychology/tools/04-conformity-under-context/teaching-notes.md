# Teaching notes - Conformity Under Context (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/04-conformity-under-context/`

**What it teaches.** Moving from a spoken to a private response cuts
conformity by about 21 points on an easy, unambiguous display but only
about 11 points on a genuinely ambiguous one, because the move only
touches the normative route, and the ambiguous condition's agreement
is mostly informational to begin with.

**Before students start.** Every condition is on the page from the
outset; there is no forced sequence for running them.

**What students do.**
1. Predict the conformity rate on an easy display with a unanimous
   group.
2. Run each of six conditions one at a time and read how much of the
   resulting rate is normative and how much is informational.
3. Press the diagnostic comparison to see the size of the privacy
   effect on the easy display against the ambiguous one.

**What to look for.** Privacy drops agreement by about 21 points on
the easy display but only about 11 on the ambiguous one, because only
the normative route depends on being watched.

**Debrief.** Why does privacy affect the two displays so differently,
given that the group's answer is unanimous in both?

**Common misconception / caution.** Going along with a group that
knows more than you is often the sensible thing to do. The ambiguous
condition here is a case where the agreement may well be correct, not
evidence of pressure overriding judgement.

**Use in class / timing.** Running all six conditions takes about 5 to
6 minutes.
