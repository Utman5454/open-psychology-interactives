# Teaching notes - Minimal Group Allocation Laboratory (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/08-minimal-group-positive-distinctiveness/`

**What it teaches.** Choosing the largest lead over the option that
gives your own category the most points costs your own side points on
every allocation. The same four choices, held fixed, produce a
different outcome depending only on whether your category started
level, ahead, or behind.

**Before students start.** Say clearly that nothing is stored or
scored and the points buy nothing. The category assignment is
narrated rather than chosen by the student.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what most people do when allocating points between two
   anonymous strangers from an invented pair of categories.
2. Make four allocations, each offering an option that gives your
   category less than the most-for-your-side option but a bigger
   lead over the other category.
3. See the same four choices applied to three different starting
   positions and read what each did to the gap.

**What to look for.** Choosing the largest lead costs your own
category between five and seven points on every single allocation,
twenty-three points in total, in exchange for raising the lead between
the two categories from eleven to forty-five.

**Debrief.** Would the same four choices have looked different if made
separately under each starting position, rather than applied to all
three afterward?

**Common misconception / caution.** This does not explain real
prejudice or racism. Real groups carry histories, unequal starting
positions and things that have actually been done to them, none of
which is present when the categories are invented and unlabelled.
What it does show is narrower: the same four choices produced
differential allocation here with no prior hostility, no competition
and no group history at all, so none of those three is necessary to
produce the effect in this stripped-down setting. That is not the same
as establishing that categorising people is a sufficient cause of
real-world intergroup behaviour.

**Use in class / timing.** All four allocations and the comparison
take about 6 to 8 minutes.
