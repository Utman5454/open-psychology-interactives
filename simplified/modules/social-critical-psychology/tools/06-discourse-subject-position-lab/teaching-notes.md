# Teaching notes - Discourse and Subject Position Laboratory (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/06-discourse-subject-position-lab/`

**What it teaches.** One of the eight entries in an undisputed ledger,
the decision to halve a programme's hours, is stated outright by only
one of five accounts of the same six months. A second account comes
close, naming a "reduced-hours model" without keeping it as the same
entry; the other three have no field for it at all. Each account also
entitles a different kind of person to say what is really happening.

**Before students start.** The five accounts are all visible together
from the outset, so any two can be opened side by side to compare.

**What students do.**
1. Predict how many of the five accounts will state the decision to
   halve the hours outright, and how the rest might handle it instead
   of simply leaving it out.
2. Open one account at a time and judge who it entitles to say what is
   really happening, then read what kind of person it produces, what
   step follows, and where responsibility lands.
3. Once three accounts are settled, put all five side by side and read
   which one states the halved hours outright, and which one comes
   close without quite matching it.

**What to look for.** Only the advocacy bulletin states the halved
hours outright. The commissioning report comes closest of the rest: it
has the hours as "a model implemented on schedule," which is not the
same entry. The other three are compatible with the whole ledger; they
simply have no field for it.

**Debrief.** What changes when the halved hours becomes "a model
implemented on schedule" rather than disappearing from the account
entirely?

**Common misconception / caution.** These are authored readings of an
invented case, not settled facts. A well-argued alternative to any of
these judgements deserves credit, not automatic correction.

**Use in class / timing.** Settling three accounts and comparing all
five takes about 6 to 7 minutes.
