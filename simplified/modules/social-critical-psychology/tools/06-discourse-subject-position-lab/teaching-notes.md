# Five Ways to Write It Up — Simplified Edition

**Module:** Social & Critical Psychology
**Duration:** 6–8 minutes
**Level:** Final year or postgraduate
**Edition:** Simplified. This keeps the fixed ledger, the five accounts and the
entry four of them cannot hold, from the **Discourse and Subject Position
Laboratory**. The original asks four questions of every account; this asks one.
The rest is at
`modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/06-discourse-subject-position-lab/`.

## Before you teach it

Nobody in this activity is lying, and that is the point. Every account is
compatible with the whole ledger. It is not a word-frequency exercise, and it
is not a claim that the events are made of language: the ledger is fixed and
stays on screen for exactly that reason. It is also not a ranking. A record
that could not note declining attendance would be a bad record, and a
safeguarding process that could not notice an unassessed setting would be a
dangerous one.

The recovery summary needs care. That vocabulary was fought for by service
users against precisely the case-note position, and it is in the activity
because a vocabulary can do what it was designed to do and still leave a
service cut looking like a personal achievement.

## Learning objectives

By the end, a student should be able to:

1. identify the subject position an account makes available;
2. say which actions it renders reasonable and which it removes;
3. say who it entitles to describe what is happening;
4. recognise what an account cannot say about anything other than the person;
5. distinguish discourse analysis from idealism about events.

## Preparation

None. Students do not need any discourse-analytic vocabulary in advance.

## Suggested use

**In teaching on institutional records, assessment or case management.**

**Alongside teaching on recovery models**, where the fourth account is the one
worth an argument.

**Projected, opening two accounts from the front and letting students do the
rest.** The case note and the recovery summary are the pair to contrast.

## Prediction question

> One of the eight entries appears in only one of the five accounts. What sort
> of entry would you expect that to be?

Something the service decided. Note that "something disputed" is marked
partial: it is a reasonable rule of thumb and nothing in this ledger is
disputed.

## Activity sequence

1. **Read the ledger aloud.** All eight entries. Nobody disputes any of them.
2. **Take the vote.**
3. **Open the case note.** Answer the authority question, then read the
   analysis. Point at "limited insight".
4. **Open the recovery summary.** Ask the room whether it is kinder. It is.
   Then ask what happened to the letter she wrote in July.
5. **Open the advocacy bulletin.** It is the only one that begins with April.
6. **Open the comparison.** Read the final column down.
7. **Ask what the commissioning report does with the hours.** It has them, as
   a model implemented on schedule. That is not the same entry.

## Debrief questions

- Which account would you rather have written about you? Which would you rather
  a tribunal read?
- The letter in July appears in four accounts. What is it in each?
- What can the case note not record, and is that a fault in the practitioner?
- The recovery summary has no ungrammatical way of saying a request went
  unanswered. What follows from that?
- Four accounts have no place for April. What does that do to who is
  responsible?
- Is the advocacy bulletin the true one?

## Likely misconceptions

- **One of them is lying.** None of them is. Every account is compatible with
  the whole ledger.
- **This shows records are useless.** It shows what a genre can and cannot
  hold. The genres exist for reasons the page states.
- **Recovery language is a con.** It was won by service users against the
  case-note position, and it can still do this. Both are true.
- **The advocacy bulletin is the neutral account.** It asserts that the
  assessment was in error rather than showing it.
- **Discourse analysis says events are made of language.** The ledger is fixed
  and on screen throughout, which is the answer to that.

## Limitations and cautions

Five accounts is a simplification of a file that would hold dozens,
contradicting one another and shifting within a paragraph. The analyses are
authored readings and a well-argued alternative should be credited. R is
invented, and nothing here describes any real person, record or organisation.

## Accessibility considerations

The account is a group of native radio buttons in a labelled fieldset. The
authority options carry three non-colour state signals plus a visually hidden
sentence naming the state, and once answered they are locked with
`aria-disabled` rather than the `disabled` attribute so they stay reachable,
each naming the account it is the right answer for. The ledger is a numbered
list on screen throughout, and the comparison is a real table with headers.

## Optional extension

Ask students to write a sixth account of the same eight entries, in a genre of
their choosing, that makes a different next step obvious. Then ask them what
their account cannot say. It is a five-minute exercise and it is the whole
module in miniature.
