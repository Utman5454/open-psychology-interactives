# Teaching notes - Discourse and Subject Position Laboratory (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/06-discourse-subject-position-lab/`

**What it teaches.** One of the eight entries in an undisputed ledger,
the decision to halve a programme's hours, appears in only one of
five accounts of the same six months. Each account also entitles a
different kind of person to say what is really happening.

**Before students start.** The five accounts are all visible together
from the outset, so any two can be opened side by side to compare.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what kind of ledger entry would appear in only one of five
   accounts.
2. Open one account at a time and judge who it entitles to say what is
   really happening, then read what kind of person it produces, what
   step follows, and where responsibility lands.
3. Once three accounts are settled, put all five side by side and read
   which one mentions the halved hours.

**What to look for.** Only the advocacy bulletin mentions the halved
hours. The other four are compatible with the whole ledger; they
simply have no field for that entry.

**Debrief.** Why are an inaccurate account and an account with no
field for something not the same problem?

**Common misconception / caution.** These are authored readings of an
invented case, not settled facts. A well-argued alternative to any of
these judgements deserves credit, not automatic correction.

**Use in class / timing.** Settling three accounts and comparing all
five takes about 6 to 7 minutes.
