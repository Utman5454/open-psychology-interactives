# Teaching notes - Epistemology Lens Switch (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/01-epistemology-lens-switch/`

**What it teaches.** The same case file supports different research
designs depending on what the researcher takes "disruption" to be from
the start. Declaring a starting point commits you to a question and a
method whether you notice it or not, and a choice that sits in tension
with your starting point is not forbidden, only worth naming.

**Before students start.** Students should have met the words
positivist, critical realist and social constructionist at least once.
The activity never uses these technical names itself: it gives each
position as a description rather than a label, so mapping "a real
pattern of behaviour," "a real tendency behind what is recorded" and
"a category that gets assembled" onto those three terms is left to the
lecturer.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what most decides which method three researchers use from
   the same file.
2. Declare a starting point, then choose a question and a method,
   reading whether each choice follows from or sits in tension with
   what was declared.
3. Reveal all three completed designs side by side and read what each
   could say, could not say and would change.

**What to look for.** A fully coherent design only clears the floor;
it is not a mark of quality. A choice in tension with the declared
starting point is not forbidden, and a good mixed design can choose
one deliberately.

**Debrief.** What would each of the three designs need to add to say
something the other two cannot?

**Common misconception / caution.** Nobody in this activity is
wrong for holding a starting point; the caution is treating a
coherent design as automatically a good one. "A category that gets
assembled" is not the same claim as "nothing happened at Meadowbank."

**Use in class / timing.** The full sequence takes about 10 minutes.
