# Teaching notes - Constructing a Category (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/02-constructing-a-category/`

**What it teaches.** The same fixed population of 400 supports very
different prevalence figures depending only on where a threshold is
set. A plausible three-item questionnaire built from the most obvious
features also misses real people whose accounts use different language
for the same difficulty.

**Before students start.** The table of five accounts and their coded
features is visible from the start, already captioned as a decision
rather than a fact.

**What students do.**
1. Predict what a later prevalence figure will most depend on.
2. Move the threshold slider and watch prevalence change while the
   same 400 simulated people sit underneath every bar.
3. Choose three of six features as a questionnaire and read how many
   of the five named people it would find.

**What to look for.** The three most obvious features, sleep,
concentration and dread, find only three of the five people. The best
any three-item combination can do is four of five, and the account it
always misses is the one written in workload language rather than
internal-state language.

**Debrief.** What do the accounts an obvious instrument misses have in
common?

**Common misconception / caution.** This is not an argument that the
underlying difficulty is fake. The model assumes one single underlying
dimension, which is exactly what makes the threshold look so
arbitrary; whether a real category has that kind of clean structure is
a separate, empirical question this activity cannot settle.

**Use in class / timing.** Moving the slider and building one
questionnaire takes about 5 to 6 minutes.
