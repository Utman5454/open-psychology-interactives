# Where the File Stops — Simplified Edition

**Module:** Social & Critical Psychology
**Duration:** 6–8 minutes
**Level:** Second or final year
**Edition:** Simplified. This keeps the two rounds of evidence and the kept
commitments from **Person or Setting: The Workplace Laboratory**, and adds a
remedies table. A third round, the tracking across three and the
report-writing exercise are in the longer version at
`modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/12-person-or-setting-workplace-lab/`.

## Before you teach it

Two conclusions this is not aiming at, and both matter. It is not an argument
that individual support is a con: two people have a documented training gap
that no rota change will close, and somebody exhausted this week needs
something this week. And it is not an argument that structural explanation wins
by default: the comparison team was matched at recruitment and is not matched
now, which is a real weakness in the strongest-looking evidence on the page.

The most important thing to protect is that round one is not a trick. The
individual reading is the best-supported reading of a file in which only
individuals were measured, and the activity says so.

## Learning objectives

By the end, a student should be able to:

1. explain why a difference between people does not locate the cause;
2. recognise that the level measured decides the level concluded;
3. distinguish describing a pattern from naming a cause;
4. say what each level of explanation would change and who would act;
5. say what each remedy would leave running.

## Preparation

None.

## Suggested use

**In teaching on occupational or applied psychology**, where the individual
remedy is the one most likely to be commissioned.

**In a critical psychology session on individualising explanations**, as the
version that does not caricature the individual account.

**Projected, with the room voting after each round.** The interesting moment is
how many change their answer and how many say the first answer was wrong.

## Prediction question

There is no separate prediction: the first commitment is the prediction, and it
is made on evidence that genuinely supports it.

## Activity sequence

1. **Read round one aloud.** All four items.
2. **Take a vote and record it.** Most rooms pick the people.
3. **Read the feedback.** It says this is the best-supported reading, and then
   asks what has just happened: a pattern was described and called a cause.
4. **Read round two.** Stress that nothing has been withdrawn.
5. **Vote again.** Both answers stay on screen.
6. **Ask who changed their mind and why.** The evidence changed, not the
   reasoning.
7. **Open the remedies table and read the final column across all three rows.**

## Debrief questions

- Was the first answer wrong?
- What did round one contain no way of noticing?
- The comparison with Team A is the strongest item. What is wrong with it?
- Which remedy would a manager most easily commission, and what would it leave
  running?
- The structural remedy needs somebody who is not in the building. What follows
  for what actually gets done?
- What would you write in the report?

## Likely misconceptions

- **The first round was a trick.** It was an accurate file about one level.
- **Individual explanations are always individualising in the pejorative
  sense.** Two people here have a real training gap.
- **Structural explanations win.** The comparison that makes them look strongest
  is not a clean one.
- **Changing your mind means the first answer was a mistake.** The evidence
  changed. That is what evidence is for.
- **One remedy is enough.** Every row's final column has something in it.

## Limitations and cautions

Every figure was written for teaching. The comparison team is matched at
recruitment and not now, which the page says three times. Two rounds is short,
and the longer version adds a third that complicates the structural reading
further.

## Accessibility considerations

Both rounds are ordinary lists. Each commitment is a choice grid with three
non-colour state signals plus a visually hidden sentence, locked with
`aria-disabled` rather than the `disabled` attribute so options stay reachable.
Both commitments are shown as readout tiles from the moment each is made. The
remedies are a real table whose every cell is a sentence.

## Optional extension

Ask students to write the two-sentence recommendation they would put in the
report, and then to underline the part of it that somebody in the building can
actually do. The gap between the analysis and the actionable half is the
practical form of the whole activity.

## Evidence and citation notes

On individualising explanations in occupational settings see Ryan and Deci
(2000) for the individual account at its strongest, and, for the critique, Fisher
(2009), **Capitalist Realism**, Zero Books, and Nixon (2020), *The
psychologisation of work-related stress*, in Wainwright and Calnan's tradition.
On job demands and resources as a structural account see Bakker and Demerouti
(2007), *The job demands-resources model*, **Journal of Managerial Psychology**,
22(3), 309–328. On why a correlation between individual differences and an
outcome does not locate a cause, the general treatment is in the Simplified
Edition activity **Four People, One Score**.
