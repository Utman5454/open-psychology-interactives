# Teaching notes - Person or Setting? Workplace Explanation Laboratory (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/12-person-or-setting-workplace-lab/`

**What it teaches.** The same four explanations, individual,
relational, structural, several at once, get committed twice as more
evidence arrives, and nothing from the first round is withdrawn when
the second adds more. The remedies table then shows the same question,
what would this explanation leave running, getting a different answer
for each of the three.

**Before students start.** None needed. The first commitment is itself
the prediction; there is no separate question beforehand.

**What students do.**
1. On four individual-level items, commit to where the problem mainly
   lies.
2. Read six more items spanning relational and structural levels and
   commit again, keeping the first answer either way.
3. Read what each explanation would change, who would have to act,
   and what it would leave running.

**What to look for.** The strongest-looking item in the second round,
a comparison team with a much lower score, is also the weakest: two
teams that were alike years ago is evidence, not a clean experiment.

**Debrief.** Why are describing a pattern and calling it a cause not
the same thing, even when the pattern itself is real?

**Common misconception / caution.** Nothing in the first round becomes
false just because the second round adds more. An individual-level
reading still has to explain why the difference between two teams is
also about individuals, rather than treating the earlier evidence as
overturned.

**Use in class / timing.** Both rounds with the remedies table take
about 6 to 8 minutes.
