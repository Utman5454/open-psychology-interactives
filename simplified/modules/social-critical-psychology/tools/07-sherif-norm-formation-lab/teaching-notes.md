# Teaching notes - Norm-Formation Laboratory (Simplified)

`simplified/modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/07-sherif-norm-formation-lab/`

**What it teaches.** A genuinely moving point, with its distance never
disclosed, still lets a private later judgement drift toward a
group's earlier value, even once the group is gone and nobody is
scoring the answer.

**Before students start.** This uses a real moving point rather than
the classic illusion, since that illusion needs darkness a lit screen
cannot reliably produce. The ambiguity here is about measurement, not
about whether anything moved.

**What students do.**
1. Predict whether judgements will stay near the group's value, revert
   to the person's own first answers, settle midway, or scatter.
2. Give three judgements alone, three with three others' values shown,
   then two alone again.
3. Compare an opening mean, the group's settled value, and a final
   private mean.

**What to look for.** A final private mean landing nearer the earlier
group value than the person's own opening judgements is the pattern
the original studies found. A short run that does not show it is not a
failure either: eight trials cannot separate someone who held their
own frame from someone reading the display consistently throughout.

**Debrief.** Why does the summary compare against the group's own
settled value rather than asking whether any one person moved toward
it?

**Common misconception / caution.** Not converging is not a sign of
independence of mind, any more than converging is a sign of giving in.
Both are ordinary outcomes on a genuinely unconstrained judgement.

**Use in class / timing.** All eight trials take about 5 to 7 minutes.
