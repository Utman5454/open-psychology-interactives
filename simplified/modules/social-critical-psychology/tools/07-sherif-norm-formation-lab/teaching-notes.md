# The Frame You Take With You — Simplified Edition

**Module:** Social & Critical Psychology
**Duration:** 5–7 minutes
**Level:** First or second year
**Edition:** Simplified. This keeps the performed task from the **Sherif Norm
Formation Laboratory** whole, because there is no way to preserve it except by
performing it. The phases are shorter and the three comparison groups are
reduced to their endpoints. The rest is at
`modules/social-critical-psychology/tools/07-sherif-norm-formation-lab/`.

## Before you teach it

Nothing is timed and nothing measures the learner. A person who converges and a
person who does not are both showing something the activity is designed to make
visible, and the ending says so explicitly in the case where they did not. What
anybody types is discarded when they leave the page.

**The display is a browser analogue, not a replication, and it is worth saying
so to the room.** Sherif used the autokinetic effect: a stationary pinpoint of
light in a fully darkened room appears to drift, because the eye cannot hold
still and has nothing to anchor against. Every participant was judging an
illusion. That illusion needs darkness, dark-adapted eyes and a controlled
viewing distance, none of which a lecture theatre full of laptops can supply,
and a display that only might move for some students would leave them
estimating something they never experienced. So the point of light here
genuinely moves, by a small amount that changes on every trial and is never
disclosed. The ambiguity is real but differently produced: it is ambiguity of
measurement rather than of existence. The page says this in the opening note
and again in the debrief.

Each trial runs for five seconds, starts when the student presses a button and
ends on its own with the field emptying. The response is a slider from 1 to 10,
labelled "1 = barely at all, 10 = a great deal". There is no unit, no ruler and
no reference object of any kind in the field, so there is no correct answer and
no way to work one out, which is the whole point.

A student who cannot see the field cannot perform the perceptual part, and the
page does not pretend otherwise. The judgement is genuinely unconstrained for
everyone, so a student who gives a number anyway is doing exactly what the
social mechanism requires and their run works normally.

## Learning objectives

By the end, a student should be able to:

1. say why a frame formed with others persists once they are gone;
2. distinguish that from compliance, which ends with the audience;
3. explain why ambiguity is what makes the figure stick;
4. recognise that different groups settle at different values;
5. describe a norm as what has stopped being a question.

## Preparation

None. Students need no numbers and no prior reading.

## Suggested use

**As the first thing in a session on social influence**, before conformity is
introduced, so that internalisation is met before compliance rather than after.

**In a lecture, with the whole room judging their own display.** Everybody gets
different others, because the others are built from their own opening
judgements, and everybody gets a different drift on every trial.

**Followed immediately by the paired conformity activity**, where the same
question is asked about a task with a right answer.

## Prediction question

> In the original studies, what happened on the final solo rounds?

They stayed near the group's value. Note that "settled midway" is marked
partial: it describes the shape and not the mechanism, because what returns is
not an average of two answers.

## Activity sequence

1. **Take the vote.**
2. **Run one trial with the room watching, and say what the field does not
   contain.** No ruler, no grid, no reference object, and the field empties
   when the trial ends, so there is nothing to measure against afterwards
   either. Ask for a show of hands on whether it moved a little or a lot: the
   spread in the room is the demonstration.
3. **Three trials alone.** Do not comment on anybody's numbers.
4. **Three with the others.** Point out that the other three speak after the
   trial and before the student answers, which is Sherif's order.
5. **Two alone again.** Point out that everything the others said has gone from
   the screen, including the columns in the table.
6. **Read the finding.** Ask how many ended nearer the group and how many
   nearer their own opening. Both are worth discussing.
7. **Read the table of three other groups.** Same rule, different openings,
   different resting places.

## Debrief questions

- On the last two trials there was nobody to be awkward in front of, nothing of
  theirs on the screen and no cost to answering as you did at the start. Why
  did the figure stay, if it did?
- How is this different from agreeing with someone you think is wrong?
- Three other groups settled somewhere else entirely. What does that tell you
  about the figure your group reached?
- What would happen if the field had a ruler down one side?
- The point of light really moved, and you were never told how far. Would
  knowing have changed what the group did to your answers?
- Somebody in the room did not converge. What are the possible reasons, and can
  eight trials tell them apart?
- If a norm is what has stopped being a question, how would you notice one you
  are inside?

## Likely misconceptions

- **They were just going along with the others.** There was nobody left on the
  final trials and nothing of theirs on the screen. Compliance ends with the
  audience and this does not.
- **The group found the right answer.** There is no right answer, and three
  other groups found different ones.
- **Not converging shows independence of mind.** It might, or the person may
  have been reading the display consistently all along. Eight trials cannot
  tell, and the activity says so rather than scoring it.
- **This is the same as the conformity studies.** Those use a task with an
  obvious right answer, which is why the mechanism is different.
- **The others were real.** They are generated by a rule printed at the bottom
  of the page, from the student's own opening judgements.
- **This is the autokinetic effect.** It is not. Sherif's light did not move;
  this one does. See the note above, and expect at least one student to have
  read about the original and to ask.

## Limitations and cautions

Eight trials is short and three simulated others is small; their rule is a
single convergence weight with noise rather than anything resembling a person.
The display is an analogue rather than the autokinetic effect, so the ambiguity
is of measurement rather than of existence. The others' centre is deliberately
set 2.6 scale points away from the student's own opening average, because
seeding them on that average puts the group's figure where the student already
is and leaves nothing to converge towards; the rule including that gap is
printed on the page so it can be argued with. And the historical studies this
borrows its logic from involved deception and conditions that would not be
approved today, which is a reason to treat their findings as a starting point
rather than as settled facts about people.

## Accessibility considerations

Nothing is timed except the five-second trial itself, which the student starts
and which ends on its own. Every state of the display is reported in text
beneath it: ready, running, and finished with the field empty. The response is
a native range input with min 1, max 10 and step 1, its anchors stated in the
label and hint and its value echoed as text; values outside the scale cannot be
entered and are clamped if they arrive by any other route. Controls that are
not yet available are marked aria-disabled rather than disabled, so they stay
reachable from the keyboard, and the start button's own text says why it is
waiting. A reduced-motion preference halves the drift and slows it rather than
removing it, because a still point would leave the student judging movement
they never saw. The chart labels each series at the end of its own line, its
description lists every judgement made, and the group trials are shaded and
also named in words.

## Optional extension

Ask students what would have to be true of a workplace, a discipline or a
family for a figure arrived at in it to be invisible as a choice. The answer,
that nobody now present remembers it being decided, is the useful sense of
norm to leave with.

## Evidence and citation notes

The logic is Sherif's: Sherif (1936), **The Psychology of Social Norms**,
Harper. On the persistence of the group frame in later solo testing, and its
transmission across generations of participants, see Jacobs and Campbell
(1961), *The perpetuation of an arbitrary tradition through several
generations of a laboratory microculture*, **Journal of Abnormal and Social
Psychology**, 62(3), 649–658. On the distinction from compliance see Kelman
(1958), *Compliance, identification, and internalization*, **Journal of
Conflict Resolution**, 2(1), 51–60.
